Welcome our new Latino Media Initiative Director
Jesenia De Moya Correa, CCM's new Latino Media Initiative Director

We are thrilled that Newmark J-School alum and former City College adjunct professor Jesenia De Moya Correa has joined CCM as its Latino Media Initiative Director! 

During her time as the Latino Communities reporter at The Philadelphia Inquirer, De Moya Correa created a Spanish-language site called El Inquirer, to deliver online news, features, and service journalism for Hispanic and Latinx audiences in the greater Philadelphia area. She also contributed to the development of the outlet’s Communities and Engagement desk and established content exchange agreements with traditional media outlets in the Caribbean.

In addition to her leadership and community engagement work,  De Moya Correa understands what it means to be a community reporter in the U.S. and abroad. Her work has appeared in outlets like Yale Climate Connections, El Diario NY, Voice of America, Salta Pa’trás Films, Diario Libre, Radio Santa María, and Listín Diario. 

In recognition of her work,  De Moya Correa was a finalist for the 2017 Eppy Awards, Newmark J-School’s 2018 Sidney Hillman Social Justice Reporting Prize, and the Society of Professional Journalists’ 2018 Mark of Excellence Awards. She has won fellowships from The Lenfest Institute, received a special award offered by the Pennsylvania NewsMedia Association, and was nominated by the general public for Al Día News Media’s Women of Merit Awards. You can read more about her experience and appointment here. 

What does it mean for you to come back to the J-School as an alum and lead this initiative? 

JC: The J-School has always been a second-second home to me, 'cause it's the place where I've found great motivation and many inspirational people in journalism -- which has an immense value to me. In the Latine world, we have a saying that goes: el buen hijo siempre vuelve a casa -- the good child always returns home. To come back to the school's campus, to lead such an important and nuanced initiative means that I now have the responsibility to motivate and inspire others, the newer and not so new journalists. So, I feel privileged and honored.

What excites you most about community media and its future? 

JC: The health and environmental crises we have experienced in recent years have highlighted the impact that community media has within their ecosystems in moments of need and despair. The wide spreading of this acknowledgment is tilting community media makers and journalists to the center of the conversations and programs that advocate for journalism innovation and sustainability. I'm excited for the trials and errors, for the depth and complexity of the journalism to be produced, for the opportunities, for the impact, for the changes we will see in the "Big J" journalism landscape and the role we will play in that movement as part of CCM looking forward.

Perhaps you can expand a bit on how the LMI is going to fit in with the existing Black and Asian Media Initiatives?

JC: Acknowledging the nuances and differences of each, the Latino Media Initiative is to continue delivering the training, research and events that best support the needs that the community media makers present nowadays, in the same way or in a similar capacity to how the Black and Asian Media Initiatives do. It will continue to produce the Latino Media Summit, to collect data and information on the Latino media landscape and to coordinate programs that continue to empower Latine journalists and media makers around the country. But, it will also recognize the similarities that exist across these initiatives and work collaboratively to address the needs in the industry.

What do you see as LMI's role in the larger Latino journalist and media landscape? 

JC: As all other initiatives at CCM, I see the Latino Media Initiative as a pillar effort that builds the capacity to connect the dots and fill in the voids. Latine journalism needs a better sense of belonging to be able to engage in new opportunities, cultivate its great talent and learn from the work being done by all the players in the field. With our vision, listening skills and empathy, I believe CCM's Latino Media Initiative will lead the conversations needed to help build a more solid foundation within the U.S. community journalism ecosystem, especially for the media creators and journalists who serve Hispanic and Latinx audiences in the American continent.

At this moment, when the Latino communities of the U.S. are considered of vital importance in the post-pandemic social and economic resurgence both here and abroad, is when we need to focus on the overall sustainability of the Latino news media, which widely influences the day-to-day lives of our people.

The University of North Carolina’s Center on Technology Policy has issued a report: Rescuing Local News Through Tax Credits: A review of policy in the U.S. and Canada. From the report: “This report provides an overview of journalism tax measures that have been proposed in the U.S., a deep dive on Canada’s tax credits for journalism, and an analysis of their impact and shortcomings. We also provide a set of six recommendations, based on Canada’s experience, to inform U.S. policymakers pursuing tax strategies to support local news.” Nieman Lab also offered an analysis of the report.

Fellowships & Opportunities

The Future Rising Fellowship supports young leaders working at the intersection of climate and gender justice. The year-long program builds leadership and storytelling skills for individuals aged 17-25 working to address critical issues at the intersection of climate and gender justice. Fellows receive a $5,000 stipend. Deadline is June 29.

The Pulitzer Center, in collaboration with Diversify Photo, is taking applications for its Eyewitness Photojournalism Grant. This grant aims to tell visual stories about and by photojournalists from historically underrepresented communities. Deadline is June 30.

The International Women’s Media Foundation is accepting its next round of applications for the Howard G. Buffett Fund for Women Journalists, which supports projects including educational opportunities, investigative reporting and media development initiatives. The IWMF is inclusive of all women, nonbinary and gender nonconforming journalists. The next round’s deadline is July 1. Applications are accepted on a monthly, rolling deadline and any submitted on or after July 1 will receive a decision in September.

The Bertha Foundation is taking applications for the Bertha Challenge, an opportunity for activists and investigative journalists to spend a year working on one pressing social justice challenge and to deliver a body of work at the end of the fellowship year. Successful applicants will receive non-residential paid fellowships and project budgets. Deadline is July 4. 

This year’s Photography Expanded Fellowship will support projects on the topic of heat and climate crisis that reimagine what a critical and ecological photographic practice might look like. Magnum Foundation is seeking proposals from photographers from all regions of the world who are producing work that responds to the theme and are excited to contribute to a creative community of fellows. Up to 12 selected participants will receive a grant of $10,000, take part in two workshops, and connect with collaborative partners and networks. Deadline is July 6.

Teen Vogue is taking applications from Student Correspondents to report on upcoming elections and voting rights in the following states: Nevada, Florida, Michigan, Wisconsin, Arizona, Georgia, and Pennsylvania. Apply by July 7. 

The Society of Professional Journalists Freelance Fellowship will support at least three SPJ members who are independent journalists attending the SPJ23 conference taking place September 28-30 in Las Vegas. Deadline is July 14.

The Solutions Journalism Network invites you to apply for its Journalists of Color Fellowship. This 9-month fellowship is designed to identify and support a cohort of diverse, next-generation leaders through a program of solutions journalism, leadership training, mentorship and community of practice. Ten mid-career U.S. based journalists who are interested in editorial management will be chosen. Deadline is July 17.

USC Annenberg’s Center for Health Journalism is accepting applications for its Data Fellowship. The program offers journalists an opportunity to transform their reporting by training them to “interview the data” as if it were a human source. Equipped with the tools to find original sources of information and perform data analysis, fellows graduate from this hands-on training program prepared to produce a major investigative or explanatory health reporting project in the months that follow. Deadline is July 17.

The Columbia University Aging Center is accepting applications for the Age Boom Academy 2023, to be held in October. The 2023 theme is Raising the Roof: America’s Housing Crisis and Our Longer Lives. Deadline is July 21.

The Institute for Journalism & Natural Resources is taking applications for its Klamath River Institute. The program is an expenses-paid, six-day Institute for up to 15 journalists that will explore the Klamath River Basin from its headwaters in southern Oregon to its mouth in northern California, and investigate how the removal of four dams will impact Indigenous rights and sovereignty, hydrology, food systems, agriculture practices, river ecosystems, fisheries, wildlife migrations, the energy system, and more. Deadline is July 28. 

The National Center on Disability and Journalism (NCDJ) is now accepting entries for the Katherine Schneider Journalism Award for Excellence in Reporting on Disability and the Gary Corcoran Student Prize for Excellence in Reporting on Disability. Entries are judged by professional journalists and experts on disability issues. Deadline is August 1. 

The Feet in 2 Worlds 2023 Editing Fellowship is open for applications. The fellow will be mentored and trained in editing for radio, podcast, and online media by a team of veteran editors. Journalists from immigrant backgrounds and from communities of color are strongly urged to apply. Deadline is August 4.

The Artist/Scholar in Residence program at the Library of Congress is now accepting applications. The program, part of LOC’s Connecting Communities Digital Initiative (CCDI) is part of the Library’s Of the People: Widening the Path program with support from the Mellon Foundation, supports artists or scholars in the creation of new scholarly and/or artists' works that imaginatively study, experiment with, and/or critique the Library’s digital collections, materials, and/or services. The program is open to a broad array of artists and scholars, including (but not limited to): visual artists, conceptual artists, dancers, journalists, cultural critics, independent scholars, academic scholars, and cultural heritage practitioners, including librarians. For 2023, the Library intends to award two 12-month grants of up to $90,000 each. Deadline is August 7. 

The Sir Harry Evans Global Fellowship provides a nine-month fellowship opportunity with Reuters (or other media outlets) and Durham University that will include undertaking an investigative project from inside a newsroom in London, New York, or Toronto. The Fellow will be mentored by top editors at Reuters while being overseen and having access to Durham University academics and research resources. Candidates should have at least two years of professional journalism experience. Deadline to apply is August 10.

Poynter is accepting applications for its Power of Diverse Voices: Writing Workshop for Journalists of Color workshop. With the support of expert instructors and fellow journalists of color, develop the confidence and skills to share your unique perspective in persuasive writing. Deadline is September 15.
