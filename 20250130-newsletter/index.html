A look ahead at 2025

We hope the beginning of 2025 has been a peaceful one for all community media publishers, journalists, and supporters!

CCM heads into this year with an exciting opportunity. Following Mikhael Simmonds' departure earlier this month, we announce a search for our next Executive Director. This presents an opportunity to lead our organization into its next chapter.

We are looking for a high-profile leader in community media who understands the unique challenges and opportunities facing community news organizations serving underrepresented groups all over the country. They should bring both vision and practical experience in strengthening community media sustainability, with a deep commitment to equity and inclusion.

Take a look at our full job description and application details. We encourage qualified candidates to apply and urge our valued stakeholders to share this opportunity with their networks. Interested candidates should submit their applications by February 28, at 12 pm EST. 

Coming Soon:

CCM staff will continue to build on what we accomplished last year and move forward with robust programming that continues to support and uplift community media in 2025. 

Black Media Initiative Director Cheryl Thompson-Morton will lead an online event in February, Black History Month, detailing how Black media archives the community’s history. Register here here for the gathering on Thursday, February 13, at 4 pm EST. The third annual Black Media Futures conference is set to take place in Mid-March (see the recordings from last year’s conference), and there will be more networking events, and an update to the Black media directory and map. 

Latino Media Initiative Director Jesenia De Moya Correa is connecting with community media outlets in the Los Angeles area about their response to the recent devastating fires, providing crucial information on relief resources and efforts to rebuild communities. The LMI will host events on how to cover Latino communities divided along socioeconomic, ethnic, racial, and political lines and a session in Spanish on what Congressional rulings and Executive Orders mean for the community and nonprofit entities. She is also updating the initiative’s map and directory. 

Asian Media Initiative Director Kavitha Rajagopalan issued a historical report last year on AAPI community media and will continue that work with research on misinformation, updating the map and directory, and continue running the Asian Media Women's Network community space. She plans to host events on how current immigration policies will impact AAPI media and journalist safety. We will share that information as it becomes available. 

With support from the NYC Mayor's Office of Media and Entertainment, we will once again run various cohorts and training programs about SEO optimization for editors, research skills, sustainable business practices, and the upcoming elections led by Communications Director Jehangir Khattak. Next week, Khattak will organize a forum, joined by four publishers in NYC - from the Jewish Post, Khasokhas, Manhattan Times/Bronx Free Press, and BK Reader - who will share their experiences, challenges, and innovative practices that have enabled them to deliver impactful journalism while exploring new revenue streams. Register here for the February 6 event.

In the coming weeks, we’ll announce our first AI Community Engagement Lab with our colleagues at J+, the professional development arm of Newmark J-School. We are looking foward to seeing how these incredible outlets leverage AI to better serve their communities. 

We're also excited to announce the LGBTQ+ Media Mapping Project that Research and Development Associate Jennifer Cheng will be working on in conjunction with News is Out, hosted by the Local Media Foundation, and the MacArthur Foundation.

Advertising Boost Initiative Director Darlie Gervais will continue to connect with publishers to discuss securing advertising, developing media kits, and tracking legislation in the city, state, and beyond that could have an impact on community media. Some important news on that front: New York State Senator Monica R. Martinez and Assemblymember Jennifer Lunsford have introduced the Lift Our Communities, Advertise Locally (LOCAL) Act, which would incentivize small businesses to place advertisements in community media. 

The proposed bill has strong support from a number of business associations as well as the Empire State Local News Coalition's more than 200 local New York newspapers. The Coalition explained that the proposal prioritizes “minority-, woman- or veteran-owned business, as well as any business with 10 employees or fewer, [which] would be eligible to receive a refundable tax credit of 80% of its first $5,000 of local advertising for a maximum credit of $4,000 per year. The program would be capped at $10 million annually.” 

"The LOCAL Act has the potential to be a game-changer for both small businesses and community media. By proposing a tax break for small businesses advertising in local outlets, this legislation would not only help them reach more customers but also strengthen the community news ecosystem that keeps New Yorkers informed. Strengthening local media with more advertising ensures they have the resources to continue delivering vital news to residents, keeping communities engaged and empowered,” Gervais explained.
