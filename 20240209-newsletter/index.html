A February preview: the Black education landscape and political advertising

We have an exciting few weeks coming at CCM and want to invite you to join us for two important events on Black History Month and elections coverage. 

Black Media Initiative Director Cheryl Thompson-Morton is hosting a news briefing for journalists covering education stories in their communities. The goal is to provide Black media journalists and those reporting on schools, school libraries, or education policy as it pertains to Black students with critical information and resources for covering the evolving education landscape. 

Between banning books about Black history, by Black authors, and even about the experiences of Black children and the ongoing debate about teaching critical race theory (CRT) in schools, that landscape is rife with important stories, conversations on how to best cover them within community media, and what role all types of media play in these narratives. 

As Thompson-Morton noted, “education has been a key topic for Black media since the Freedom's Journal, the first US newspaper owned and run by Black people. In a time when education is being weaponized to shroud accurate depictions of American history, Black History Month provides us with an opportunity to reflect on the past, and learn better ways to report on the history that is currently taking place in this sector.” 

A panel of experts including Denisha Allen of Black Minds Matter, José Luis Vilson of EduColor, and Joyce Burges of National Black Home Educators will discuss a host of issues including a primer on the history of education in Black America, anti-CRT measures, and the role and impact of homeschooling and Black-led schools in teaching curricula that counter anti-CRT and book banning policies. 

The event will also empower journalists with connections to experts in CCM’s Black Media Green Book, a directory of experts who have worked with or those who would like to work with Black media. Experts come from a range of fields including education, censorship, and law and the directory is updated regularly. 

Register for the event taking place on February 15 from 3-4pm EST!

CCM Communications Director Jehangir Khattak is also hosting an event later this month with a focus on understanding the ethical issues regarding political advertising and how that could impact community media’s reporting on elections this year. 

Led by Subramaniam (Subbu) Vincent, Director of Journalism and Media Ethics at the Markkula Center for Applied Ethics at Santa Clara University, the event will tackle issues of ethical guidelines, editorial integrity, and fairness amid the battle political candidates face in claiming local political ad dollars as well as campaign finance and fundraising. According to Axios, U.S. elections-related advertising is projected to reach approximately $16 billion this year and not all of that will be for the presidential race as mayoral and statewide office races are expected to be tense and competitive in many communities across the country. 

As Khattak explained, political campaigns, political action committees (PACs), and other associated groups reaching out to community media outlets is not new and, at least anecdotally, will continue this election cycle especially in the New York City metro region. And, this kind of outreach is not only invitations to public speeches or events, but also press releases, direct advertising, and asking for endorsements for not only specific candidates but also positions on certain initiatives on the ballots.  

“The main reason is the reach, readers' trust and loyal following community media publications enjoy in the communities they cover. This is especially true for in-language media,” Khattak said. 

The goal of this event is to help community media journalists recognize the terminology used in various types of political advertising as well as “identify the claims that are riding in the ads from ballot initiatives, what types of claims these are, and the implications for advertising decision-making and the outlet's branding,” as Vincent noted. 

He will discuss real world case studies/examples of political advertising to give journalists an idea about how they can make decisions on what political advertisements to accept for publication, the ethical implications of doing so, and “identifying the types of persuasive communication” used by advertisers, Vincent explained. 

In an era of fighting for sustainable business models and funding, rapidly evolving social media, misinformation, conversations on how community media can best cover elections are both crucial and need to include more information about the impact and implications of political advertising money.  

Register and join us on February 22 from 2-3:30pm EST!
