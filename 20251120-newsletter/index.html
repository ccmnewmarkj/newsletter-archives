✨CCM Highlights✨ 

🕵️‍♂️ CCM is also hosting a training on Smart Fact-Checking: Tools, Techniques, and AI Methods for Journalists today, Thursday, November 20 at 2 pm ET. The session will equip community media journalists with practical tools to combat misinformation and strengthen digital investigation skills. Through hands-on fact-checking exercises and advanced verification methods, participants will learn to authenticate sources, verify online content, and navigate AI-driven manipulation. Key techniques include using tools like Google Fact-Check Explorer, reverse image search, WatchFramebyFrame, Deepfake-o-meter, Rolliapp.com, and applying advanced search and geolocation strategies to track disinformation across platforms such as Discord, Reddit, Telegram, and VKontakte. 

💻CCM will also host a session on Digital Safety and Best Practices for Journalists on Thursday, December 11 at 2 PM ET. Hacking, phishing, doxing, surveillance, and identity theft - these are just some of the cybersecurity threats journalists might face today. Any security lapse could not only compromise a journalist’s safety, but that of their newsroom and also may affect the trust of their audience. This session will cover how you can secure your devices, data, your presence online, and communications, as well as how to be more aware of potential risks.

📰Community Media Minute🌎

What's happening in the community media world

🤝🏼Press Forward, an organization supporting local news through funding and partnerships, and Commoner Co., the consultancy co-founded by Newmark J-School’s Entrepreneur in Residence Darryl Holliday, have launched the Journalism Support Exchange (JSX). The tool, still in beta testing, aims to help local newsrooms quickly find the resources and organizations best suited to their needs. The database brings together more than 300 support providers — from legal help to audience development to tech tools — and lets users filter by newsroom size, location, or service type. Instead of relying on word-of-mouth or scattered lists, publishers can now make more informed, efficient choices about where to turn for help. JSX also offers a clearer view of the broader support ecosystem and the gaps that still need investment or expertise. 

📖One Good Read📒

Useful studies and reports to include in your week's reading

📈💰📊Based on three years of hands-on work with 357 outlets across the U.S. and Canada, a new report from LION Publishers, in partnership with Impact Architects, offers one of the clearest pictures yet of what helps independent local newsrooms actually become sustainable. (Nieman Lab has a good summary of the 21 key steps to take away from the report),. Their Sustainability Audit program combined a diagnostic assessment, one-on-one coaching, a customized action plan, and small grants to implement those changes, and the results were striking: Most participating newsrooms made real, measurable improvements, and those that reported back saw their median revenue grow by about 60% in a single year. One finding stands out for community and ethnic media especially: Outlets with even one person dedicated to revenue — not just editorial work — earned roughly 700% higher median revenue than those without such roles, showing how essential business capacity is to survival. The report also names a persistent gap many publishers face: Weak business systems and operations, which can hold back even the strongest journalism.

🧑🏽‍🏫Fellowships & Opportunities✒️

Newmark J-School’s J+, in collaboration with the News Product Alliance, are taking applications for the News Product Management Certification (NPMC) program. Mid- to senior-level news professionals who want to take on more product responsibilities are encouraged to apply for the part-time and virtual program that will bring together a diverse cohort of 25-30 participants from across the global news industry. At the end of the program, participants will present a final project that showcases their ability to lead strategic decision-making for a news product.The deadline is November 21.  

The International Women’s Media Foundation’s Safety Ambassadors America Fellowship is taking applications. The program will equip a cohort of 24 journalists with the skills, tools, and knowledge to identify and mitigate the physical, digital, and mental health risks associated with working in newsrooms. After completing the program, Safety Ambassadors will be able to provide peer-to-peer support, speak at journalism conferences, and conduct training. The deadline to apply is November 23. 

ProPublica is taking applications for its Photojournalism Fellowship. The NYC-based, two-year program is open to anyone with at least three years of professional photojournalism experience, as a staffer or freelancer. The Fellow will report to the senior editor of visual storytelling and work most closely with visual editors and reporters. The deadline to apply is December 1. 

Ohio University is accepting applications for its 2026 Kiplinger Fellowship. The focus this year will be on ‘Saving Local News: A Practical Guide’ and will be held at the Scripps College of Communication on campus in Athens, Ohio, between May 11-14, 2026. The deadline to apply is December 7. 

The National Press Foundation is accepting applications for its Widening the Pipeline Fellowship, which focuses on helping journalists of color with seven or less years of experience stay in and lead newsrooms around the country. The yearlong program involves virtual workshops, in-person training in Washington, D.C., and one-on-one mentorship with experienced industry professionals. The deadline to apply is December 14. 

The Tarbell Fellowship is a one-year program for journalists interested in covering artificial intelligence. Fellows will participate in a paid, nine-month placement at a major newsroom from June 8, 2026 to May 31, 2027. Fellows will also take part in a 10-week course covering AI and journalism fundamentals and attend a weeklong journalism summit in the San Francisco Bay Area. Host newsrooms in 2025 included Bloomberg, the Guardian, MIT Technology Review, Time Magazine, and many other leading outlets. The deadline to apply is January 7.
