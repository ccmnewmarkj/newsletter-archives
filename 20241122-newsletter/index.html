Welcome to our latest newsletter! A lot is happening this fall —from post-election reporting resources to grant opportunities, job listings, and events. Let's jump right in!


News 

Poynter’s MediaWise and YouTube have partnered to launch a teen-focused media literacy curriculum for educators to use. In a related story, Newmark J-School has also created a journalism curriculum for underserved high school students in NYC. 

The AI Community Engagement Lab is a six-month training program designed to empower community media journalists with artificial intelligence tools and techniques and enhance audience engagement. Developed by CCM and J+, Newmark J-School’s professional development arm, the tuition-free program will run from March to October 2025 and consists of one in-person meeting, then virtual biweekly classes, workshops, discussion groups, and a final project. Deadline is December 6. 

NBC News reports on the post-election, mass exodus from X, formerly known as Twitter, to other social media platforms because X is owned by tech billionaire Elon Musk. The recent Trump appointee has been eliminating many of the platform’s features and staff which were keeping bots, harassment, and misinformation at bay. In the UK, PressGazette says several news outlets have made the switch already to Bluesky, which NPR reports has seen a 500% increase in traffic in the last few weeks. ​​​​CCM is staying on Twitter for now but you can also follow us on LinkedIn and now Bluesky as well! Our friends over at Trusting News have put together what the platform calls a ‘starter pack,’ a collection of accounts that makes it easy to find and follow organizations and people you know working on a particular issue. There are more starter packs from Baltimore Beat, The CITY, one on solutions journalism, and one from MLK50 which includes their journalists and other mid-south community media outlets. 

Poynter reports on how Wisconsin Watch is pushing to fill fact deserts as local news disappears. This became particularly important during this recent election cycle. “Wisconsin Watch runs the state’s only standalone fact-checking initiative verified as a signatory of the International Fact-Checking Network’s Code of Principles.”  Nearly 30 U.S. states have no dedicated fact-checkers. 

The Local Media Association shares some philanthropy success stories from WRAL, Amsterdam News, and Post and Courier. The panel summary includes discussions about audience listening sessions, fortifying your outlet’s digital presence, and securing funding through local news collaborations. 

Pew Research Center has a new report on news influencers, which Pew defines as “individuals who regularly post about current events and civic issues on social media and have at least 100,000 followers on any of Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, X (formerly Twitter), or YouTube.” Some of the findings include that Americans1 in 5 , and even more for those under 30, said they get their news regularly from influencers on social media and the report found that most of these influencers were men with no journalism background, as Nieman Lab reported. 

Columbia Journalism Review reports on regulations involving reporting on protests and the associated interactions with police officers, an issue that may come up more often around the presidential inauguration especially. 

Nieman Lab tackled the issue of high grocery prices across the country by calling for a Wirecutter-type of grocery store review service local news outlets can provide their audience. 

The Civic Information Index is a new tool by the Listening Post Collective and Brown Information Futures Lab, with support from Press Forward. Using more than 20 indicators to assess “civic health” across U.S. counties, the Index has the goal of providing action items to make local news more sustainable and robust. 

InsideRadio reports that the Reynolds Journalism Institute and The Local Media Consortium, are expanding the Digital on Demand Services (DODS) program courtesy of a $2.5 million investment form the John S. and James L. Knight Foundation. The groups co-founded DDOS in 2022, and it supports local newsrooms with accessible content, training, and development resources to drive their digital transformation.

Fellowships & Opportunities

Newsrooms can now sign up for free resources to enhance their coverage of the post-election period in the U.S. through Election Urgent Care from a coalition of organizations led by the Knight Election Hub, Votebeat, and Hearken. Journalists covering U.S. elections may face unexpected challenges following the election. The Election Urgent Care team will help newsrooms continue to provide essential information to their communities during the post-election transfer of power. The team also includes representatives from Muckrock and OpenNews. Deadline is rolling and the Slack will be operational until December 11.  

The Pulitzer Center is seeking applications from journalists who want to report stories as part of Connected Coastlines, a nationwide climate reporting initiative in U.S. coastal states. Started in 2019, this initiative is building a consortium of newsrooms and independent journalists across the U.S. to report on the local effects of erratic weather patterns on coastal populations using the latest climate science. Deadline is rolling. 

Logic(s) magazine is accepting applications for its new fellowship for Palestinian journalists, co-administered with the Arab Reform Initiative. This program aims to support community-led storytelling on the intersection of technology and Palestinian experiences, strengthen the magazine’s contributions to this area of reporting, and redistribute resources to journalists from these communities. Logic(s) will provide training, workshops, and informal mentoring to fellows on domain-specific topics like secure communication, algorithmic models, and techno-culture. ARI provides bilingual (English/Arabic) programming including a workgroup on tech in the Middle East North Africa region. Final deadline is November 27. 

Quanta Magazine from the Simons Foundation is looking for an early-career science journalist for its spring 2025 writing fellowship. This six-month program will give the successful applicant extensive experience writing news and features about one or more areas that Quanta covers: physics, mathematics, biology and computer science. Deadline is November 30. 

The Transmitter from the Simons Foundation is seeking a science reporting fellow to report and write news articles for the site. This is an excellent opportunity for an early-career science journalist to cover the fast-moving field of neuroscience. This paid fellowship is full-time (40 hours per week) and is expected to start in January 2025 and will last 6 months. The fellowship will take place in-person at the Simons Foundation's New York City office. Deadline is November 30. 

Poynter is taking applications for its Leadership Academy for Women in Media (March 2025). Designed for female and nonbinary journalists, this program will support you to strengthen your managerial strengths; navigate ethical decision-making; strategize ahead of difficult conversations and negotiations; manage up, down and across generations; delegate with purpose; and create your unique formula for work-life chemistry. Deadline is December 6. 

Marquette University is accepting applications for its O’Brien Fellowship. In a world filled with untold stories, the O’Brien Fellowship aims to bring those to light. This Fellowship gives reporters the tools they need to document inequity and examine potential solutions. Fellows spend nine months digging deep while mentoring student journalists for a $75,000 salary stipend and additional support. Deadline is January 19. 

The Institute for Citizens and Scholars is accepting applications for its Higher Ed Media Fellowship. The Fellowship currently focuses on issues related to postsecondary Career and Technical Education (CTE), with topics including data sourcing and analysis, best practices for covering underrepresented communities, and the intersection of postsecondary education and civic learning. Funding is awarded every year to early and mid-career journalists completing CTE reporting projects. Deadline is February 1.

Events

December 3 (Online): Solutions Journalism Network is hosting a webinar to explore the basic principles and pillars of solutions journalism, talk about why it’s important, explain key steps in reporting a solutions story, and share tips and resources for journalists interested in investigating how people are responding to social problems.

December 5 (NYC): Visual Investigations, an exhibition that recently opened at The Architekturmuseum der TUM in Munich and Cooper Union are co-hosting an event about the emerging field of visual investigation, examining how architecture operates between advocacy, journalism, and law in the pursuit of justice and accountability. A series of seven case studies will be presented to contextualize the multifaceted roles architecture can play within these domains.

December 9 (NYC): Join WNYC’s Kai Wright, Newmark J-School’s Linda Villarosa and Terry McGovern from CUNY’s School of Public Health with stories from the early days of AIDS — and the people who refused to stay out of sight. The panel will be introduced by the Director of Audio Journalism Kalli Anderson.
