Community media is often defined by the resilience of those who produce it. This week, we honor that spirit with a must-read essay from CCM co-founder Garry Pierre-Pierre, who explores the deep, often painful work of claiming belonging in the face of exclusion. Pierre-Pierre is also the founder of The Haitian Times and director of partnerships at URL Media.

Resilience requires resources. This month’s Bulletin is focused on equipping you for the long haul. Inside, you’ll find emergency safety funds for Minnesota-based journalists, a grant for investigative reporting on race and criminal justice, and fellowships spanning everything from covering the Arctic climate to the aging beat. We’ve also curated a list of leadership and editorial roles to help you take the next step in your career.

As always, if you have a resource, read, or opportunity you’d like to see highlighted here, please send it our way. Email Newsletter Editor LaMonte Guillory at lamonte.guillory@journalism.cuny.edu.

One Good Read

“Always on the outside: Exile isn't about the country you leave." 

  • The Author: Garry Pierre-Pierre, Co-founder of CCM, Founder of The Haitian Times, and Director of Partnerships for URL Media.
  • The Blueprint: In this searing personal essay, Pierre-Pierre confronts the "pet-eating" hoax in Springfield, Ohio, not just as a political lie, but as a diagnosis of how the U.S. still views Black immigrants. He weaves a narrative that connects his parents' flight from the Duvalier regime to his own "dual exile"—the feeling of belonging everywhere and nowhere at once. He argues that exile is not just geography; it is the distance between who you are and who the world insists you must be.
  • The Gain: A masterclass in resilience and moral clarity. For community media publishers and others facing attacks, disinformation, or systemic erasure, this piece is a reminder that belonging is not granted by the state—it is built by our own hands, through our institutions, and our refusal to be silenced.
  • Read “Always on the outside” by Garry Pierre-Pierre. Does this piece resonate with you? If it does, reach out to us at lamonte.guillory@journalism.cuny.edu.

Professional Growth Opportunities

Trainings, Courses and Webinars​​​​​​

Beat Academy: The midterms, data and America’s safety net

  • The Blueprint: A free webinar, from the Poynter Institute, featuring lead faculty Jon Greenberg and Kate Cox, focused on the GOP's "One Big Beautiful Bill Act" (OBBBA) and its cuts to safety net programs like SNAP and Medicaid. The session features expert insights from guest panelists Colleen Heflin (Syracuse University), Elvia Malagón (Chicago Sun-Times), Paul Overberg (The Wall Street Journal), and Denice Ross (National Conference on Citizenship). Together, they will guide journalists on tracking how new work requirements affect specific communities using county and state-level data, while offering workarounds for accessing federal data that may be withdrawn or degraded.
  • The Gain: You will learn to quantify the local impact of these policy shifts, transforming abstract budget cuts into tangible stories about your audience's daily lives. The session provides the data literacy needed to report on safety net changes with authority and credibility.
  • Register for the Beat Academy session – Feb. 26, 1 pm ET / 12 pm CT / 10 am PT (Free)

Introduction to Investigative Journalism Training Course

  • The Blueprint: A free, intensive online course from the Global Investigative Journalism Network (GIJN) and iMEdD designed for journalists with general reporting experience (5+ years) but little to no background in investigations. Over 10 hands-on sessions, you will learn to "follow the money," conduct open-source research, and master interview techniques, all while working with a dedicated mentor.
  • The Gain: Transform your reporting practice by adding a powerful investigative toolkit to your repertoire. You’ll leave with the ability to dig deeper, expose systemic issues, and hold power accountable, regardless of your beat.
  • Apply for the Investigative Journalism Course by March 6

2026 Reporting Workshop: The Aging Beat

  • The Blueprint: A three-day in-person intensive workshop hosted by the Craig Newmark Graduate School of Journalism at CUNY (May 4-6) for journalists with at least three years of experience. Supported by the Congregational Home Legacy Fund (CHLF)—established to continue a century-long legacy of care for New York’s elderly—this program covers urgent issues like how climate change affects older adults and aging in prison, while providing hands-on training in data reporting and nursing home coverage.

  • The Blueprint: From the EVOME Institute (supported by the National Science Foundation and Woodwell Climate Research Center), a two-week immersion at the Toolik Field Station in the Alaskan Arctic (June 14-28). Fellows will work side-by-side with these experts to study how ecosystems adapt to rapid climate change, and get a front-row seat to research in a region warming three times faster than the global average.
