Empowering Asian, Black, and Latine-led newsrooms in fundraising

CCM is excited to announce its inaugural Fundraising for Community Media cohort, a group of 15 AAPI, Black, and Latine-led newsrooms who will be empowered to learn fundraising strategies. The tuition-free program, in collaboration with The Lenfest Institute for Journalism’s News Philanthropy Network, is the first of its kind, and was designed to address the specific issues of media leaders of color.

The rate of news outlets closing across the U.S. is increasing, illustrating the challenges of running a media outlet. Philanthropy has helped sustain some news operations, but nonprofit funding has historically not been beneficial for leaders of color. The jobs of community media publishers continue to be difficult as they face the challenges of navigating often walled-off funding opportunities, access to government agency advertising money, and the precarious nature of the journalism industry as a whole.

However, news philanthropy continues to remain an important potential lifeline for community media and organizations that support these publishers and journalists on the frontlines of daily American life. Media funders like the collaborative Press Forward initiative have made headlines of late for their announcements of multi-million dollar funding rounds.  

As Lenfest Institute’s Chief Advancement Officer Annie McCain Madonia noted: “Philanthropy is an essential part of the revenue mix for sustainable local news organizations. As an increasing number of funders and donors take an interest in supporting local journalism, it’s important that publishers have the fundraising skills necessary to make the most of these opportunities.” 

CCM’s Directors Cheryl Thompson-Morton (Black Media Initiative), Kavitha Rajagopalan (Asian Media Initiative), and Jesenia De Moya Correa (Latino Media Initiative) hope this program not only educates more community media leaders about philanthropy as a critical revenue stream, but also educates philanthropy about the importance of investing in community media as well. As Thompson-Morton explained: “Community media upholds many of the values that philanthropy cares about. Our aim with this program is to help the newsrooms leverage their unique strengths to build more financially resilient news organizations that can meet the information needs of these underrepresented communities.” 

Our cohort was chosen from a pool of over 80 applicants. These traditional and digital-first news operations serve a diversity of Asian American, Latine, multiracial, and Black communities. 

Beginning next week, instructors with decades of experience working as leaders of color in journalism will train the selected teams from Asian, Black and Latine-led newsrooms around the country over the course of three months through biweekly virtual sessions to develop business strategies and new revenue streams in addition to expanding the teams’ grant writing skills, audience prospecting, fundraising campaign planning, and understanding of the news philanthropy field as a whole to uncover new opportunities for funding. 

The cohort will kick off with a session led by Aligned Strategy Group on dismantling barriers to fundraising as leaders of color, and will include coaching and mentorship from community media leaders Melanie Figueiredo, Vice President of Advertising and Sponsorship Sales at URL Media; Kimberly Griffin, Publisher and Chief Revenue Officer of Mississippi Free Press; and Vandana Kumar, Publisher and CEO of India Currents. 

Our cohort also represents geographic diversity as one effort to combat news deserts - a particularly important task in an election year. Cohort members are based in California, Colorado, Florida, Georgia, Louisiana, Maryland, Michigan, North Carolina, Washington and the District of Columbia. "That's why it's crucial for donors and funders to support this ecosystem with resources. We see this program as a step toward financial sustainability for news outlets and a complete game-changer," said Mikhael Simmonds, CCM’s Executive Director.

Almost evenly split between nonprofit and for-profit organizations, the cohort reflects the understanding that all news media organizations, regardless of their business structure, should have access to funding opportunities.

Two-thirds of our first cohort are from newsrooms that have five or fewer full-time employees and some are even one-person operations. The reality is that these numbers need to be taken into account when discussing fundraising and training how to fundraise given the time and effort not only grant applications take, but also reporting requirements and outreach. 

Perhaps the most important statistic we found about the cohort: a majority of our selected organizations said they have applied for grants but could not raise funds or said they have no experience with fundraising. But, we do have newsroom teams who have had some success and are looking to enhance their skills in this cohort because this program is not only for teams to learn from the instructors, but to also build their networks and learn from each other as well.
