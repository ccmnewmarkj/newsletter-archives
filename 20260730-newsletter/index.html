Faith, Migration, and Civic Belonging
RNS Special Project: The Atlas of American Belonging | Religion News Service

  • The Blueprint: A multimedia reporting initiative and digital hub produced by Religion News Service (supported by $1 million in grants from the Henry Luce Foundation) examining how faith and immigration shape who belongs in America. Built as a lasting civic resource leading up to America’s 250th anniversary and beyond, the project collaborates with local newsrooms (such as El Paso Matters) to co-publish reporting across four central pillars: America’s Promise, Faith That Shelters, A State of Enforcement, and An Exodus.
  • The Gain: Faith institutions are often the most trusted, deeply rooted civic hubs within community and ethnic news ecosystems, yet faith as a beat is rarely integrated into local accountability reporting. This initiative provides community media leaders with a framework for covering faith not just as a matter of worship, but as a driving force behind immigration defense, legal aid, civic organizing, and local resilience.
  • Explore the Atlas of American Belonging here.

AI, Deskilling, and the Evolution of Newsroom Craft
Journalism Practice Study: The Skills Journalists Are Losing (and Gaining) from AI | Nieman Lab

  • The Blueprint: A new academic study published in Journalism Practice (and featured by Nieman Lab) examining how AI adoption leads to both "deskilling" and "upskilling" among working journalists. Drawing on in-depth interviews with reporters and editors, researcher Shangyuan Wu explores risks like the atrophy of foundational research and source-tracking skills, alongside the rising demand for higher-order human skills, specifically fact-checking, information discernment, copy editing, and original story ideation.
  • The Gain: As local newsrooms integrate AI tools for efficiency, leaders need a framework to ensure these technologies support, rather than erode, core reporting standards. This study offers newsroom managers practical insights into where reporters need guardrails against "cognitive debt" and where training should focus to sharpen editorial discernment, storytelling, and human-led investigative depth.
  • Read the Nieman Lab summary here.

Resources & Tools

Local Government Meeting Transcription & Video Highlight Platform | SeeGov

  • The Blueprint: A free, nonprofit digital platform that automatically collects, transcribes, and summarizes local government meetings. Currently used by more than 85 newsrooms across 26 states, the platform features automated meeting alerts, moment-by-moment summaries, smart transcripts with speaker identification, keyword search, and built-in tools to extract text quotes, vertical shorts, or embedded video clips directly without requiring video editing skills.
  • The Gain: Local journalists and civic creators can scan lengthy government proceedings faster, verify quotes, and produce short-form social video highlights or embedded story clips to boost audience engagement while maintaining full editorial control over context and coverage.
  • Access SeeGov for free here. (Free for monitoring up to three local governments, or up to 15 through the Civic Partner Program by publishing two monthly video highlights)

Campaign Finance AI Search Agent | DataTalk by Big Local News & Stanford OVAI Lab

  • The Blueprint: A free, open-access research tool developed by Big Local News (a program based at Stanford University) and the Stanford Open Virtual Assistant Lab that allows journalists to query federal campaign finance data using plain English. The agent converts natural-language questions about donors, candidate fundraising, and committee spending into database queries that run against official Federal Election Commission (FEC) bulk data, displaying the query, source code, data import dates, and result tables alongside every answer for complete verification.
  • The Gain: Local newsrooms without dedicated data specialists or programming expertise can quickly analyze complex federal election data for local accountability stories, track out-of-state money, and investigate major political donors while maintaining strict verification standards.
  • Access DataTalk for free here. (Questions or support can be directed to biglocalnews@stanford.edu)
