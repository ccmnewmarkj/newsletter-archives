CCM Highlights 

We are thrilled to share the LGBTQ+ Media Mapping Project, launched earlier this week. This groundbreaking project was the result of a partnership between News Is Out, a national collaborative of leading local queer news publishers, and CCM, the Local Media Foundation and the John D and Catherine T. MacArthur Foundation. The Map is a novel look at local LGBTQ+ media, with in-depth analysis of all aspects of their reach, size, impact and increasing need in a hostile and threatening political and cultural climate for LGBTQ+ people.  CCM’s Research and Development Associate Jennifer Cheng was the data visualization whiz behind the interactive map. Community Media Minute

What's been happening in the community media world

Networks and collaborations continue to strengthen the industry. The Current, a nonprofit news organization serving Lafayette and South Louisiana, is joining Deep South Today, becoming the third newsroom in the network alongside Mississippi Today and Verite News in New Orleans. The merger allows The Current to expand its reporting, staff, and access to resources through the network. 

Mergers and acquisitions are also becoming increasingly common. Noisy Creek, the parent company of The Stranger and Portland Mercury, has acquired the country's first and longest-running alternative weekly Chicago Reader. The publication, founded in 1971, joins the company’s other print alt-weeklies as part of Noisy Creek's plan to reinvest in local print media and show how local journalism can thrive through community support. 

In New York state, SUNY's Institute for Local News is experimenting with student reporters as a way to bolster independent and public news organizations across New York state. The Institute's first cohort of 20 paid journalism interns were placed in established newsrooms this summer, with plans to scale up in 2026. 

The ripple effects of the cuts to Corporation for Public Broadcasting funding are having an impact on media serving rural and Indigenous communities. High Plains Public Radio is planning on building a "contributors network" of part-time and volunteer community members to expand regional coverage while keeping costs down, per Nieman Lab. The station covers more than 300 communities across the Oklahoma and Texas panhandles. Stay tuned for how to join this network, once it launches.

One Good Read

Useful studies and reports to include in your week's reading

Here at CCM, we think a lot about how communities receive critical information. An insightful new report is out from Commoner Company, the consultancy co-founded by Newmark J-School’s Entrepreneur-in- Residence Darryl Holliday. The report proposes expanding the definition of local news beyond traditional journalism to include "information stewards"—anyone who intentionally creates and shares news and information with a defined audience information. Their new framework is a necessary shift that will help us all get closer to the ultimate goal of ensuring that communities are equipped with the news they need to help us all build a thriving and just democracy

Pro Tip

A tip or tool for your newsroom

New research from German scholars identifies 11 distinct relationship forms that journalists develop with audiences, ranging from traditional distant informing to more interactive approaches like cultivating fans and coping with troublemakers. Knowing what kind of relationship reporters, editors, and publishers have with your audience could help determine strategy to deepen that relationship and grow or expand your audience, and this research is an excellent tool for those wanting to delve deeper into audience development.

Fellowships & Opportunities

Tarbell offers grants of $1,000 - $15,000 to support original reporting on AI published in established outlets and its impacts across five key areas: investigations into frontier AI companies, national and international AI policymaking, integration of AI in governments and militaries, AI capabilities and evaluations, and the future of work and society. The application deadline is September 14.

The University of Chicago Booth School of Business’ Stigler Center has a 12-week Journalists in Residence program that is offering a $14,000 stipend and the opportunity to audit classes at Chicago Booth while participating in seminars and networking with scholars. The application deadline for the Spring 2026 program is October 1.

Tiny News Collective is hiring its inaugural Collective Fellow. The fellowship is aimed at early-career professionals with 1 to 3 years of experience who want to increase their knowledge and skills in community-rooted media, community management, nonprofit management and the journalism support landscape. There will be an information session on September 17.
