Primary Season in Full Swing: Elections Fellows Report

With voters in seven states heading to the polls for primary elections this week and New Yorkers gearing up for upcoming June 28 and August 23 primaries across the state, election news is in the headlines and CCM’s 2022 NY State Elections Reporting Fellows are hard at work.

Redistricting remains a hot topic in New York community media, with an explainer from Andrea Pineda-Salgado for Queens-based Epicenter; a story on the court battle over the redistricting map from South Asian Times’ Parveen Chopra; a report on a new district created in Queens from Yiyan Zheng for Chinese-language World Journal. For BKLYNER, Liena Zagare covers the candidate slated to win a new Brooklyn senate district; for Manhattan Times, Sherry Mazzocchi describes the shifting districts in the Bronx and Washington Heights; for Bushwick Daily, Amanda Salazar reports how gerrymandering almost broke apart two districts in Brooklyn; and Emma Davey reflects that new district maps are kind of wild for Greenpointers. AJ Woodson looks at what voter suppression and redistricting mean for Black representation in Black Westchester.

Several fellows are looking at how engaged their communities are in local elections. For The Runner News, Mohammad Abedin reports that the Bangladeshi community has emerged as a key factor in Buffalo politics; for The Parkchester Times, Marufat Ibrahim asks how much of a stake Muslim leaders have in the electoral process; in Korean-language Korea Daily, Eunbyul Kim reports on waning Korean immigrant support for the Democratic Party.

Some fellows are covering candidates running to represent Chinese, Nepali, South Asian, and Muslim communities; while others are highlighting low interest in voting, how candidates are not addressing their communities’ concerns or what they have to say about the affordable housing crisis, what elections mean for immigrants, and why many NYers still don’t understand ranked-choice voting.

Follow #NYSElectionReportingFellows on Twitter for more great community media coverage of the 2022 Elections, through Election Day in November.

Latino Media Summit Responds to the Demands of the Times



This weekend, we were delighted to bring the fifth annual Latino Media Summit back in person, at The Clemente Soto Vélez Cultural and Educational Center on New York City’s Lower East Side. With 40+ speakers over 20 hours, the summit brought together journalists serving Latinx communities across the U.S. in powerful conversations, challenging workshops, inspiring networking and brainstorming sessions, and uplifting performance.

Pivoting to respond to the tragedy in Uvalde, Texas, the summit introduced a new opening session just days in advance, featuring a difficult and ultimately deeply motivating conversation between The New Yorker’s Stefania Talaridad, The Trace’s Mike Spies, and activist and father of a slain Parkland student Manuel Oliver. The summit offered prescient and resource-rich responses to some of the hardest questions on how to support Latinx news audiences through division, trauma, exploitation, and survival.

Asian Media Women’s Network Launches



On May 27, the last week of AAPI Heritage Month, CCM convened the first gathering of the Asian Media Women’s Network, a community space for and by women and female-identifying people working in Asian community media. Journalists and newsroom leaders serving Chinese, Korean, Nepali, Vietnamese, Indonesian, Malaysian, and pan-Asian American communities joined this first gathering.

Meeting the last Thursday of each month, with a biweekly bulletin and messaging group, the network will offer a private space for women journalists to share resources and find mutual support as they navigate misogyny, online harassment, disinformation, anti-Asian hate, and resource strain in their daily work to serve often overlooked Asian American communities. In this space, women in Asian media will also be able to celebrate and learn from each other's insightful journalism on the diverse, complex communities they serve. Are you interested in joining the network? Email Kavitha.
