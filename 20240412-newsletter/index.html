News from Inside the Community Media Sector


 
Our sincere condolences to the loved ones of James A. Washington. The President and General Manager of The Atlanta Voice, 73, passed away last week. Washington had participated in past cohorts of our Black Media Initiative, was a tireless advocate for Black media, and was a 2019 National Association of Black Journalist Legacy Award winner. Washington is survived by his wife, The Atlanta Voice publisher Janis L. Ware, children, grandchildren, and many family members, friends, and colleagues.
 

The Editor of Cleveland.com/The Plain Dealer wrote a letter about the news outlet’s coverage of Donald Trump after several readers’ accused them of bias against the former president as he runs for office once again. “We tell the truth, even when it offends some of the people who pay us for information,” wrote Chris Quinn. 

Better News explored how The Times Union in Albany, NY partnered with a nearby university to build a data-rich, low-cost weather and climate product modeled after sibling Hearst outlet San Francisco Chronicle. 

ProPublica announced its 50 State Initiative, committing the outlet to publishing in every state over the next five years. The stories will come from a growing team of journalists reporting from communities across the country and groundbreaking partnerships with local news organizations through the Local Reporting Network program.

Yahoo is buying AI news app Artifact from the Instagram co-founders, according to The Verge.

Nieman Lab explored how legislation aimed at fake news and misinformation might actually be detrimental to stem the tide in 2024, which has the world participating in an unprecedented number of elections worldwide.

After more than 50 media orgs, including WHYY News, and community groups collaborated to cover the mayoral election last year, the Every Voice, Every Vote initiative returns to Philadelphia with new focus.

NPR discussed how fossil fuel giant Chevron owns the main local news source in Richmond, CA, The Richmond Standard, which means many stories in the area are not covered. When open flames shot upward from four smokestacks at the Chevron refinery on the west side of the city, residents were forced to stay indoors – without any real safety information.

Here’s a dive into how artificial intelligence is quietly revolutionizing the news, from Quartz.

An OP-ED from Jeff Jarvis of Newmark J-School appears in Nieman Lab about how The California Journalism Preservation Act would do more harm than good and how the state might better help news outlets.

NPR defends its journalism after a senior editor says it has lost the public's trust.

An OP-ED in Poynter takes a look at how newsrooms should treat the electorate, its audience,  like the hiring committee for public servants that it actually is.

Poynter also reports on how New Orleans’ news outlet The Lens, led by Karen Gadbois, is a pioneering nonprofit newsroom and ponders a future beyond its founder.

Fellowships & Opportunities

Newmark J-School is taking applications for its Executive Program in News Innovation and Leadership. This year-long certificate program — which runs from September 2024 to June 2025 — invites global media leaders with backgrounds in editorial, product, business and technology from local, national, and international news organizations to apply. The program operates on a hybrid model, allowing participants to engage in virtual sessions while convening three times a year for in-person residencies in New York City. Akin to an MBA, the curriculum imparts holistic leadership, business, and management training to senior-level journalists and media managers. Deadline is May 3.

U.S. Democracy Day was launched in 2022 as a collaborative effort to raise awareness about the threats to democracy in the U.S. The collaboration strives to continue its mission of meeting newsrooms of all shapes and sizes where they’re at by creating its first-ever Ambassador program, which will empower news leaders and changemakers to recruit new participants across public media, broadcast media, and the national press. Deadline is April 15.

Democracy Now! is accepting applications for its Fellowship program. The fellowships are full-time, one-year work placements that pay a livable wage and include fully paid medical and dental benefits. The fellowships provide support and mentorship to underrepresented early-career journalists and digital media professionals, including BIPOC, people with disabilities and gender nonconforming people.

KFF, an award-winning newsroom that covers all aspects of the U.S. healthcare system and examines how health issues impact people’s lives, is taking applications for its Peggy Girshman Fellowship. This full-time, salaried position will preferably be based in KFF’s Washington, D.C., office.

The Google News Initiative’s Pre-launch Lab program, supporting early stage entrepreneurs in journalism, is taking applications. The eight-week, fully-remote program helps entrepreneurs launch successful, trusted digital news organizations. Solo founders or teams of aspiring entrepreneurs who have identified a community’s need for better news and want guidance and coaching on how to launch, or continue to develop, a news offering that delivers a solution rooted in journalism, are welcome to apply. Deadline is May 31. 

Southern California journalists who have been affected by layoffs are welcome to apply for employment support through the Asian American Journalists Association - LA Chapter’s Randy Hagihara Uplift Fund. Deadline is June 2.

Events

April 16 (NYC): Newmark J-School is hosting Decoding the Latino Vote: How It Will Shape the 2024 Election. The panel discussion will feature Democratic strategist Chuck Rocha and Republican consultant Mike Madrid. The conversation and Q&A will be moderated by Noticias Telemundo’s Arantxa Loizaga.
