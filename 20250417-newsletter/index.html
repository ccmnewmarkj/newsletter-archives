News 

Newmark J-School’s Journalism Protection Initiative, led by Joel Simon, and the journalist safety coalition ACOS Alliance, introduced the Journalist Expert Safety Support (JESS) project to improve access to safety guidance for a greater number of journalists, particularly freelancers and those without in-house security teams. The AI-powered tool will be a clearinghouse for advice and guidelines from security experts across large media organizations and NGOs to navigate reporting on conflicts, protests, natural disasters, and other reporting scenarios. 

Houston Landing announced it is shutting down in May due to fundraising challenges. The nonprofit newsroom launched in February 2023 with a $20 million investment after a survey from American Journalism Project demonstrated a need for those in Houston to have access to deep, local reporting. The board said it is in discussions with the Texas Tribune to establish a local presence as it expands its coverage around the state. 

The Alliance for Audited Media launched an ethical AI certification to help media outlets navigate the use of AI in newsroom operations and editorial functions responsibly and to help build trust with their audiences. The program covers accountability, bias, transparency and disclosures, privacy, and permissions. 

The Spokesman-Review is going nonprofit, per Poynter. The Cowles family is donating the Spokane, Washington, newspaper to Comma Community Journalism Laboratory, a nonprofit based at Gonzaga University, so it can continue to operate and serve the community. 

From The Oregonian, state legislators have introduced a bill that could require large tech firms like Google and Meta to fund local newsrooms to the tune of at least $122 million a year. Proponents say search engines and social media platforms have benefitted from locally-produced content. However, tech companies are not the only opposition to the bill. Local news outlet Lookout Eugene-Springfield’s founder spoke out against the bill. The recently launched newsroom is largely funded by the Google News Initiative. 

Audience analysis can not only provide useful insights for editorial teams, but also teach newsrooms about generative AI, Poynter reported. 

Gannett, parent company to USA Today and more than 200 newsrooms across the country, launched a true crime product last month based on its robust local coverage. The standalone subscription to Witness gives readers everywhere access to locally-led crime reporting from the company’s network of reporters, Nieman Lab reported. 

Nieman Lab also looked at how college newspaper reporters are taking national stories about everything from tariffs to environmental legislation rollbacks and Title IX and making them relevant through a community lens.

Fellowships & Opportunities

The International Women’s Media Foundation hosts the Elizabeth Neuffer Fellowship that provides academic and professional opportunities to women journalists covering human rights and social justice. Fellows complete research and coursework at MIT’s Center for International Studies and participate in internships with media outlets, with a stipend. The deadline to apply is April 20. 

For early career journalists, the Robert Novak Journalism Fellowship at the Fund for American Studies is taking applications. Fellows can pursue projects requiring deep reporting they otherwise would not be able to in the year-long program which also provides a grant for income and project costs. The deadline to apply is April 27. 

Apply to be a Visiting Journalist at the Russell Sage Foundation. Journalists have the opportunity to do a reporting project for up to four months alongside scholars at the foundation about any topic falling under RSF’s “mission to "improve the social and living conditions in the United States." The deadline to apply is May 6.

Events

April 14 - May 11 (Online): The Knight Center hosts a free, four-week training session, Prompt Engineering 101 for Journalists, with Joe Amditis, an expert in AI and journalism at Montclair University. 

April 23 (Online): URL Media hosts a session for those interested in launching a media company with newsroom founders and entrepreneurial experts, including S. Mitra Kalita (URL Media, Epicenter-NYC), Mazin Sidahmed (Documented), Travis Rundlet (Rundlet Advisory) and Janelle Zagala (URL Media, Epicenter).

April 30 (online): Online News Association hosts a webinar, Advanced AI Data Journalism Tools, that will explore PDFs, data scraping, and analysis tools - some outside of AI. Tickets are $25 or free for ONA members.

May 2-3 (NYC): The Society of Professional Journalists holds its Region 1 Conference at Newmark J-School. The conference theme is Reporting in Turbulent Times and includes several panels and a keynote address from CNN correspondents Polo Sandoval and Gloria Pazmino. 

May 8 (Online): Online News Association also hosts a case study webinar with New Hampshire Public Radio to discuss the organization’s strategic marketing, audience development, and revenue generation that have made it sustainable, with The Podglomerate.

May 15-16 (Denver): The Center for Cooperative Media at Montclair State University is hosting the 2025 Collaborative Journalism Summit. The Summit is still open to session pitches and will feature two days of lightning talks, workshops, and participatory discussions about a wide array of topics and projects.

May 19-20 (Detroit): Online News Association (ONA) and Newsroom Robots host the AI Leadership Summit to gather leaders and executives to discuss how AI has impacted the news industry and newsroom leadership and culture in outlets of all sizes. 

May 29 - June 1 (Los Angeles):  The Association of Health Care Journalists hosts Health Journalism 2025, a four-day conference of field trips, sessions, and workshops. Journalists will be joined by health care professionals, policymakers, and other experts to tackle the most pressing issues facing health care, public health, and news coverage. 

September 3-5 (St. Louis): Local Independent Online News (LION) Publishers hosts the Independent News Sustainability Summit, a national conference that will gather independent news publishers, industry experts, and supporters of independent news to share expertise, lessons learned, and actionable advice on running a news business for solopreneurs and others.
