News from Inside the Community Media Sector

Our friends over at the Center for Cooperative Media issued a report on the state of collaborative journalism in the U.S. in 2024. The Center is also wrapping up its Collaborative Journalism Summit in Detroit. 

Lookout Santa Cruz, Invisible Institute, and City Bureau are just some of the smaller newsrooms that can be found all over this year’s Pulitzer Prize winners list; this was also the first year two winners disclosed using AI in their reporting.

As the Los Angeles Times reported, working-class Latino students now have a pathway to becoming journalists. 

The Jersey Bee discussed using journalism to improve public health in their community. 

The Chattanooga Times Free Press helped readers decide what to eat next with a paid newsletter initiative, Better News reported.

A new group formed by former disinformation czar Nina Jankowicz, the American Sunlight Project, has joined the fight against online misinformation - especially as it relates to the 2024 elections, The New York Times reported.

OP-ED in Nieman Lab: The press rewards negative politicians with attention, and that engenders more negativity.

A new Pew Research Center survey of more than 5,000 adults, found that a majority of people think local news providers are doing very well or somewhat well financially, Poynter reported.

AI is disrupting the local news industry: Will it unlock growth or be an existential threat? A new report from Northwestern University’s Medill Local News Initiative tries to answer that question. 

Former New York Times reporter and co-creator of the new HBO Max show ‘Girls on the Bus,’ Amy Chozick spoke to Poynter about the show based on her 2018 memoir “Chasing Hillary: Ten Years, Two Presidential Campaigns and One Intact Glass Ceiling” which explored her experiences covering Hillary Clinton’s presidential campaigns in 2008 and 2016. 

Eight daily newspapers are suing Microsoft and OpenAI for allegedly “harvesting millions of copyrighted articles to create their cutting-edge ‘generative’ artificial intelligence products including OpenAI’s ChatGPT and Microsoft’s Copilot.” MediaNews Group-owned Mercury News, Denver Post, Orange County Register and St. Paul Pioneer-Press; Tribune Publishing’s Chicago Tribune, Orlando Sentinel and South Florida Sun Sentinel; and the New York Daily News are the outlets involved in the suit. In another lawsuit against OpenAI, The New York Times has spent $1 million thus far.

Fellowships & Opportunities

Local publishers can now apply for Press Forward’s first open call for funding, which is focused on addressing historic coverage gaps in local news. At least 100 local newsrooms will receive grants of around $100,000. Local news organizations with budgets up to $1 million are eligible. Sign up for complimentary fundraising coaching and grant writing support, offered in partnership with the Lenfest News Philanthropy Network. Press Forward is hosting a Q&A with its staff on Monday June 3 at 2 p.m. EDT/11 a.m. PDT. Register here. Deadline is June 12.

The Evanston RoundTable, the community’s leading source of news about local government, schools, civic and artistic activities, is partnering with Northwestern University to offer a one-year full-time fellowship to cover race and social justice in Evanston, IL. Deadline is rolling. 

The National Association of Black Journalists (NABJ) is taking applications for its Apple News Fellowship. The program is designed to expose the fellow to the app’s key editorial areas, including breaking news, audio, and newsletters. You must be a recent graduate and a current NABJ member in good standing. Deadline is May 17. 

Applications are now open for the Local Media Association Lab for Journalism Funding to learn how to develop reporting projects that can be funded through philanthropy. Up to 20 media companies will participate in the six-month program planned from June through November 2024. Apply by May 24. 

The Lenfest Institute is expanding their Engaged Cities Community of Practice, which convenes news organizations with engagement-first approaches to serving U.S. cities. Up to 15 organizations will be selected to join Engaged Cities as the community’s second group of participating publications. Members will have opportunities to collaborate with peers, apply for grant funding, and attend in-person gatherings. If your organization seems like a good fit, submit an interest form by May 24.  

The Google News Initiative’s Pre-launch Lab program, supporting early stage entrepreneurs in journalism, is taking applications. The eight-week, fully-remote program helps entrepreneurs launch successful, trusted digital news organizations. Solo founders or teams of aspiring entrepreneurs who have identified a community’s need for better news and want guidance and coaching on how to launch, or continue to develop, a news offering that delivers a solution rooted in journalism, are welcome to apply. Deadline is May 31. 

Applications for The 19th’s 2024-25 Frances Ellen Watkins fellowship are open. Named for the “mother of African-American journalism,” the program was launched to provide recent graduates, mid-career alums and former students of Historically Black Colleges and Universities with full-year, salaried fellowships with full benefits in the areas of reporting, audience engagement and news product and technology. Deadline is May 31. 

Southern California journalists who have been affected by layoffs are welcome to apply for employment support through the Asian American Journalists Association - LA Chapter’s Randy Hagihara Uplift Fund. Deadline is June 2. 

Documented is accepting applications for its third annual Isaac Rauch Fellowship. The program is designed to enable a reporter to pursue a story they would not be able to without adequate longer term funding. The money can be used on a series of stories about a certain topic, or one ambitious feature. It can be a documentary or a written story. Deadline is June 8.  

The New York Times is taking applications for its Local Investigations Fellowship. This program is a one-year investigative reporting fellowship to develop the next generation of great reporters to do this essential type of accountability journalism at the local level. They are looking for fellows who have a few years of professional experience covering a local beat and who are ready to tackle an ambitious investigative project but lack the time, resources and guidance to produce it. Deadline is September 3.

Events

May 15 (Online): Scripps is hosting a webinar on Transitioning Your Career from Print to Broadcast. You will hear from several veteran journalists who changed career paths and learned new skills to focus on video-driven storytelling with the E.W. Scripps Company.

May 16 (Online): CCM is hosting Demystifying AI Visuals for Community Media. Please join John Smock, Director of Photojournalism at Newmark J-School and Paris M. Brown, Associate Publisher, The Baltimore Times, for a 90-minute training session that aims to examine how newsrooms can ethically use AI-generated visuals while also keeping issues and risks in mind. 

May 23 (Online): CCM is hosting Green Book Kickoff: How to Build Your Tech Stack. Join our Black Media Initiative and Michael Grant, the CEO and founder of Get Current Studio, to discuss newsrooms looking to adapt to the evolving digital landscape, ensuring they can efficiently produce, distribute, and monetize content across various platforms and channels. A well-constructed tech stack also enables seamless integration between different software and platforms which enhances operational efficiency and delivers compelling content experiences to their audience. In 2023, the Black Media Initiative commissioned the Black Media Green Book, a resource that aims to make connecting with freelancers, experts and technology partners easier and more accessible for Black media outlets.

June 6-9 (Detroit): Black Owned Media Equity and Sustainability Institute (BOMESI) is hosting the Black Owned Media Weekend conference that will bring together Black media owners/publishers for actionable insights tied to business longevity, authentic community-building and resource sharing, and enriching activities related to our history. They are still accepting applications from speakers as well.
