Newmark J-School hosts Black Twitter Summit
  
Credit: Yuri Samoilov
On February 15, the Newmark J-School hosted the Black Twitter Summit. Conveners including an academic panel of Drs. Meredith Clark of Northeastern University, André Brock Jr. of Georgia Institute of Technology, Charlton McIlwain of NYU, and Johnathan Flowers of California State University, Northridge. The Tow-Knight Center for Entrepreneurial Journalism was also a co-producer of the all-day event.

The roughly 30 attendees included communications scholars, journalists, two of the original developers of the platform, funders, and activists. CCM Black Media Initiative Director Cheryl Thompson-Morton and Executive Director Mikhael Simmonds were also in attendance.

The purpose of the event was to examine in more depth the history, current state, and future of Black communities across social media platforms. Of particular interest was to examine how the communities' experiences on Twitter have changed since Elon Musk bought the company in October 2022. Among Musk’s reforms of the platform: letting go of teams in charge of combating misinformation, the team ensuring the health of interactions on the platform to combat abuse and harassment, and employee resource groups - Twitter Blackbirds among them.

The day was divided in six sessions: the history and significance of Black Twitter, corporate social media and the perils of acquisitions, marginalized community-centered design of digital spaces, social media alternatives to Twitter for the Black community at large, and an off the record session.

As with any event on the record, there were a variety of reactions to speakers, invited panelists, and the topics being discussed, which can be examined under the #BlackTwitterSummit hashtag.

"The Black Twitter Summit brought together a diverse set of voices to look at the social media platform. Throughout the day participants learned important aspects of the technologists' view of developing Twitter, the need to hear from a wider diversity of voices in the Black community, and set up a foundation for future conversations surrounding digital safety. We also explored how nuance can be lost in the 24/7 news cycle, and how we might tailor social media platforms to meet the needs of different communities," said Thompson-Morton, summarizing the day's events.

News from Inside the Community Media Sector

Congratulations to CEO Peter Bhatia, Editor in Chief Mizanur Rahman, and the rest of the team on the launch of Houston Landing, a new outlet with the goal of serving communities in Houston, Texas with no paywalls or subscription fees! The digital-only outlet was borne out of a survey done by American Journalism Project and a call from communities to stop being “left out of the news” in the wake of Hurricane Harvey. 

Three cheers to the coalition of Indiana organizations and philanthropists who raised over $10 million to launch the Indiana Local News Initiative! 

A round of applause for the Prison Journalism Project for launching the Prison Newspaper Project, a hub to support the work of prison newspapers and magazines around the country. By PJP’s latest count as of January 2023, 22 prison newspapers currently operate across 11 states.

The Golden Transcript, one of the news outlets owned by Colorado Community Media, investigated its own history with the help of Black community groups, librarians, and historians and the result is a front page story titled, For the record: Examining how the Transcript contributed to systemic racism.

For publishers looking to launch a new product, check out how the San Antonio Express News created a winning newsletter strategy. 

Poynter writes about how the Hearst newspapers hired a therapist to help journalists struggling in the midst of covering difficult stories on everything from school shootings, violence against and within the Asian, Latino, and Black communities, to the pandemic. The first newsroom in the effort is the San Francisco Chronicle. 

The Detroit Free Press discusses how it uses personas to better gauge readers’ interest and offer them a wider variety of content. 

The American Press Institute takes a look at the future of social media beyond Twitter and Meta.

In collaboration with Gallup, the Knight Foundation is publishing a series of reports about the state of the news industry. The first part examines how Americans look at the news and the urgency of developing revenue models that will support trustworthy journalism today. The second portion of the series expands the understanding of the emotional factors that drive attitudes about and trust in the news. Trusting News dives a bit deeper into Part 2 and wrote about why news outlets need to be vocal with their communities about why they need money. 

Along those lines, The Lenfest Institute has published nine lessons from the 2023 News Philanthropy Summit on fundraising and development efforts. 

The Pivot Fund’s Tracie Powell dives deeper into the recent “The Roadmap for Local News” report for Nieman Lab, calling for more space at the proverbial table for not just more people of color in the news industry but also “a diversity of lived experiences and thought” in the types of people and news outlets which get funding. 

Steven Waldman, founder of the new initiative Rebuild Local News, spoke with The Guardian about why local news outlets in the U.S. need tax breaks to help save democracy.

Read about how ProPublica’s local stories reach the communities being reported on through its Local Reporting Network.

Fellowships & Opportunities

The Dart Center for Journalism and Trauma is accepting applications for the 2023 Ochberg Fellowship, a seminar program for senior and mid-career journalists who wish to deepen their knowledge of emotional trauma and psychological injury, and improve reporting on violence, conflict and tragedy. Deadline is March 1. 

Type Media Center is accepting applications for the Ida B. Wells Fellowship. The one-year fellowship helps reporters complete their first substantial work of investigative reporting by providing a $25,000 award and intensive mentoring from dedicated editors at Type Investigations. Deadline is March 12. 

ProPublica is taking applications for its Investigative Editor Training Program, which is designed to help expand the ranks of editors with investigative experience in more newsrooms across the country, with a focus on people from underrepresented backgrounds. Deadline is March 13.

The Open Notebook is accepting applications for the Sharon Dunwoody Science Journalism Mentoring Program, a free, nine-month mentoring program aimed at increasing the diversity of voices covering science and supporting journalists who are from underrepresented communities or who have experienced higher than average barriers to entry to the field. Deadline is March 17. 

The New Jersey Civic Information Consortium has an open call for grant proposals. In 2023, the Consortium will focus on funding projects supporting the journalism pipeline, civic engagement, and election-related work. Grants up to $100,000 will be awarded from a $1 million pool. Deadline is March 31.

The Women's Media Center's Progressive Women's Voices 2023 is taking applications for its leadership and training program. Representing a range of expertise and diversity across race, class, geography, sexual orientation, ability, and generation, participants receive advanced, comprehensive training and tools to position themselves as media spokespeople in their fields, thereby changing the conversation on issues that fill headlines. Deadline is April 2.
