The 2024 elections are less than a year away

While it may seem far away, this past week’s debate between Republicans vying for the party’s presidential nomination put one date into much sharper focus: Tuesday, November 5, 2024. 

Not only is President Joe Biden up for re-election but so are a plethora of Congress members, state legislature officials, and local officials. As experts in what your community cares about and how they may vote on a set of issues or even for a specific candidate, the phrase ‘all politics is local’ has never been more true than a flashy - and perhaps controversial - presidential election year. 

The media landscape has changed since the last big election and that may affect how you cover certain races and political rallies. Anti-semitism, Islamophobia, anti-Asian and Black hate incidents have been on the rise. (Back in June, we hosted a symposium regarding how hate crimes rise during election years. You can read the recap here and watch the recordings.) We are officially ‘post Covid’ but the threat of infection still looms. Twitter has imploded, taking with it a crucial news source for covering events from afar and relating it to your audiences’ day to day lives. 

But, as journalists we always find a way to get the story and serve our communities in a way larger media outlets do not and cannot - and this coming year, albeit more challenging, will be no different. 

To help with that, we have compiled a list of resources we think will help go on the campaign trail in your city, state, or region and cover everything from mayoral races to ballot measures and election law quirks. 

One saying to keep in mind: “horse race” 

New York University journalism professor Jay Rosen has been imploring newsrooms to focus on the consequences of the election rather than the theater of the race itself. Rosen is referring mostly to the presidential election and broadcast news, but the idea is a good one for any newsroom to use as a north star. Rather than the wardrobe choices or lifestyle of a candidate, there are important stories to be told about how that candidate feels about school board budget appropriations, funding recycling programs and green jobs training, or initiatives to bring new investments to the area. As Better News put it, the goal is to create “a significant, authentic public service experience around local politics.”

Ballot measures are king

Unlike larger news outlets, community media publishers know first hand how reporting about ballot initiatives is on some level even more important to do properly than a statewide or national candidate’s election. Lawmakers have gotten increasingly more sophisticated with their language and wording as well, which could prove to trip up even experienced native English speakers let alone those in our communities whose first language is not American English. The Journalist’s Resource wrote about research on how ballot format, wording and news coverage affect voters. 

Many of you may prepare an election guide for your audiences to understand the consequences of a particular ballot measure and Hearken has tips on how to make your Voter Guide iconic. 

Staying safe

Journalist safety is every newsroom’s first priority and during a politically charged time in America, it is an ever bigger concern. The International Women’s Media Foundation has a free online course on How to Report Safely and A Guide to Protecting Newsrooms and Journalists Against Online Violence.

As community-based journalists your role often goes beyond just reporting to being on the frontlines of protecting American democracy. Election SOS has a scenario planning guide for journalists to not only protect each other from harm but also protect the act of voting and the results of any election. 

CCM also has an active partnership with Cornell Law's First Amendment Clinic. Anyone looking for pro bono representation can reach out to Heather Elyse Murray, managing attorney of their local journalism project: hem58@cornell.edu

Stay tuned to this space as we will continue to provide resources about reporting on the elections. And, please contact us with any election-related trainings you would like to see or questions you have.

Big congratulations to Baltimore Beat, City Bureau, Conecta Arizona, Documented NY, El Timpano, Outlier Media, Prism, Resolve Philly, Scalawag, TransLash Media, and Charlottesville Tomorrow! 

In the Washington Post, a right-wing site’s reporting on mayor raises ethics questions after his death.

Poynter reported on how Russian falsehoods spread to the US through faux local news.

Nieman Lab discusses how 13 news publishers are using WhatsApp Channels.

Miles W. Griffis and Betsy Ladyzhets launched a reader-funded digital newsroom dedicated to covering Long Covid. Called The Sick Times, the co-founders “didn’t want to just cover Long Covid. We will cover all the chronic, complex, post-viral, post-pathogen, “mysterious” diseases of the past and future. We will cover these diseases through the context of science and health, of course, but also address their economic and cultural contexts, accountability for powerful institutions, day-to-day problems for people dealing with symptoms, and so much more,” Ladyzhets wrote. 

As the conflict in Gaza continues, the Washington Post reported that hundreds of journalists in major US newsrooms have signed an open letter protesting how their organizations have been covering Israel and Palestine, and the responsibility mainstream media has in the public perception of the conflict. 

Nostalgia as a beat? How Newsday in Long Island, NY is turning look-back coverage into a surprise driver of new subscribers, in Better News.

ESPN tries to compete with DraftKings and FanDuel by launching ESPN Bet, its sports betting platform, Axios reported. 

Caitlyn Dewey, a Buffalo, NY-based journalist, offers up 11 ways she has used artificial intelligence to improve her journalism, for Poynter.

Reporters Without Borders and 16 partner organizations have released the Paris Charter on AI and Journalism, which seeks to establish a set of ethics and principles journalists, newsrooms, and media outlets around the world will be able to adopt and apply in their work with artificial intelligence.

Federal Communications Commission (FCC) Chairwoman Jessica Rosenworcel recently shared a proposal to further the agency’s support for local journalism. The FCC has not provided much detail on it just yet, but the proposal “explores incentivizing the production of local programming by prioritizing the processing and review of applications from broadcast stations that invest in and prioritize local programming in communities across the country.”

Fellowships & Opportunities

The Pulitzer Center provides grants to cover the hard costs of reporting projects. The only broad parameter is that projects address global systemic crises. The Pulitzer Center supports projects, not just one-off stories. The overarching mission is to raise awareness of underreported global crises, so the most successful applications demonstrate that the journalist has thought about how to reach the widest possible audience. Deadline is rolling. 

Sierra Club/Sierra Magazine is hiring an editorial fellow. This is a 6-month fellowship geared toward early career journalists interested in environmental justice, climate science, and conservation. This is a fully remote fellowship that can be done from anywhere in the US. It runs from January - June on part time hours (28 hours/week), and pays $20/hour. Except for those two hours of weekly meetings, specific working hours are flexible. They are accepting applicants on a rolling basis until the positions are filled.

Fortune Media is accepting applications for the Fortune Newsroom Fellowship running from January through June. Fellows can work in newsrooms in New York, Chicago, or San Francisco or remotely. Deadline is rolling. 

Flashlight, a non-profit giving circle, is giving $500 - $1,000 grants to U.S.-based freelance investigative journalists. Deadline is November 19.

The Grist Fellowship Program is a paid opportunity to hone your skills at a national news outlet and deepen your understanding of environmental issues. The fellowships run March 3, 2024 to February 28, 2025 and will be in the following areas: Environmental Justice, Indigenous Affairs, Climate News, and Climate Solutions. Deadline is November 26. 

Sojourners is accepting applications for its first Sojourners Journalism Cohort. The cohort will accept three news reporters and three opinion writers who will each write approximately five faith and justice articles for sojo.net over the program. Stipend of $400/month, starting in February 2024. Deadline is November 27.

The James Beard Foundation has opened the entry portal for the 2024 James Beard Awards. This year they have split the media awards into Journalism Awards and Broadcast Media Awards. Deadline is November 30; later deadlines for entries published in December 2023.

The Journalism And Women Symposium (JAWS) Health Journalism Fellowship will support eight emerging and early career journalists who have an interest in and passion for upping their healthcare reporting skills. Fellows’ projects are supported with a $4,000 grant, a one-year membership to JAWS, and complimentary registration and travel expenses to CAMP for 2024. Deadline is December 8.

The CatchLight Global Fellowship focuses on the power of visual storytelling to change the world. Proposals must represent excellence in the visual field and innovative engagement ideas. $30,000 stipend, plus personal development opportunities, including executive coaching. Deadline is December 9.

USC Annenberg Center for Health Journalism is taking applications for the 2024 California Health Equity Fellowship. Fellows receive grants of $2,000-$10,000 to cover expenses for their projects, a week of training, and five months of mentorship. Freelancers must have an assignment. Deadline is December 10.

The Reynolds Journalism Institute is accepting applications for the 2024 Women in Journalism Workshop, scheduled for April 12-14 at the University of Missouri. Deadline is December 15.

The International Women’s Media Fund has opened the entry portal for the 2024 Kim Wall Memorial Fund grants, which award $5,000 to cover reporting on stories about “subcultures” and related costs including travel, logistics, insurance, visa fees, professional stipends, and payments for producers and translators. Women and nonbinary journalists with at least a year of professional experience are welcome to apply. Deadline is December 17. 

IRE is accepting applications for the 2024 Gary Marx Scholarship to attend the NICAR Conference March 7-10 in Baltimore. Deadline is January 8

The Chronicle of Philanthropy is accepting applications for its Philanthropy & Nonprofit Accountability Fellowship from US news organizations, with preference given to local or regional reporting teams. The fellowship is directed by the Chronicle of Philanthropy, a nonprofit news organization that covers nonprofits, foundations, and others involved in advancing the social good. The Chronicle is committed to helping local news teams access the tools and training they need during the fellowship and over the long haul to better inform and engage their audiences on topics as varied as the arts, climate, housing, and health. Deadline is January 8.

The Social Science Research Council seeks applicants for the third cohort of its Just Tech Fellowship. This fellowship supports and mobilizes diverse and cross-sector cohorts of practitioners to imagine and create more just, equitable, and representative technological futures. Deadline is January 31.

Biographers International Organization is accepting applications for the Frances “Frank” Rollin Fellowship, which awards $5,000 each to two authors working on a biographical work about an African American figure or figures whose story provides a significant contribution to our understanding of the Black experience. Deadline is February 1, 2024.
