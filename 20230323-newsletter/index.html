Community media and public health go hand in hand

Image from Wikimedia Commons
CCM’s Asian Media Initiative Director Kavitha Rajagopalan and Black Media Initiative Director Cheryl Thompson-Morton were both invited to speak on a panel for Project ReFOCUS (Racial Ethnic Framing of Community-informed and Unifying Surveillance), an initiative funded by Centers for Disease Control (CDC) and the CDC Foundation, and co-led by Dr. Monica Ponder of Howard University and Dr. Chandra Ford who leads UCLA’s Center for the Study of Racism, Social Justice and Health. The panel focused on how community media serving historically marginalized communities covered overlapping public health crises from the COVID-19 pandemic to the opioid crisis, and beyond.

The goal of the panel was to gather policy recommendations that can be shared with the CDC and other public health professionals because Project ReFocus leaders feel “emergency response funding should always include support for journalists who do critical work during public health crises,” Dr. Ponder noted. 

Both Rajagopalan and Thompson-Morton gave presentations on the importance of Asian and Black community media outlets, particularly as the COVID-19 pandemic had a disproportionate effect on both communities. 

“Community media for and by immigrants and people of color have a critical role to play in keeping immigrants and communities of color safe during public health crises,” Rajagopalan said. Mainstream media was covering many angles of the scientific research, state and federal policies, and mask mandate stories, but community media was bridging a crucial cultural gap as well as providing relevant information on local business closures, in-language information to keep people safe, and resources on health issues like comorbidities prevalent in their communities. 

Rajagopalan pointed out that community media is “a vital and often underutilized resource for public health experts who need to quickly and reliably reach communities of color during crises.”

Other speakers included Sally Lehrman, the Chief Executive of the Trust Project; film producer for ReImagine ATL, Reeyana Sehgeh; Dr. Loren Saxton-Coleman, Assistant Professor of Communications and Media Studies at Howard University; and Audrey Duncan, Director of Community Affairs at BronxNet Television.

“So much of public health comes down to how we communicate what we need and there are a lot of different ways to approach this challenge,” Thompson-Morton said, adding that the diversity of speakers was important and reflected the diversity of approaches needed to tackle a public health crisis. The event was not only about how community media was reporting about the pandemic, but “how communities can be part of creating the media…so it was really an exciting conversation,” she explained. 

Small businesses get the spotlight from our latest cohort of Fellows 

Since February, our newest fellowship cohort of community media journalists in the New York City metro region has been reporting on local, community-owned businesses’ challenges, successes, and lessons learned three years after the global pandemic began. 

As the fellows continue to investigate and report over the next two months in their communities, they will look at the changing environment and circumstances for small businesses. The survival of these businesses also impact community media outlets and the local media ecosystem in general, as many lean greatly on local advertising and the vitality of the community.

CCM's Director of Communications Jehangir Khattak said, "The impact and ripple effects of the pandemic have been discussed in mainstream media on a large scale, but New Yorkers' experience is unique and the situation for community-owned media and businesses can not only provide real insight into the long-lasting effects of the pandemic on communities which are often ignored by larger news outlets, but allow community media journalists to explore stories they may not have had the opportunity to without the Fellowship." 

The funding for this opportunity was provided by the Mayor's Office for Media and Entertainment. 

Some of the published pieces include a look at the hard road faced by New York City taxi medallion owners and drivers by Fellow Sujeet Rajan for the India Overseas Report. Fellow Ricky Rillera wrote about a catering service and a funeral home owned and operated by members of the Filipino community in Queens for the Philippine Daily Mirror. Fellow Parveen Chopra described the struggles for four South Asian beauty parlors to stay open during the pandemic in Hicksville, Long Island for The South Asian Times. Fellow Anna Bradley-Smith documented for The Brownstoner the issues faced by a Brooklyn florist owned by a Mexican immigrant during the pandemic when government assistance was not easily accessible. Caribbean American Weekly reporter and Fellow Pearl Phillip profiled an entrepreneur with an incredible story of overcoming struggle. Stay tuned to our Twitter account for more of the Fellows’ work about small businesses and the pandemic’s continued impact on their communities.
 

Making progress for Connecticut community media

Connecticut introduced HB 6347, a proposed bill which would mandate that a certain proportion of government advertising dollars be allocated to local news outlets owned and operated in the state of Connecticut.  The bill is in a similar vein of the CCM-pioneered initiative in New York City and the replicated program in Chicago, which have both passed this policy via executive order signed by the respective Mayors’ offices. New York City followed up and made the policy a permanent law after successfully implementing the program over two years as well. The Connecticut bill, while not exclusively focused on ethnic or BIPOC-led media, would benefit many print, digital and broadcast news outlets owned and operated in the state. 

Several community media supporters submitted written testimony in support of the bill. CCM shared its experiences with the initiatives in New York City and Chicago to assist Anna Brugmann, Director of Policy Development at Rebuild Local News and Bruce Putterman, CEO and Publisher of the CT Daily Mirror, who testified in person at the March 20th CT Legislature committee hearing considering the bill. 

As Brugmann relayed to CCM, no members of the Government Administration and Elections committee questioned the premise of the bill to support local media with advertising revenue, and they posed several questions about how to best make the bill a success for these outlets as well as the state. 

Rep. Kate Farrar (D-20th District) first introduced the bill and asked for a few changes to the bill’s original language during the committee hearing including that state advertising be directed towards locally owned ‘news organizations’ and not just locally owned media, per Brugmann. The distinction would help clarify where state agencies’ advertising revenue is currently being directed. Other requested changes requested were to clarify that state agencies are responsible for the distribution of advertising revenue as well as reporting requirements. 

The next step is getting a report from the committee and after that a floor vote could be scheduled soon.
