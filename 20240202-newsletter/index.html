News from Inside the Community Media Sector

We’re always proud to be part of the Newmark J-School, but especially so after the school received a $10 million gift from Craig Newmark Philanthropies and set itself on a path to offer a tuition-free education to half of the incoming August 2025 class and hopefully all students in the near future! 

Congratulations to our friends at Epicenter - NYC for securing a Mellon Foundation grant to establish a community hub in the borough of Queens! 

Have you ever been laid off in the journalism industry? The Institute for Independent Journalists wants to hear from you in this survey aiming to understand how layoffs and workforce reductions impacted the U.S. media industry and individual journalists in the past year.

The Center for Innovation and Sustainability in Local Media at UNC-Chapel Hill is conducting a survey for a research study on current and former U.S. local journalists. The goal of this study is to follow up on their previous report on burnout in local journalism and to better understand why people stay in or leave local journalism.

Here’s what El Tímpano learned training 100+ Latino immigrants on disinformation defense to prepare for this election year. 

Vermont Public is covering the 2024 election by asking readers “What do you want the candidates to be discussing as they compete for your votes?”

The New York Times examines the role and mission of billionaires and “saving” the news industry. 

A weekly newspaper in Colorado, The Ouray County Plaindealer, had hundreds of copies stolen from newsstands in their coverage area after printing a story about a teenage girl’s alleged assault during a party at the local police chief’s home. 

LAist discusses why and how it tracked Los Angeles Mayor Bass’ big promise on homelessness.

Nieman Lab reports on New Orleans-based The Garrison Project, a news outlet looking to bridge the gap between national and local criminal justice reporting through partnerships with news outlets and organizations like Investigative Reporters and Editors (IRE). 

We are saddened by the many recent layoffs around the industry, particularly at the L.A. Times which gutted its team of reporters covering Latino communities in the region. However, our Latino Media Initiative Director Jesenia De Moya Correa offers up some hope - and a list of news outlets - for those looking to keep reading, watching, and hearing news about the communities in the Los Angeles metro area. 

Readers are offering The Atlantic their views on the local news landscape where they live. 

A recent Pew Research Center study concluded that fewer than half of Black Americans say the news often covers the issues that are important to them. 

Several Kansas lawmakers have mounted a bipartisan effort to direct the state’s attorney general to release information from an investigation into a police raid last August on the Marion County Record, a weekly newspaper in central Kansas. The newspaper had been looking into the local police chief’s past at the time of the raid, the Associated Press reports. 

Poynter has a working paper on why Google and Meta ‘owe’ news publishers more than $11 billion.

Fellowships & Opportunities

Entrepreneurs and creators in journalism are invited to apply for the Entrepreneurial Journalism Creators Program (EJCP) at Newmark J-School. This unique 100-day online certificate program is now open for applications through February 2. 

Newmark J-School is taking applications for its Emerging News Leadership Program, a six-month online, tuition-free training program that prepares a diverse group of rural and local media leaders to run sustainable news organizations by focusing on advanced digital skills, the business of journalism, management, and leadership. Deadline is February 9. 

CCM is taking applications for its 2024 Elections Reporting Mentorship! Open to community media in the metro New York City area, the fully-remote spring mentorship will guide 15 journalists in enhancing and expanding their coverage of election campaigns, identifying community stakes in the process, and amplifying community voices and participation in local, state and national races. Deadline is February 12. 

Applications are also open for CCM’s Inaugural Fundraising for Community Media cohort for POC-led newsrooms! As philanthropic spending continues to grow in the journalism industry, we partnered with The Lenfest Institute for Journalism and its News Philanthropy Network to remotely train teams from Asian, Black and Latino newsrooms around the country to develop business strategies and new revenue streams around fundraising. Deadline is February 19. 

Newmark J-School has partnered with the Center for Journalism & Democracy at Howard University to launch The Du Bois Data Lab. The pilot will leverage curriculum from the Newmark J-School to teach students how to find and analyze data to tell stories that carry weight and impact. Fifteen undergraduate students from HBCUs will take part in a one-week intensive workshop in Washington, DC taught by professional journalists of color, from May 29 to June 5, 2024. The Du Bois Data Lab will be free for attendees and cover travel, housing, and meals. Admitted students will also receive a $400 stipend. Deadline is February 26.

The Maynard Institute is accepting applications for its Maynard 200 Fellowship to promote the diversity pipeline in news media and dismantle structural racism in its newsrooms. The program has trained media leaders, storytellers, editors, managers and entrepreneurs and is designed to sharpen skills, provide hands-on training as well as a one-to-one year-long mentorship, and build a community of peer support for diverse journalists. In 2024, the program returns with two weeks of in-person training rounds — specifically designed to support the success of newsroom editors and managers. Hosted by the Bob Schieffer College of Communication at TCU in Fort Worth, Texas, the program will convene in March and July of 2024. Deadline is February 12.

​​​​​Columbia University is accepting applications for the Ira A. Lipman Center For Journalism and Civil and Human Rights’ Fellowship, which provides a $10,000 grant plus editorial and reporting guidance. Each fellow produces a 5,000-word story or series regarding civil and human rights broadly construed, such as voting rights, use of force by police, employment and other discrimination, human trafficking, child labor and more. Deadline is February 15.

The Carter Institute at NYU is accepting applications for the Matthew Power Literary Reporting Award, which awards $12,500 to support the work of a promising early-career nonfiction writer on a story that uncovers truths about the human condition. Deadline is February 22.

The California Local News Fellowship is now taking applications from early-career digital, print, video, audio, photo and multimedia journalists to work in local newsrooms across the state. Fellows earn a full-time salary and benefits from the University of California for two years and receive intensive mentoring, training, and support from experienced journalism leaders. Deadline is February 25. 

The City Bureau, in Chicago, runs a Civic Reporting Fellowship that offers 16 weeks of learning and work experience for emerging journalists with little or no professional journalism experience. The spring session will begin April 8; pay is $6,000. Deadline is March 3.
