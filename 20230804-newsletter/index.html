Community media is represented at national conference
(L-R) Asian Media Initiative Director Kavitha Rajagopalan; Kishor Panthi of Khasokas; Jinxia Niu of PiYaoBa; Chang Yul Lee of Korea Times

Editing Note: Our newsletter will be taking a brief summer hiatus for the rest of August. Our regular bimonthly schedule will resume after Labor Day. 

Last month the Asian American Journalists Association held its annual conference in Washington, DC with thousands of journalists in attendance. Asian Media Initiative Director Kavitha Rajagopalan hosted a stellar panel of community media journalists including: Khasokhas’ Kishor Panthi, Jinxia Niu of Justice Patch and PiYaoBa, and Chang Yul Lee of Korea Times. 

The panelists discussed the need for and challenges faced by in-language community media in the larger media landscape, specifically for the Nepali, Chinese, and Korean communities across the U.S. 

Lee pointed out that many second generation Korean immigrants do not have the skills to read Korean and also noted how the expense of printing a physical newspaper has gone up significantly in recent years. Niu and Panthi spoke about how advertisers prefer larger and more English-language media to reach audiences. There is also the challenge of how social media platforms lack support for many languages and can mark their news outlet’s pages and posts as spam, making it difficult to reach new online audiences in their communities and hampering their ability to attract more advertisers. 

However, there was a lot of positivity in the room as well. The panelists celebrated their unique position among the many large media outlets represented at the conference. It was clear to the audience that community media members are dedicated to serving their growing AAPI communities. This is especially true for the members of those communities who do not have the language skills yet or the cultural understanding to fully grasp stories in mainstream media outlets that could have an impact on their and their communities’ day to day lives. The panelists said their mission was particularly crucial during the fear and confusion brought about by the pandemic and during local elections. 

As Rajagopalan explained: “We hope this is the beginning of an important effort to include and learn from community media in processional media spaces, and bring sorely needed resources and connections to these innovative, tireless leaders. For far too long journalists in mainstream newsrooms have viewed community media at best as unpaid sources and fixers, at worst as quaint novelties. As our immigrant communities grow and expand across the country, and continue to navigate disinformation and racism, in-language community media remains a critical resource for informing and supporting AAPI.” 

She also announced that our Asian Media Initiative will be publishing the first comprehensive directory and map of media outlets serving AAPI communities this Fall. For more information or to be included in the directory, please contact kavitha.rajagopalan@journalism.cuny.edu.

Fellowships & Opportunities

The Feet in 2 Worlds 2023 Editing Fellowship is open for applications. The fellow will be mentored and trained in editing for radio, podcast, and online media by a team of veteran editors. Journalists from immigrant backgrounds and from communities of color are strongly urged to apply. Deadline is August 4.

The Artist/Scholar in Residence program at the Library of Congress is now accepting applications. As part of LOC’s Connecting Communities Digital Initiative (CCDI) under the Of the People: Widening the Path program, the grant program supports artists or scholars in the creation of new scholarly and/or artists' works that imaginatively study, experiment with, and/or critique the Library’s digital collections, materials, and/or services. The program is open to a broad array of artists and scholars, including (but not limited to): visual artists, conceptual artists, dancers, journalists, cultural critics, independent scholars, academic scholars, and cultural heritage practitioners, including librarians. For 2023, the Library intends to award two 12-month grants of up to $90,000 each. Deadline is August 7. 

ProPublica is taking applications for its inaugural Diamonstein-Spielvogel Visual Journalism Fellowship. Over two years, this fellow will expand their investigative reporting skills by conceptualizing and producing photography across platforms, collaborating on ProPublica’s in-depth investigative projects and pitching and executing independent visual stories. Deadline is August 8.  

The Sir Harry Evans Global Fellowship provides a nine-month fellowship opportunity with Reuters (or other media outlets) and Durham University that will include undertaking an investigative project from inside a newsroom in London, New York, or Toronto. The Fellow will be mentored by top editors at Reuters while being overseen and having access to Durham University academics and research resources. Candidates should have at least two years of professional journalism experience. Deadline to apply is August 10.

The National Association of Science Writers’ application portal is open for the New Horizons Travel Fellowship, which offers up to $1,200 for journalists from smaller metropolitan print and broadcast news outlets, as well as freelance and online journalists with a demonstrated interest in science writing, to attend SciWri23 in Colorado in October. Deadline is August 10.

The Environmental Reporting Collective is accepting applications for the 2023 ERC Collaborative Reporting Grants, which support projects on tracing environmental crimes across borders with at least two journalists or news outlets in different countries. Budgets may include stipends for freelancers. Deadline is August 15.

The New England First Amendment Coalition is accepting applications for the New England First Amendment Institute, scheduled for October 29-31 in Boston. Deadline is August 18.

Poynter is running a weekly virtual training ($750) from October 3 - November 14 that will help reporters up their game and develop the essential skills they need to continue growing in a time when resources are few and the need for local journalism is great. Deadline is August 31. 

Poynter is accepting applications for its Power of Diverse Voices: Writing Workshop for Journalists of Color workshop. With the support of expert instructors and fellow journalists of color, develop the confidence and skills to share your unique perspective in persuasive writing. Deadline is September 15.

Report for America is accepting applications from newsrooms that want to partner with RfA to hire journalists. Report for America has now matched more than 600 corps members with hundreds of local newspapers, public radio stations, digital platforms, and television outlets, since its launch in 2017. This includes providing up to half of the corps member’s salary and providing newsroom tools and resources to raise funds locally to support their share. Deadline is September 18. 

The American Academy in Berlin is accepting applications for the 2024 Berlin Prize Fellowship. Applicants working in journalism or filmmaking must be based permanently in the U.S. and have a significant record of publication or production. For all projects, the Academy asks that candidates explain the relevance of a stay in Berlin to the development of their work. Deadline is September 29.
