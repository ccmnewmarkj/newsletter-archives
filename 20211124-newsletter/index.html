Listening to Indigenous Journalists on Thanksgiving

Much of the country gathers around tables this week to celebrate Thanksgiving, a holiday premised on a mythologized narrative about the relationship between Pilgrim settlers and the Wampanoag people in present-day Massachusetts.

You can read an essay on the Wampanoag side of this narrative here, and listen to a re-telling of the story of Thanksgiving from a Wampanoag citizen here. The Mashpee Wampanoag Tribe share their history, with a detailed timeline of contact with Europeans throughout the 1600s, here. 

We are reading Native writings deconstructing Thanksgiving myths, like this essay on Oyate, a Native-led organization dedicated to ensuring Native lives are portrayed with integrity; this essay from the Citizen Potawatomi Nation in present-day Oklahoma; and this breakdown of six Thanksgiving myths. We also found this interview with non-Native historian David Silverman, who has written a book on the Wampanoag, insightful.

Last week, Newmark J-School hosted a panel discussion, Decolonizing Journalism: Media By, For and About Indigenous Peoples, with Mark Trahant (Shoshone-Bannock) of Indian Country Today and Suzette Brewer (Cherokee Nation), author of “Real Indians: Portraits of Contemporary Native Americans and America’s Tribal Colleges.” The moderators were Amy Stretten (Chickahominy), a Newmark J-School alum, former fellow at the Tow-Knight Center for Entrepreneurial Journalism and creator of The Chief of Style, and Robert Pluma (Coahuiltecan), current Newmark J-School Tow-Knight Scholar and Indigenous futurist, whose work can be found here. 

Opening with a rich discussion on the evolving style preferences surrounding “Indian,” “Indigenous,” and the use of Tribal affiliation names, the conversation went on to interrogate a range of concepts – from what (and where) is “Indian Country,” to sovereignty and more. You can watch a recording of the full conversation here.

In January, the school will be hosting a workshop on covering Indigenous issues, led by investigative journalist and member of the Cherokee Nation Graham Lee Brewer as part of the January Academy.

New Infrastructure Act to Invest in Bridging Digital Divide

November 15, President Joe Biden signed the Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act (IIJA) into law, which included an estimated $65 billion investment in broadband and digital equity – the largest in the nation’s history to date. Included in this investment is the Digital Equity Act of 2021, which appropriates $2.75 billion to be distributed through two grant programs. 

Accessing and implementing these funds will require a significant amount of organizing. The National Digital Inclusion Alliance (NDIA) offers a range of resources to those interested in joining or building a digital inclusion coalition and community digital inclusion plan. On December 7, NDIA will host a webinar on Digital Inclusion Funding in the Infrastructure Bill. Register here.

Food for Thought

During this season of holidays and giving, we’d like to share some great ways to lend your support to community media serving communities that have survived oppression and violence:

Indian Country Today celebrates its 40th anniversary this month. In response to a growing disavowal of the term “Indian” by Indigenous and Native peoples across North America, many of whom are moving away even from official names assigned to their nations by the Bureau of Indian Affairs, the outlet is redesigning its logo as ICT. Read more about the outlet’s rich history here, and donate to their anniversary fundraiser here.

Kinara Park Kids hosts the Kwanzaa Awards each year, to recognize individuals and organizations that embody the 7 principles of Kwanzaa. Nominate a Black leader or Black-owned / -led organization (especially your favorite Black media outlet) by November 30 here.

Texas After Violence, a community-based archive and documentary project examining the impacts of state-sanctioned violence in Texas is hosting its annual campaign to end state violence by elevating the voices of those most impacted. Donate to the Revealing Voices Fundraising Campaign here.
