Community Media Respond to Mass Shootings

In the aftermath of a devastating school shooting in the majority-Latinx southern Texas town of Uvalde, which claimed the lives of 19 children and two teachers at Robb Elementary School on May 24, Spanish-speaking community and local media journalists supported terrified families seeking news of their missing children. We began sharing Latino, bilingual, Black, and local community media coverage on Twitter. We will continue to add to this thread and amplify community media coverage, as weekly and other outlets with longer publication windows respond with their reporting.

This tragedy comes on the heels of a series of mass shootings targeting Black shoppers in Buffalo, New York; Taiwanese churchgoers in Laguna Woods, California; and Korean hair salon workers in Dallas, Texas earlier in the month. Community media for and by Black and Asian communities quickly responded to these crises.
Cover by JP Designs Art via The Challenger News on Facebook

The May 14 racist attack that killed 10 Black shoppers at a grocery store in Buffalo, New York, tragically claimed the life of Kat Massey (72), a longtime contributor to the city’s two legacy Black media outlets, The Challenger and The Buffalo Criterion, and a lifelong activist who has often spoken out against gun violence. Read this beautiful essay in The Challenger, which is also sharing resources for grieving and impacted community members. Eva M. Doyle, a former Buffalo public schools teacher, who has written a column called “Eyes on History” in The Challenger and now The Criterion since 1979, remembers the shooting victims in her latest.

One day later, a shooting targeting Sunday worshippers at the Irvine Taiwanese Presbyterian Church killed one person and injured five more. Reporting locally in Orange County, Chinese-language World Journal published a series of reports on the attack, including reportage on the shooter’s state of mind and his attitudes toward Taiwan and Taiwanese people, along with ways to support the victims’ families. Chinese-language Sing Tao Daily also reported locally on the attack and the community grieving Dr. John Cheng (郑達志), and shared joint condemnations of the presumptive hate crime from Taiwanese community organizations.

Earlier that week, on May 11, a shooter targeted a Korean-owned hair salon in Dallas, Texas, injuring three women in an apparent incident of anti-Asian violence. Dallas is home to the second-largest Korean community in the U.S. South after Atlanta, where Korean communities were targeted by a shooting spree last year. Korean-language Dalkora covered the Dallas Korean community’s shock and fear following the attack. Korea Times Texas reported on the details of the evolving police investigation but also centered the perspective of a victim in a reported feature and shared details of a fundraiser to support victims by the Korean Society of Dallas. A community-based journalist reporting for NBC Asian America reported this story about Dallas Koreatown’s long push for official recognition.

Community media often work outside of the so-called breaking news cycle, first responding as members of a community to support, then moving on to report. Their coverage will continue for months and years after a tragedy. We will continue to follow and share Latino, Black, and Asian media coverage in the long shadow of these attacks.

One Week To Latino Media Summit!

The 2022 Latino Media Summit is just one week away! The premiere celebration of and for Latino media in the U.S., the theme for the fifth annual LMS is disruptive journalism that stands with our community. With more than 40 speakers from all over the U.S. and a packed two-day agenda revolving around the issues most critical to Latinx journalists and their news audiences, #LMS22 will impart new skills, foster transformative connections, and inspire you to better serve your communities.

In a year that has seen Latinx communities targeted by violence, strained by polarization and disinformation, and hobbled by barriers to voting and other civil rights, Latino media have valiantly innovated to inform, heal, and empower their communities. At #LMS22, you can hear about the rise of bilingual podcasting, how investigative journalists collaborated with delivery worker labor organizers, how the incarcerated are practicing journalism, and more in feature presentations and lightning talks. In-person attendees can enjoy live bilingual comedy and watch the first screening open to the general public of the award-winning documentary film, Mija, or learn political cartooning, short news documentary production, visual journalism, news product tools and more in our workshops – spots are still available for pre-registration.  

Make sure to get your tickets today! Depending on your budget and comfort level, you can find both virtual-only and in-person ticket options. We look forward to seeing you at The Clemente on New York City’s Lower East Side June 3-4.
