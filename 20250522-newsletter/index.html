Community Media Gets $72 Million Boost from NYC Policy, New Report Shows

Photo by Julius Motal

Newmark J-School has published a report highlighting the transformative impact of New York City’s legislation supporting community media outlets through advertising revenue as well as the impact of CCM’s Advertising Boost Initiative (ABI), led by Darlie Gervais. The report also recommends significant changes to bring more transparency to government spending and ensure editorial independence.

The Five-Year Impact Report shows that the New York City government has directed over $72 million to community media outlets in the past five years through Executive Order 47, signed by then-Mayor Bill de Blasio in 2019, and the subsequent 2021 legislation, Local Law 83, that codified it. The order and legislation mandated that city government agencies allocate at least 50% of their advertising budgets to community and ethnic media. 

The report also evaluates the impact of the ABI, which was established in 2020 in response to the 2019 Executive Order. ABI serves as an information bridge between city agencies, advertising agencies and community media organizations, assisting the latter to become more effective in accessing their share of city agencies’ advertising budgets. ABI also tracks compliance with Local Law 83, and publishes regular reports that are meant to provide more transparency and government accountability. 

The substantial funding from ad placements has strengthened trusted news sources in dozens of languages and ensured New Yorkers receive critical government information through accessible channels, of particular importance during the pandemic. The report also states that the legislation pushed the city’s advertising procurement process to diversify beyond a small number of traditional media outlets. 

This policy redirected almost 84% of the city's ad budget that year, or nearly $10 million to more than 200 community media outlets in 2020. In 2021, the city invested over $15 million.

"Community media provide essential information to communities that are often underserved by mainstream media," said Graciela Mochkofsky, Dean of the Newmark J-School. "They are a critical public good. And while public funding should never be the sole source of revenue for community media, it ought to be a key pillar of support."

However, despite having this support, the report identifies other challenges for community media securing this advertising revenue because of inconsistent compliance with the 50% advertising budget allocation mandate. The report also recommends an independent structure for the Mayor’s Office of Ethnic and Community Media and calls for immediate steps to increase transparency, including public disclosure of advertising decisions and improved accountability measures.
