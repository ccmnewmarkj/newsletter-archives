2022 Year-End Edition: CCM Looks Back on Resilience and Renewal in Community Media
As the year winds down, we want to thank all of our supporters and community media outlets for your participation, support, and incredible work during a challenging time for journalism. CCM is proud to have been able to host 20 programs this year for community media journalists and publishers to receive free training and network with each other. We’re thrilled to also report we had more than 2,000 people register for our events, and more than 800 were first-timers.

Maps of the U.S. and world that show the locations of people who registered for CCM events in 2022
In February of this year, we published an exciting update highlighting the news that more than 230 community media outlets in NYC received a record $15.6 million in city agency advertising in fiscal year 2021, an increase of more than $5 million from the previous year. This amount represents 82 percent of the city’s $19.1 million total print and digital budget for the year. The tireless work of Advertising Boost Initiative (ABI) Manager Darlie Gervais did not just end there. In October, with CCM’s support and using the ABI as a template, the Chicago Independent Media Alliance was able to lobby Chicago Mayor Lori Lightfoot to sign an Executive Order committing at least half of the city’s annual departmental advertising spending to community media outlets.

​​​​​The Black Media Initiative, led by Cheryl Thompson-Morton, launched its first-ever cohort for the Black Media Product Strategy program in March. Twelve Black-led newsrooms from across the U.S. were selected to be part of the six-month, tuition-free program which trained Black-owned newsrooms to build product strategies for digital transformation, audience growth, and sustainability. Participating outlets developed social media strategies, launched newsletters and magazines, and held several community events to name just a few of the exciting projects that resulted from the program.

In the Spring, we also introduced our 30 NY State Elections Reporting Fellows, who serve communities all over New York State – from Brooklyn to Westchester County and Queens to western New York State. Through the year, these fellows and their media outlets worked hard to bring important elections stories from and to their communities as part of their commitment to report at least four in-depth articles on federal, state, or local elections.

In June we were excited to host the Latino Media Summit, in-person rather than virtually. The hybrid, bilingual Spanish-English, two-day event brought together more than 40 journalists, entrepreneurs, artists, and industry leaders to talk about ideas, innovations, the rise of bilingual podcasting, polarization, voting rights, and opportunities for Latinx journalism and how best to cover specific issues. We also have plans to expand our reach to Latinx community media in 2023 with new programming and events.

In September, we launched the Asian Media Initiative, led by Director Kavitha Rajagopalan, to support and sustain Asian media. The launch event was a town hall on the evolving Asian media landscape, and how Asian media can support and connect both new arrivals and news audiences in the face of social media disinformation, political polarization, anti-Asian hate crimes, and more. Earlier in the year, Rajagopalan also launched a peer-to-peer group for women journalists and publishers in Asian media to share resources and support, the Asian Women’s Media Network. Email her if you or someone you know is interested in joining.

​​​Director of Communications Jehangir Khattak and ABI Manager Darlie Gervais also launched the Growth Mentorship Program: Building a Plan for Audience and Revenue Growth to support New York City metro area publishers as they continue their work of sustaining a community media newsroom in an ever-expanding digital landscape. Drawing from survey results and daily conversations with publishers, the pilot project of ABI follows two tracks: digital analytics and media kit design and development. The initial media outlets serve Latino, South Asian, Arab, Russian, African, Irish, Black / Caribbean and local communities in multiple languages.

But, we’re far from finished! We are working on a number of reports to be published in 2023, including media content analysis of community media outlets in New York State and and a preliminary report on FOIL requests to New York State agencies regarding their media advertising spending by Newmark J-School Associate Professor Barbara Gray.​​​​​

Elevating Elections Coverage by Community Media

 

Last month, nearly 50 journalists, students, and community organizers joined us for Asian Media, Disinformation, and the AAPI Vote, an intimate conversation between in-language reporters in Chinese and Korean media, fact checkers serving Chinese Americans and Indian Americans, and disinformation defense experts. Watch a recording of the event.

As AMI Director Kavitha Rajagopalan said on the importance of events like this one to highlight and boost in-language community media: “In the last few election cycles, both major political parties have attempted to appeal to Asian American and immigrant AAPI voters, but with limited nuance or awareness about in-community discourse, the influence of politics in countries of origin, and about political diversity in AAPI communities. Furthermore, very few campaigns and even local governments offer information in Asian languages. This limits their engagement with AAPI voters.”

This fascinating conversation was just part of the coverage of the 2022 midterms elections done by community media. Our 30 NY State Elections Reporting Fellows also wrote several stories on the elections and issues surrounding voting logistics, voting rights, and local and state elections.

Community media in Georgia has done extended elections reporting as well because of the state’s runoff election between Rev. Raphael Warnock's win over Herschel Walker to keep his seat in the U.S. Senate. Larger and out-of-state media outlets often do not capture the impact or effect of such elections on communities, particularly communities of color, the way these outlets can. We have started a thread on Twitter to track all of this runoff election coverage by Georgia’s community media outlets.

News from Inside the Community Media Sector



Big congratulations to the Ark Republic on receiving two new funding awards! The Valentine Foundation awarded Ark Republic $10,000 for its forthcoming podcast series, Being Beloved. The podcast is a 12-segment podcast that plans to narrate women’s experiences in different stages of reentry into society after incarceration. The Black woman-owned publication was also named as one of the 450 newsroom recipients of Google’s News Equity Fund, being awarded $11,000 to create more podcasts and stories centering justice and equity.

Three cheers for the November 1 launch of a new magazine, The Amp, focused on arts and culture and run by the Asian American Arts Alliance.

The National Federation of Community Broadcasters writes about how the Federal Communications Commission’s changes to the Emergency Alert System, as well as a significant Notice of Proposed Rulemaking, could affect community radio stations across the country.

Lydia Polgreen of The New York Times wrote about why she, as an international news-focused journalist, is supporting community news outlets like THE CITY (in NYC) and Sahan Journal (in Minnesota) this holiday giving season.

The Conversation (Australia) published a piece on how ethnic community media can play a key role in a crisis, but it needs more support.

The New York City Mayor's Office of Media and Entertainment (MOME) conducted its inaugural economic analysis of NYC's publishing industry and outlined recommendations on city-led programs to better address inequities and support the industry’s growth. MOME provides suppport to CCM so that we can provide essential digital skills trainings and workshops for New York City’s vibrant community media sector.

Films from Iran For Iran is offering screenings of over 20 films from 15 female artists, with a focus on non-fiction and experimental work as a fundraiser to support Iranian protesters.

Fellowships & Opportunities

Investigative Reporters and Editors is offering its Total Newsroom Training, designed to increase newsrooms’ abilities to provide watchdog and enterprise coverage of communities. Selected newsrooms can choose for their training to be conducted virtually or in person and the dates on which the training will take place. Deadline is December 9.

Environmental news outlet Grist has opened up applications for several paid fellowships on a variety of desks including climate solutions, news and politics, indigenous affairs, data, and environmental justice. The program runs for 12 months beginning in March 2023. Deadline is December 19.

NPR is accepting applications for its Reflect America Fellowship. This is a year-long, paid position aimed at bringing a greater diversity of voices to NPR's news coverage. The fellow will join the new Climate Desk and report on vulnerable communities, particularly Indigenous communities. Deadline is January 8, 2023.

The Southeast Asian Diasporas (SEAD) Project is accepting applications for its Sea Change Lab. The youth leadership cohort will work with emerging artists and activists paired with Southeast Asian professionals with matching interests. Deadline is January 11.

The Knight Science Journalism program at MIT offers full-year fellowships, and week-long workshops to journalists to increase their understanding of science, technology, engineering, medicine, and the environment. Applicants must have at least three full years of experience covering science, technology, the environment, or medicine; or at least five years of other journalism experience and a desire to protect those areas in the future. Deadline is January 15.

The World Journalist Fellowship provides international journalists with funding to study at one of the masters programs at NYU’s Arthur L. Carter Journalism Institute. The fellowship covers tuition and fees for two of the three semesters of the journalism graduate program, as well as a stipend of $13,000 for each of the two semesters and is open to international journalists with at least two years experience at a journalism publication and fluency in English and at least one other language. Deadline is January 15.

The O’Brien Fellowship in Public Service Journalism offers a 9-month fellowship for journalists with at least 5 years of professional experience to work on a project at Marquette University in Milwaukee, Wisconsin. Fellows produce a rigorous, multimedia public service journalism project with the potential to have a major impact, lead to significant reform, and investigate and explain how individuals and groups can identify creative solutions to social problems. A stipend is provided for the fellow as well as living, travel, and relocation allowances. Deadline is January 20.

Investigative Reporters and Editors is also offering Educator of Color Scholarships to attend a Data Bootcamp for Educators taking place in July 2023, partially at the University of Missouri. educators of color teaching, or interested in teaching, data journalism within one’s curriculum. No data journalism experience or data teaching experience necessary. Rolling deadline is April 3.
