Community Media Reacts to the U.S. Supreme Court Decisions 

On June 29, the U.S. Supreme Court ruled that the affirmative action admission policies of universities - specifically Harvard and the University of North Carolina - are unconstitutional because the policies took into account prospective students’ race. 

National media outlets covered the decision quickly and in-depth, calling on national education experts, lawyers, and groups like the NAACP, ACLU, and Ivy League professors and alumni. The social media debate over the monumental decision to roll back decades of work to increase diversity in higher education was swift, emotional, and raised a series of questions about the fate of marginalized students of color. While community media journalists were not silent on the issue online, outlets took a slightly different approach to covering the historic ruling. 

Community media outlets do often cover and even break large news events, but Asian, Black, and Latino media outlets covered the affirmative action ruling with a longer lens, informing and platforming stories for and about the communities they serve, what it means for students from those communities, and gave key, nuanced insight into how the country is reacting to the Supreme Court’s decision. 

Directors of our Asian Media Initiative, Kavitha Rajagoapalan; Black Media Initiative, Cheryl Thompson-Morton; and Latino Media Initiative, Jesenia De Moya Correa, have compiled a list of stories published by community media outlets about the decision on affirmative action and another landmark Supreme Court ruling. 

It was a busy day for the nine justices, as another ruling concerning the LGBTQ+ community was also issued saying that a woman who wanted to potentially begin designing wedding websites could legally refuse to work with same-sex couples. LGBTQ+ media provided an important perspective on the ruling not only for their core audience but for all readers worried about the decision to exclude same-sex couples from being treated equally and fairly.

Our list consists of stories from a variety of communities across the U.S., and represents a cross-section of views on the political spectrum to show the depth and breadth of community media coverage. 

If your outlet has stories not on this list, we are happy to share them with our community on Twitter and LinkedIn as well. Please email our Newsletter Editor with any links.

News from Inside the Community Media Sector

Congratulations to the four teams participating in the American Journalism Project’s local news incubator! The chosen teams are focusing on building local news operations to: serve Miami’s diverse population, communities of color in Boston, Phoenix’s LGBTQ+ community, and address the Oregon news landscape’s gaps while combating misinformation.

Three cheers to the four Emerging Media Leaders chosen by the International Center for Journalists’ summer fellowship! The fellows - Giulia McDonnell Nieto del Rio from Documented; Héctor Alejandro Arzate of WAMU 99.5/DCist.com; Liliana Castano of Televisa Univision; and Soudi Jiménez of L.A Times - are journalists from U.S. newsrooms that hosted fellows from Latin America and the Caribbean in the fall of 2022. The reciprocal exchange trip will be a two-week study tour of newsrooms, media institutions, civil society organizations, and cultural sites 

From the American Press Institute, a piece on how multitasking is actually detrimental to your mental capacity and ways to fix that. The piece is part of API’s Mental Health Reset series, coming out every week of July, which will discuss leadership challenges that will “directly and positively impact your mental health, and by proxy, the health and well-being of your team.”

The European Journalism Centre has identified five challenges to implementing solutions journalism into media organizations. 

Nieman Lab has written about the rise of the “passive” news consumer - part of its analysis of the Reuters Institute Digital News Report.

Newmark J-School’s Wesley Lowery talks journalism education, objectivity, and learning by doing with Nieman Lab. 

Poynter reports on how two newsrooms - The Nevada Independent and CT Mirror - are improving news access for Spanish-language readers. 

“Alaska Weather,” the only daily program produced by the National Weather Service, is going off the air after 47 years. The program was designed to serve those living in rural and largely roadless Alaska, with separate forecasts for mariners, aviators and residents to help decide whether they can safely hunt, fish or fly in a state known for extreme weather. 

In Maine, local journalism is being preserved as the nonprofit National Trust for Local News has agreed to purchase five of the state’s 6 daily newspapers and 17 weekly publications, the Press Herald reports. 

Queeralize is a new resource for LGBTQ+ reporters and news outlets. From the website: “Local news can play a major role in sharing the stories of LGBTQ+ communities. It is also important that LGBTQ+ journalists have the opportunities and resources to tell our own stories. Even within LGBTQ+ news media, those stories should represent the diversity and intersections that exist: age, race, ethnicity, ability, sexual and gender identity….[the site] provides questions that reporters might consider when approaching a story — identifying key topics to (queer)alize, or make relevant for LGBTQ+ audiences.”

ABC10 in Sacramento established a Race and Culture team in 2021 to help build trust with marginalized communities, Poynter reports on how the station in the California capital did it. 

Poynter also takes a look at how journalists cover lies is the most important issue in news media.

National Geographic has laid off its last remaining staff writers and will continue operations with freelance writers and editors piecing together reports. 

Better News reports on how WGCU in Florida was able to grow its audience by expanding its news and hurricane coverage. 

The International News Media Association has insight as to how quizzes can help readers remember fact-checked content. 

CBS reports on the new strategies South Carolina newspapers are using in order to survive.

Fellowships & Opportunities

The Society of Professional Journalists Freelance Fellowship will support at least three SPJ members who are independent journalists attending the SPJ23 conference taking place September 28-30 in Las Vegas. Deadline is July 14.

The Solutions Journalism Network invites you to apply for its Journalists of Color Fellowship. This 9-month fellowship is designed to identify and support a cohort of diverse, next-generation leaders through a program of solutions journalism, leadership training, mentorship and community of practice. Ten mid-career U.S. based journalists who are interested in editorial management will be chosen. Deadline is July 17.

USC Annenberg’s Center for Health Journalism is accepting applications for its Data Fellowship. The program offers journalists an opportunity to transform their reporting by training them to “interview the data” as if it were a human source. Equipped with the tools to find original sources of information and perform data analysis, fellows graduate from this hands-on training program prepared to produce a major investigative or explanatory health reporting project in the months that follow. Deadline is July 17.

The Columbia University Aging Center is accepting applications for the Age Boom Academy 2023, to be held in October. The 2023 theme is Raising the Roof: America’s Housing Crisis and Our Longer Lives. Deadline is July 21.

The Institute for Journalism & Natural Resources is taking applications for its Klamath River Institute. The program is an expenses-paid, six-day Institute for up to 15 journalists that will explore the Klamath River Basin from its headwaters in southern Oregon to its mouth in northern California, and investigate how the removal of four dams will impact Indigenous rights and sovereignty, hydrology, food systems, agriculture practices, river ecosystems, fisheries, wildlife migrations, the energy system, and more. Deadline is July 28. 

The National Center on Disability and Journalism (NCDJ) is now accepting entries for the Katherine Schneider Journalism Award for Excellence in Reporting on Disability and the Gary Corcoran Student Prize for Excellence in Reporting on Disability. Entries are judged by professional journalists and experts on disability issues. Deadline is August 1. 

The Feet in 2 Worlds 2023 Editing Fellowship is open for applications. The fellow will be mentored and trained in editing for radio, podcast, and online media by a team of veteran editors. Journalists from immigrant backgrounds and from communities of color are strongly urged to apply. Deadline is August 4.

The Artist/Scholar in Residence program at the Library of Congress is now accepting applications. The program, part of LOC’s Connecting Communities Digital Initiative (CCDI), is part of the Library’s Of the People: Widening the Path program with support from the Mellon Foundation, supports artists or scholars in the creation of new scholarly and/or artists' works that imaginatively study, experiment with, and/or critique the Library’s digital collections, materials, and/or services. The program is open to a broad array of artists and scholars, including (but not limited to): visual artists, conceptual artists, dancers, journalists, cultural critics, independent scholars, academic scholars, and cultural heritage practitioners, including librarians. For 2023, the Library intends to award two 12-month grants of up to $90,000 each. Deadline is August 7. 

ProPublica is taking applications for its inaugural Diamonstein-Spielvogel Visual Journalism Fellowship. Over two years, this fellow will expand their investigative reporting skills by conceptualizing and producing photography across platforms, collaborating on ProPublica’s in-depth investigative projects and pitching and executing independent visual stories. Deadline is August 8.  

The Sir Harry Evans Global Fellowship provides a nine-month fellowship opportunity with Reuters (or other media outlets) and Durham University that will include undertaking an investigative project from inside a newsroom in London, New York, or Toronto. The Fellow will be mentored by top editors at Reuters while being overseen and having access to Durham University academics and research resources. Candidates should have at least two years of professional journalism experience. Deadline to apply is August 10.

Poynter is running a weekly virtual training from October 3 - November 14 that will help reporters up their game and develop the essential skills they need to continue growing in a time when resources are few and the need for local journalism is great. Deadline is August 31. 

Poynter is accepting applications for its Power of Diverse Voices: Writing Workshop for Journalists of Color workshop. With the support of expert instructors and fellow journalists of color, develop the confidence and skills to share your unique perspective in persuasive writing. Deadline is September 15.

Report for America is accepting applications from newsrooms that want to partner with RfA to hire journalists. Report for America has now matched more than 600 corps members with hundreds of local newspapers, public radio stations, digital platforms, and television outlets, since its launch in 2017. This includes providing up to half of the corps member’s salary and providing newsroom tools and resources to raise funds locally to support their share. Deadline is September 19.
