All politics (news) is local

We are having a busy Spring here at CCM! Not only are we running our inaugural fundraising cohort, but we are also underway with helping community media better cover this year’s elections and keeping all of you updated on what’s happening in Albany with state legislative proposals meant to help make community media outlets more sustainable. 

Last month, we were thrilled to launch the cohort of our Elections Reporting Mentorship. Sixteen experienced community media journalists from the New York metro region were chosen from dozens of applicants to participate in the three-month virtual program that includes mentorship, training seminars, and producing three stories related to the 2024 elections for their respective community-serving outlets.

These fellows represent Caribbean, Latino, Arab, Irish, Indian, Chinese, Black, and other communities in the metro area and their stories will be published in English, Spanish, Nepali, Chinese, and Arabic. Much of mainstream media covers the presidential race or well-known U.S. Senate races and often, especially in broadcast media, the ‘horserace’ is what is covered extensively because of their general audience.

Community media journalists, on the other hand, provide a crucial, hyperlocal perspective on not just national elections but state and local elections as well as ballot initiatives — the parts of American politics that impact their communities’ day-to-day lives in tangible ways but are often overlooked and underappreciated by larger media outlets and their audiences.

The stories the cohort has produced so far come from northern Manhattan to Harlem, Jackson Heights to eastern Queens, and all over Brooklyn. Here’s a selection of their reporting:

Sherry Mazzocchi wrote for the Manhattan Times about how artists in northern Manhattan are confronting issues like the migrant crisis and racism we are encountering in this year’s elections across the presidential, state, and local ballots.

For City Limits, Mary Cunningham reported on New York state’s version of the Equal Rights Amendment and its provision regarding reproductive rights being included on the ballot in November. 

Rommel H. Ojeda did a bit of service journalism for Documented, providing useful information for naturalized citizens voting for the first time in NYC. 

Alec Meeker of Bushwick Daily explored how redistricting is affecting Bushwick and how that intersects with a changing demographic in the district. Check out his custom maps! 

Reporte Hispano’s Gery Vereau looked at the elections through the lens of chifa, a Peruvian-Chinese dish made with meat and rice, and spoke to voters at various chifa restaurants in the metro area while weaving in the perspective of how immigrant communities often bring their experiences with politics in their home countries with them in the U.S. 

For Harlem Community News and Right On!, Cynthia Horner wrote about the power of Black women as voters and candidates at all levels of politics. 

Sujeet Rajan tackled the growing beast of fake news and its intersection with the elections, the lightning-fast emergence of AI, and social media for India Overseas Report. 

Part of their mentorship also involves trainings on elections coverage not just for reporters, but also their outlets as a whole. We want to thank Katie Honan, a reporter with THE CITY, who presented an insightful seminar on how to report on local elections in particular to our cohort. And, earlier this week the journalists were able to learn about how to secure political advertisements for their outlets and how to navigate that on the editorial side of operations in terms of endorsements and maintaining journalist integrity and fairness for their readers. Soon the cohort will also be learning about the ins and outs of money in politics, campaign finance laws in our city and state, and how to frame their reporting around that data. 

This program is made possible with the generous support of the NYC Mayor’s Office of Media and Entertainment.

We are also sending general election-related issues throughout this year and you can reference our first few installments on a few basic reporting tips and misinformation.

New NY State Legislation may make newsroom staffing easier

Our Advertising Boost Initiative, led by Darlie Gervais, has great news to share about the New York State Fiscal Year 2024-25 Budget. Not only did it recently pass, but the budget also included an investment of $30 million in tax credits each year, for the next three years, to support local journalists statewide.

Supported by the Empire State Local News Coalition, the Local Journalism Sustainability Act will allow publishers to utilize tax credits to cover half of the salaries of journalists - up to $50,000 each year. The credit can also be applied to current staffers but publishers looking to hire new staff are in for a bonus. An additional $5,000 in tax credits has been earmarked to help bring on new staff to expand their newsrooms. The bill is now part of the budget and will proceed to a regulatory process to elaborate on the final details.

For more information please contact darlie.gervais@journalism.cuny.edu.
