News 

National Trust for Local News, which operates local news outlets in Maine, Georgia, and Colorado, appoints Tom Wiley as its new CEO. Wiley is president and publisher of The Buffalo News. He said of his new position: “Journalism that audiences value is our mission and our business model. I am excited to help our newsrooms produce even more great local journalism, and help our sellers and marketers build a strong business around it.” 

Outlier Media partnered with a Detroit-area property data analyst to help residents claim millions of dollars they were owed after Wayne County sold the residents’ former homes for a profit in tax foreclosure auctions, Nieman Lab reported.

A federal judge has allowed Emmerich Newspapers Inc., a Mississippi company that oversees dozens of publications across Mississippi, Louisiana and Arkansas, to continue its lawsuit against the Mississippi River Commission, per Courthouse News Service. The 2024 suit alleged that the Commission failed to keep adequate public records about its decisions and did not hold public meetings to make decisions as required under the Freedom of Information Act. The Commission was set up by Congress in 1879 to work alongside the Army Corps of Engineers in an attempt to prevent and control flooding. 

Local Media Association explained how The Nevada Independent used event programming across the state with community leaders to generate revenue and increase audience engagement. Listening to what a particular community wants in an event and presenting chances to brand the events have been crucial to this success, according to the news outlet’s executives. 

Bloom/Tampabay.com charted the rise of Cliff Carithers’ Tampa Latest news site, which began as a social media experiment and now boasts more than 200,000 followers for its community storytelling and event listings. 

Only a third of Americans feel the media is actually free to report the news without reprisal, but a majority feel press freedom is important according to a new Pew Research Center survey, Poynter reported. 

A new study by Pace University showed that nonprofit news is still mostly dependent on philanthropy for the bulk of its funding, per Nieman Lab. Associate Professor Katherine Fink interviewed 21 nonprofit news leaders from the Institute for Nonprofit News’ membership directory for the study.

Fellowships & Opportunities

Apply to be a Visiting Journalist at the Russell Sage Foundation. Journalists have the opportunity to do a reporting project for up to four months alongside scholars at the foundation about any topic falling under RSF’s mission to "improve the social and living conditions in the United States." The deadline to apply is May 6. 

The American Press Institute invites applications for slots in its next API Local News Summit this June in Denver. The summit will explore how local media can enable intergenerational problem-solving and make their content relevant across generations to build and maintain their audiences. The deadline to request an invitation is May 7. 

Apply to be the Joan Cirillo Emerging Journalist Fellow for Oregon Public Broadcasting. The one year fellowship will consist of rotations between departments and programs as well as career counseling and a salary. Assignments may include reporting and editing stories for OPB news, digital production, or producing for a daily talk show, news magazine, or an OPB TV program, such as Oregon Field Guide or Oregon Art Beat. The deadline to apply is May 9.

Events

April 14 - May 11 (Online): The Knight Center hosts a free, four-week training session, Prompt Engineering 101 for Journalists, with Joe Amditis, an expert in AI and journalism at Montclair State University. 

May 2-3 (NYC): The Society of Professional Journalists holds its Region 1 Conference at Newmark J-School. The conference theme is Reporting in Turbulent Times and includes several panels and a keynote address from CNN correspondents Polo Sandoval and Gloria Pazmino. 

May 8 (Online): Online News Association (ONA) hosts a case study webinar with New Hampshire Public Radio to discuss the organization’s strategic marketing, audience development, and revenue generation that have made it sustainable, with The Podglomerate.

May 15-16 (Denver): The Center for Cooperative Media at Montclair State University is hosting the 2025 Collaborative Journalism Summit. The Summit is still open to session pitches and will feature two days of lightning talks, workshops, and participatory discussions about a wide array of topics and projects.

May 19-20 (Detroit): Online News Association (ONA) and Newsroom Robots host the AI Leadership Summit to gather leaders and executives to discuss how AI has impacted the news industry and newsroom leadership and culture in outlets of all sizes. 

May 29-June 1 (Los Angeles): The Association of Health Care Journalists hosts Health Journalism 2025, a four-day conference of field trips, sessions, and workshops. Journalists will be joined by health care professionals, policymakers, and other experts to tackle the most pressing issues facing health care, public health, and news coverage. 

July 30-August 3 (Seattle): The Asian American Journalists Association hosts its annual conference. The programming includes panels, career development sessions, workshops, and networking sessions with regional chapters.

August 18-21 (Chicago): Local Media Association hosts LMA Fest. The conference includes two tracks: C-level executives and digital sales managers. 

September 3-5 (St. Louis): Local Independent Online News (LION) Publishers hosts the Independent News Sustainability Summit, a national conference that will gather independent news publishers, industry experts, and supporters of independent news to share expertise, lessons learned, and actionable advice on running a news business for solopreneurs and others.
