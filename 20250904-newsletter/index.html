Meet our new Executive Director Jean Friedman-Rudovsky

Welcome back to another fall semester here at Newmark J-School and a new chapter at CCM under our new Executive Director Jean Friedman-Rudovsky! Bringing a deep commitment to community media, equity and inclusion, and Philly sports teams, the staff are excited to work with her on how to serve journalists and their communities even better going forward. 

CCM: You’ve had this amazing global career as a reporter, editor, co-founder, and now Executive Director. Tell us a little more about what brought you from being a freelancer in South America and Asia to founding projects like Resolve Philly in Philadelphia.  

JFR:  The very simple answer is: family is what brought me back to Philadelphia. I had been living abroad for almost 10 years, and I was recently married and pregnant, so we decided that we wanted to be closer to my family here and moved back to the States in March 2015. My husband's from Germany, so for him, this was the first time living in the States! So that's the simple answer about what brought me back here from the career that I had led elsewhere. 

The deeper answer is that I really had to rethink what I was doing in journalism and how I was spending my time.The type of reporting I was doing at that point was mainly long form reporting. I would go on immersive reporting trips for one or two weeks at a time but I knew that wasn't going to work for me having a little baby and wanting to be around and spend time with the family that we were building. So, I really needed to rethink what my journalism work would look like in the future. And from there, I had been connected with people at Solutions Journalism Network, and was really passionate about reporting about responses to social problems, rather than just reporting about the problems themselves. 

Through that, I ended up building the Reentry Project, which was a solutions oriented collaborative initiative amongst 13 newsrooms here in Philadelphia. That was my first kind of experience with, and foothold in, local news and thinking really differently about news and information than I had as an international journalist. At that point, I was more focused on reporting about people for an audience that doesn't know them very well, and who I thought should understand their lives. I shifted to thinking about reporting for people, what are their information needs, and what information do they need in their lives to survive and thrive? 

CCM: It sounds like there is a lot in common between solutions-focused journalism and what community media publishers are doing.

JFR: There really is because I think at the end of the day, we are all yearning to understand the world around us in a way that doesn't make us feel like everything is falling apart all of the time. Certainly, there are really grave problems in the world, and also every day there are people working around us to solve them and to do things for and with one another. So yes, solutions journalism is maybe a relatively new kind of framing or phrase in the journalism world, but there are people who have been doing this work for decades in community media. 

We need to understand things like when the school board is meeting because our kids are in school, and we need to know when those meetings are and what they're going to talk about, and get some good reporting on what happens to put all these larger issues like institutional racism and public health crises in context.

CCM: What made you want to come work for the Center for Community Media (besides working with some lovely people who deeply care about journalism serving communities)? 

JFR: What led me want to wind down my work at Resolve Philly was related to ‘founder syndrome,’ was a real thing in the nonprofit world and in the journalism space. I felt like it was really important that Resolve have new leadership that leads it into its next stage of life. 

I spent some time exploring many opportunities in and outside of journalism, but what really attracted me about this role at CCM was a few things. One, I was excited to stay in the community journalism space and to be able to use some of the knowledge, experience, and expertise that I had built up by leading Resolve in the national sphere. We always used to say about Resolve that it is ‘locally rooted and nationally relevant.’ The appeal is being able to really take that to the next level at CCM, where, obviously, there's a lot of great work done in New York City, and that is part of our mandate.

I was also excited about the opportunity to be at a center whose sole mission is to support those doing the important work of information for communities, and not just about communities - these groups and areas that have long been harmed by mainstream media narratives in this country. I was excited about the opportunity to work with this great team at the Newmark J-School, which is a pretty incredible place for its work, for its student body, faculty, and values, which CCM’s work really embodies. 

CCM: What do you see as the future of community journalism? And what would you say to people who want to serve their communities by starting or reviving a news outlet?  

JFR: The first thing I would say to folks who are thinking about starting or reviving a community news outlet is really understanding your audience, their information needs, what else is out there, and who else might be serving them. And, I don't just mean media outlets. There are so many information stewards in our daily lives: whether it's the newsletter from a school or local business, a community organization, or, in Philadelphia, we have something called community development corporations (CDCs). There are so many ways that people access vital news and information for themselves and their families and neighbors, and so, I would say, find out a little bit where people are already gathering their news from. What are the gaps? And how can you fill those gaps? How can you work with those who are already news and information purveyors or stewards? 

You know, for those of us who consider ourselves journalists, like with a capital J, how do you add on that journalism layer of real reporting and verifying information that is so critical in complement to other sources of news and information for whatever community or group you're trying to report for and serve. 

But if you're wanting to revive a news outlet that is no longer publishing, or has paused publishing, I think a key question is: what will I do differently? What are some keys to sustainability that maybe those who led it before didn't have access to or weren't able to try or work for whatever reason? 

CCM: Any words for the publishers already doing this work about journalism and CCM? 

And for those who are already doing this important work, I would say one, thank you! Number two, we at CCM are here to support you. We've done that in a number of ways over our history. 

We are entering a new life cycle of this organization, and we'll be working really hard to figure out how to meet this current moment and the future moments, thinking really critically about the work that we do now and what's important to carry forward and what new opportunities are out there for us. We also plan to be in conversation with many of you so that you can help inform our new direction and making sure that we're as meaningful and valuable to you as we should be. 

To contact Jean, please email j.friedman-rudovsky@journalism.cuny.edu
