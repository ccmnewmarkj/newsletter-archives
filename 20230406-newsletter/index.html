Black Media Futures 'dreams and schemes' 

A collection of future headlines created during the Black Media Futures conference

Black Media Futures was a half-day virtual conference which took place on March 15, 2023, hosted by CCM’s Black Media Initiative Director Cheryl Thompson-Morton and included our Executive Director Mikhael Simmonds as well. 

There were four sessions starting with ‘Black Media Past,’ which examined a historical view of the importance of news outlets serving the Black community. Speakers included author Victor Luckerson, who is using several pieces of archival research from Black media for his forthcoming book about Tulsa, Oklahoma’s Black Wall Street and the 1921 massacre which destroyed the Greenwood District. The session also included Joe Torres, Reparative Policy and Program Senior Advisor at Free Press; Brandon Nightingale, Manager of the Black Press Archives Digitization Project; and Sofiya Ballin, creator of the Black History Untold project.

Thompson-Morton said any conversation about the future of Black media needed to also include a look at the past, adding that the session prompted participants to examine “what were some of the key catalytic moments of Black Media, what are the lessons we can learn from the historical context, and what spirit we need to take with us going forward.” 

Josie Gonsalves of Public Square Amplified, Dr. Frances ‘Toni’ Draper of AFRO-American Newspapers, Retha Hill of Arizona State University’s Cronkite School of Journalism, and Venneikia Williams, Media 2070 Campaign Manager at Free Press were among those who discussed how technology, society, and culture will continue to shape Black media in the future. Thompson-Morton said this session proved critical because it was a “broader conversation about what we need: support with talent development, thinking about the business model and sustainability, while also thinking about how we can be radical in our approach.”

It was followed by a session hosted by Reparative Journalism Program Manager at Free Press, Diamond Hardiman, which allowed participants to “dream and scheme,” as Thompson-Morton described it. The 'Black Future Headlines: 'I Saw Things I Imagined'' session gave participants a chance to use the design tool Canva to create headlines and news stories they want to see in Black Media in the future. 

Some of the headlines discussed artificial intelligence and ridding algorithms of implicit bias, dramatically lower Black maternal mortality, resolution of housing issues in historically disadvantaged communities, and philanthropy of a new Black billionaire class. 

The conference concluded with a session on how to create Augmented Reality on an iPhone and the use of AI to help tell more immersive stories - a true look into the future of Black community media.

Fellowships & Opportunities

The 2023 News Leaders Association Awards are accepting submissions. NLA is requiring that all digital and print outlets that submit for any NLA award agree to participate in the next News Leaders Association Diversity Survey or conduct and publish their own newsroom’s survey for NLA to share. Deadline is April 14. 

The Institute for Journalism & Natural Resources is accepting applications for Field Reporting Grants. IJNR invites proposals for grants to help defray the costs of reporting projects that focus on natural resources, the environment, energy, development, agriculture, environmental justice, and public health. Deadline is April 14. 

Narratively is taking applications for its 2023 Profile Prize. The storytelling platform is seeking surprising and awe-inspiring stories about one-of-a-kind people or groups. The winning stories will receive cash prizes, prominent publication on Narratively, and a chance to connect with judges like Lisa Lucas and Rebecca Traister. Deadline is April 14. 

The Solutions Journalism Network is accepting applications for its Solutions Journalism Student Media Challenge. A cohort of student newsrooms at colleges and universities for the 2023-24 academic year will do solutions reporting on the urgent topic of student mental health. The Challenge includes funding and training for the student newsrooms in solutions reporting. Deadline is April 17. 

High Country News is accepting applications for the Virginia Spencer Davis Fellowship, which is designed for early-career journalists interested in reporting on rural communities in the West, and how those communities are changing. It will provide on-the-ground reporting opportunities and a chance to dive deep into important stories, as well as professional mentorship and career development. Deadline is April 21. 

Grist is accepting submissions for its Imagine 2200: Write the future, climate fiction short story contest. This is an invitation to writers from all over the globe to imagine a future in which solutions to the climate crisis flourish and help bring about radical improvements to our world. Deadline is June 13.

The Solutions Journalism Network will also host the nation’s first HBCU Educator / Black Press Academy & LEDE Fellowship, focused on the power of solutions journalism to increase trust, engagement and equity in journalism. This fellowship will bring together members of the Black press, which has a deep legacy of response-focused reporting, and HBCU educators keen to sharpen their skills in solutions journalism. Deadline is April 24
