Year in Review

This past year has been an exciting one for CCM filled with new research projects, an expansion of our team, and collaborations with other groups working to amplify and help sustain community and local media all over the country. 

We started the year off strong with announcing our Small Business Reporting Fellowship, led by CCM’s Director of Communications Jehangir Khattak with support from the Mayor’s Office of Media and Entertainment here in New York City. 


An interactive map of the Fellows' stories designed by CCM's Jennifer Cheng

The past three years took a major financial and emotional toll on the many small businesses that are the economic hub of ethnic, linguistic, and geographic communities of the NYC metro area. Whether the COVID-19 pandemic brought on dire circumstances or exacerbated existing ones, there are a plethora of undocumented stories of resilience, lessons learned, and successful recovery. The survival of these small businesses impact not just neighborhoods and people but the community media ecosystem as well with the vitality of local advertisers and the community as a whole. 

To help tell these stories, we selected 16 journalists from across the NYC region. The fellows serve Spanish and Russian speakers; and South Asian, African, Filipino and Caribbean communities. They covered parts of Long Island and northern New Jersey, and NYC neighborhoods including Bushwick and Ridgewood, Jackson Heights and Parkchester. You can read the breadth of their reporting through their first, second and third set of stories. 

Khattak has also led several trainings for community media journalists throughout the year including sessions with outside experts on the use of social media, harnessing advertising revenue, digital analytics, pricing digital news products, what the emergence of artificial intelligence could mean for community media, and the use of data in reporting about communities.

Also starting the year off strong was our Black Media Initiative, led by Director Cheryl Thompson-Morton. In January, the Initiative named its first Bridge Project: Audience Revenue Cohort. For six months, the six selected Black publishers received technology and design support to launch subscription, membership, and donation campaigns. 

We were fortunate that just a month into 2023, we could welcome our Executive Director - and Newmark J-School alum - Mikhael Simmonds to our team! Before joining CCM, Simmonds served as the director of new relationships at the Solutions Journalism Network, where he co-led operations, strategy and execution around how SJN trained journalists developed teaching products, and coached news outlets. Read our Q+A with Simmonds here.

Our Asian Media Initiative has also had a landmark first, full year. Director Kavitha Rajagopalan has continued to support journalists through the Asian Women’s Media Network and also began an important series of discussions on possible defenses against misinformation and disinformation in Asian American communities. The Asian American Digital Politics Collective (AADPC) was convened by Dr. Jonathan Ong, Associate Professor of Global Digital Media at University of Massachusetts Amherst, bringing together experts in the fields of media studies and communications, Asian American studies, history, information sciences, and public policy, along with community advocates and community media journalists.

Thompson-Morton then hosted the Black Media Futures conference, which included a range of panels and interactive workshops examining Black media’s past, present, and future. The convening resulted in journalists discussing their concerns about sustainability but also their hopes and successes, as well as designing their dream headlines. 

Community media and its supporters are nothing if not collaborative. In that spirit Thompson-Morton and Rajagopalan teamed up to address an overlooked, but crucial topic relevant to both Black and Asian communities: gender-based violence. It resulted in a powerful session on the role of community media in covering an engrained problem. The experts convened from both communities shared their work with attendees but also their best practices for protecting survivors of gender-based violence in environments where silence and shame are not just common but encouraged and perpetuated. You can watch a recording of the webinar.

CCM had many collaborations this year, too. In April, we collaborated with National Trust for Local News (NTLN) on research about sustainability and succession in  community media outlets, funded largely by The Lenfest Institute for Journalism. The report compiled results of a survey conducted by CCM of 103 leaders of community media outlets serving racial, ethnic, or immigrant communities in 24 states. One of the main takeaways from this report is that 53% of community media outlets surveyed said they expected to go out of business within five years if current revenue trends continue. 

Not to be outdone, our Latino Media Initiative went into full swing after its new director and our talented colleague, Jesenia De Moya Correa, joined our team in June! During her time as the Latino Communities reporter at The Philadelphia Inquirer, De Moya Correa created a Spanish-language site called El Inquirer, to deliver online news, features, and service journalism for Hispanic and Latinx audiences in the greater Philadelphia area. You can read our Q+A with her here.

The Black Media Initiative also produced a thorough and groundbreaking project this past November: The Black Media Green Book. Taking its title from the historical publication known as The Negro Motorist Green Book, or simply The Green Book – a travel guide that listed hotels, restaurants, gas stations, and other businesses that were friendly and safe for Black travelers during the era of racial segregation in the U.S. 

CCM’s Green Book offers a comprehensive guide for publishers on a number of issues concerning staffing, sources, and business operations. It contains three sections, which are meant to be living databases of contacts and knowledge. The Freelancer Directory contains the contact information for a plethora of journalists from all over the world who want to work with Black community media. The Expert Directory is home to a growing list of social justice, science, tech, and education experts - many of whom have had media training and are eager to help Black media outlets with their work. The Green Book also provides a number of case studies about Black media publishers’ use of technology, surveys ranking the best content management and email systems, analytics platforms, and audience revenue and digital advertising solutions. 

CCM’s Advertising Boost Initiative, led by ABI Manager Darlie Gervais, also produced an impactful research report by Barbara Gray, Newmark J-School Associate Professor and Chief Librarian, with data analysis and visualizations by CCM’s Research and Development Associate Jennifer Cheng, and research and writing support from Khattak. The report, titled ‘Community Blindspot: Study of NYS agency advertising practices reveals lack of transparency that leaves community media business sector and audience overlooked’ was the result of a year-long research project. 

Based on data received from a third of New York state government agencies contacted about their advertising allocations, the researchers analyzed $216 million spent on 90 advertising campaigns covering various periods between 2015 and 2023. They found that the state’s community media received a meager 2.6%, about $5.6 million of the total $216 million spent on media buys over the decade. This was compared to New York City, which allocated $25 million to advertising in community media in 2020 and 2021 alone. 

We ended 2023 on a high note with the Asian Media Initiative taking on the enormous task of creating the first-ever map and directory of AAPI community media outlets. Director Rajagopalan, along with Cheng, Khattak, and researchers Basil Rodriguez and Selina Ho documented, categorized, and mapped nearly 700 outlets serving a variety of Asian American and diaspora communities. 

An interactive map of the AMI directory listings designed by CCM's Jennifer Cheng

The outlets are both traditional and digital media, and in 54 languages. From the total 228 municipalities large and small, AAPI media serve 58 U.S. metropolitan areas. The directory and interactive map provide a crucial resource to journalists and researchers in understanding the impact of AAPI community media and the communities themselves in a way that has not been documented before. 

Every entry in the ever-growing directory has been independently reviewed and verified against open-source and public record information. If you’d like to directly verify details about your outlet, correct any information in the directory, or add your outlet, please fill out the AAPI Media Directory Verification Form. 

The AMI is also asking for feedback on the directory and map heading into early 2024, when a subsequent research report detailing the initiative’s analysis of the directory and other findings will be published. 

Of course, research and trainings are not our only functions. CCM strives to serve as a resource for community media journalists and funders alike. Throughout the year, we responded to news cycles and aimed to provide clarity, links to amplify outlets’ coverage, and timely sources for your reporting and social media strategies on everything from mass shootings in California around Lunar New Year to the ongoing conflict involving Israel, Palestine, and Hamas and the upcoming U.S. elections. 

None of this would be possible without our own generous funders, journalists, and those working to support, sustain, and amplify community media. We want to express our gratitude for engaging with us, attending our training sessions and events, and participating in our research. We'll be back on January 11 with another edition of this newsletter and here's to a wonderful 2024!
