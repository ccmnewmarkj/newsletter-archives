Reporting on Small Businesses Three Years into the Pandemic
A street business operator sets up shop on an NYC sidewalk. Credit: Jehangir Khattak
CCM is excited to start the year off with a fellowship for community media journalists in the New York City metro region. Investigative journalists from the sector will have the opportunity to cover the challenges and successes of local community-owned businesses through the paid, three-and-a-half month Small Business Reporting Fellowship. The Fellowship intends to highlight not just the overall health of small businesses three years after the pandemic began, but also the future of the community media sector which heavily depends on the advertising support from it.

As CCM’s Director of Communications Jehangir Khattak explains: “New York City-based community media has been under tremendous pressure because of the changing media and business environment. The pandemic added to this media's vulnerabilities. Many outlets suspended operations and many more transitioned to a more robust digital presence, increasing the need for training.”

This is one of the many reasons why CCM is so grateful for the support of the Mayor’s Office of Media and Entertainment (MOME), which has provided the funding for this fellowship. With their support, CCM has also organized training sessions on a variety of subjects crucial to the sustainability of community media like preparing a professional media kit, reaching out to advertisers, branding on social media, and monetizing newsletters, among others. Khattak says that the relationship between CCM and MOME has also “helped us strengthen the capacity of the community media to generate more impactful journalism, add to media producers’ skill sets and enhance their capacity to establish a more robust digital presence.”

Applications for the Fellowship are due January 17. 
               

Social Media Tips for 2023

  
With the beginning of a new year, many community media publishers are expanding, or planning to expand, their audience through social media. Meena Thiruvengadam, a journalist with nearly 20 years of experience working with publishers to build high-value audiences across platforms, held an incredible training session with CCM last November and we want to share some of her important tips to help gain more readers and subscribers this year.
  • Start with asking three important questions: Where is your audience (Facebook, Twitter, print, etc)? What do they care about? Why are you here and how are you giving them news and information they care about?
  • Think about what platforms best align with your goals:
    • LinkedIn - A place to stay up to date and maybe drive traffic.
    • YouTube/TikTok - Is your news operation focused on video?
    • Reddit - Do you cover news relevant to a particular subreddit?
    • Instagram - Are you focused on photography and visuals? This can be a modest source of traffic.
    • Facebook - The largest audiences have been on here in recent years. Are your readers engaging with your page?
    • Twitter - This is usually right behind Facebook in terms of social referrals for many publishers.
    • Mastodon - A new platform that is decentralized and has risen as an alternative to Twitter.
  • Important things to remember: You don’t have to be everywhere! Focus on the platforms where your content fits best and where your audience usually spends time.
  • Don’t reinvent the wheel - there are resources available with best practices for each platform and copying styles of other media outlets is a good way to build presence.

As Thiruvengadam explains: “One way to lighten the load of creating for multiple platforms is to create content elements—bits of text, images, graphics and videos that can easily be adapted. It isn’t an Instagram post. It’s a visual you can use on Instagram, Facebook, Twitter and your site.” A great place to start is watching her strategy training session and participating in the exercises she outlines.

Black Media Initiative's Bridge Revenue Cohort

  
CCM’s Black Media Initiative also launched its inaugural Black Media Initiative Bridge Project: Audience Revenue cohort. “Our kickoff provided a valuable mix of learning, networking, and support to help these publishers build successful audience revenue programs and grow their businesses. The session was full of excitement and vulnerability from our publishers with them sharing the challenges and opportunities they faced previously in developing audience revenue. We are excited to see what comes from this group," BMI Director Cheryl Thompson-Morton says. The six-month program provides technology and design support for Black publishers looking to launch an audience revenue campaign. The six selected newsrooms are both traditional print media and digital-first organizations and represent communities all over the country from Maryland, Mississippi, Colorado, Ohio, Tennessee, and New York.

News from Inside the Community Media Sector

 

Congratulations to Joel Simon who joins the Newmark J-School on February 1 as the founding director of the Journalism Protection Initiative, which will conduct and host research into the growing threats to journalism and journalists in the U.S. and around the world, with a focus on disinformation and propaganda, tech regulation and policy, rising authoritarianism, and the use of violence, repression, and lawfare to suppress critical voices.

The National Federation of Community Broadcasters has filed public comments to the Federal Communications Commission (FCC) regarding how changes to the Emergency Alert System could affect community radio stations across the country.

Arizona State University’s Cronkite News spent a year researching how to bolster and improve news for small communities. The student-led project has developed ideas for local news organization Wick Communications on how to engage the community in deciding how they receive local news, how to better diversify their content and how to alleviate concerns about misinformation.

18by Vote has launched its Youth Movement Media project, which will be a combination of video and written content to inform youth, highlight youth leaders, and uplift youth voices.

Fellowships & Opportunities

The Knight Center for Journalism in the Americas at the University of Texas at Austin has a new online course: ‘How explanatory journalism informs and engages audiences,’ to be held January 16 - February 12. Students will learn techniques and formats for adding background and context to the news to help readers make sense of the daily outpouring of news, as well as to fight misinformation.

The Fund for Investigative Journalism is accepting proposals for grants to cover the expenses of investigative stories that break new ground and uncover wrongdoing in the public or private sectors. Grants are for up to $10,000 for stories on any topic. The deadline is January 30.

PEN America is looking for candidates for its Emerging Voices Fellowship, which provides a virtual five-month immersive mentorship program for early-career writers from communities that are traditionally underrepresented in the publishing world. Deadline is January 31.

The Newmark J-School is now accepting applications for its Entrepreneurial Journalism Creators Program. Students will earn a certificate after completing the 100-day training. Independent or employed journalists can apply to develop their own newsletters, podcasts, local sites, and other niche news products. The deadline is February 3.

The Metcalf Institute is hosting its annual Science Immersion Workshop for Journalists virtually and in-person at the University of Rhode Island in Kingston. It will explore the natural, social, and engineering research guiding an equitable transition to clean energy. Participants will meet in person June 11-16, 2023, and virtually on June 21, 23, 28, and 30 for a two-hour Zoom panel presentation each day between 12 and 2 p.m. ET. Deadline to apply is February 6.

Mongabay’s Conservation Reporting Fellowship is accepting applications. The program provides opportunities for journalists from biodiversity hotspots in tropical countries to report on critical environmental issues. Deadline is February 10.

The American Journalism Project, with support from the Google News Initiative, is now accepting applications for its Local News Incubator, intended to support entrepreneurs who want to explore launching a new nonprofit local news organization to serve their communities. Individuals and small teams with ideas for new local news organizations can apply for this 18-month incubator, which will provide both startup capital and expert counsel. The deadline to apply for the cohort is February 15.

The Council on Foreign Relations is accepting applications for the Edward R. Murrow Press Fellowship. Applicants must be U.S. citizens and have covered international news as a working journalist for print, broadcast, or online media widely available in the U.S. One fellow spends ten months full time in residence at CFR’s headquarters in New York. Deadline is March 1.

NYU’s Arthur L. Carter Journalism Institute is now accepting applications for the Matthew Power Literary Reporting Award, a grant of $12,500 to support the work of a promising early-career nonfiction writer on a story that uncovers truths about the human condition. Deadline is March 2.

The Al Día Foundation is looking for candidates for the Félix Varela Fellowship which will offer a full year of comprehensive training in AL DÍA News Media headquarters in Philadelphia. Deadline is April 3.
