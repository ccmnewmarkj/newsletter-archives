Community Media Minute

What's happening in the community media world

The exponential growth of the creator/influencer economy presents an opportunity for community media to collaborate, deepen trust, and grow their audience. PublicSource in Pittsburgh built a map of creators who are “trusted messengers” in the city who use Instagram, TikTok, and other social media, members of the newsroom told BetterNews. So far PublicSource has fostered conversations with creators, built formal partnerships, and increased their following on Instagram. 

The Current in Lafayette, LA shared its foray into community event organizing with Nieman Lab. The outlet runs two annual events - an awards ceremony recognizing people within the Lafayette community and a summit for leaders from mid-sized cities - that it says has helped the outlet increase revenue, build partnerships, and grow trust in its brand within the community. This piece is a great read for anyone looking to start or advance your event strategy, culminating in an excellent list of questions to ask yourself to determine which events are the best fit for your outlet and your community. 

One Good Read

Useful studies and reports to include in your week's reading

With the growing use of AI by resource-strapped newsrooms, chatbots are another way to harness the power of tech to help publishers and newsroom staff find better ways to run their business and serve the editorial needs of their community audience. Sound daunting? It may not be as hard or expensive as it may seemA new report from the UNC Center for Innovation & Sustainability in Local Media (CISLM) documented how four local newsrooms created and launched their own AI chatbots. Given financial and technical support from CISLM, The News Reporter in Columbus County, NC;  Henrico Citizen in Richmond, VA; Chapelboro, the digital arm of a radio station in Chapel Hill, NC; and the Atlanta Civic Circle all launched their chatbots in May and CISLM’s report tracked feedback over a 45-day period. The chatbots served a range of purposes from after-hours customer service inquiries, archive navigation, and answering basic reader questions. The report showed mixed results: the chatbots used for services worked well to reduce strain on staff but the engagement was low and sometimes chatbots designed to address reader questions yielded incorrect answers. Overall, it’s a fascinating read on how newsrooms that aren’t the New York Times or CNN are experimenting with AI, and what it looks like when an audience interacts with these tools. 

Pro Tip

A tip or tool for your newsroom

Trusting News, a research and training organization with the goal of helping journalists better serve their audiences, has advice regarding the use of AI. While it has become essential to learn and keep up with how to ethically use AI in newsroom and business operations, there are other benefits to understanding various tools. They offer some practical suggestions on how to demystify AI for your community audience, which could also be another way to build trust.

Fellowships & Opportunities

Tiny News Collective is hiring its inaugural Collective Fellow. The fellowship is aimed at early-career professionals with 1 to 3 years of experience who want to increase their knowledge and skills in community-rooted media, community management, nonprofit management and the journalism support landscape. If you have questions, email Careers@tinynewsco.org. 

The University of Chicago Booth School of Business’ Stigler Center has a 12-week Journalists in Residence program that is offering a $14,000 stipend and the opportunity to audit classes at Chicago Booth while participating in seminars and networking with scholars. There is no minimum experience required, but the program is suitable for those looking to learn more about the political economy. The application deadline for the Spring 2026 program is October 1.

Are you working on a‘follow the money’ story? The Harold W. McGraw, Jr. Center for Business Journalism might be able to support you! Journalists with at least five years experience and reporting expertise on the subject of their pitch can apply for  grants up to $15,000 and the editorial support needed to produce deeply reported enterprise and investigative stories with a strong economic, financial or business angle. The deadline for fall fellowships is October 13.
