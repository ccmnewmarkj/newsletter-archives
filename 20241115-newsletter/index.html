Community media and the post-election landscape

We are a little over a week out from the historic 2024 presidential election and there have been a slew of stories analyzing and theorizing about the exit polling data, why voters chose Donald Trump over Kamala Harris, and what all of it means for communities across the country. . 

Here at CCM we  continue the work to help bolster community media leaders  and journalists through our sustainability programming, training programs, virtual and in-person events, and our various media initiatives. 

Our National Elections Reporting Grant Program has concluded its coverage with crucial reporting from Michigan, Georgia, Nevada, Pennsylvania, and Wisconsin where not just the presidential race hung in the balance, but also important House, Senate, and state races. 

Latino Press in Detroit documented the reactions of the community in southwest Detroit to Trump’s second win. NotiVisión Georgia did the same all over the state, analyzing Latino voter turnout during the 2024 presidential election, which their reporting showed was marked by an increase in mobilization and a shift toward former President Donald Trump. Al Bustan News reported on Philadelphia’s Young Arab and Muslim voters’ feelings about the election and the crisis in the Middle East. These outlets, along with The Atlanta Voice, Nyob Zoo TV in Milwaukee, the Philippine Times of Southern Nevada,  The Arab American News, and BridgeDetroit produced  important reporting about voting rights and procedures, local races, and information about the presidential campaigns relevant to their communities this election season. 

CCM also ran a similar program for New York City area media earlier in the year. The 2024 Elections Reporting Mentorship Cohort included 16 seasoned journalists from community media outlets in the region who were guided by a mentor to produce nearly 50 stories about local ballot initiatives and races, voters’ reactions, voting registration guidelines, and the presidential election. With the additional training sessions and funding these journalists received they were able to write about everything from how artists in the cities were responding to the elections, an equal rights amendment on the statewide ballot, immigrants’ relationship with politics in their home counties and how that impacts voting patterns here, and other issues surrounding local races. 

In addition to these programs, the Asian, Black, and Latino Media Initiatives have been working steadily on bolstering community media publishers and journalists this election season. The Black Media Initiative published Why Black Media Matters Now in October 2021 but it is still relevant today and this look back is an important one when talking about the impact of Black voters. The report was a comparative analysis between Black and mainstream media during the last presidential election cycle. The research showed that Black media talked about topics of importance to Black communities more often, and connected news events to history in a way mainstream media did not. 

On October 17, Black Media Initiative Director Cheryl Thompson-Morton was joined by Aswad Walker, Associate Editor of The Houston Defender; Kaia Shivers, Founder/Editor in Chief of Ark Republic and Black Farmers Index; and Macollvie Neel, Special Projects Editor of The Haitian Times to discuss how that work has evolved in this election cycle. You can watch a recording of the event here. 

Asian Media Initiative Director Kavitha Rajagopalan shared resources through the AMI Elections Project and hosted an Elections Forum to discuss new tools and research for community media to cover the diversity of AAPI voters, who have the largest increase in registered voters among all voting demographics. You can watch a recording of the event here, which includes panelists from several community organizations and researchers. 

Latino Media Initiative Director Jesenia De Moya Correa hosted a journalist safety event in Spanish with Dagmar Thiel, CEO, Fundamedios USA and Patricia J. Peña, Associate, Davis Wright Tremaine LLP. The training session focused on the threats and violence that community journalists and Latino reporters experience in the field, how to get information and take photos at voting centers, protests, and demonstrations, legal safeguards, and how to deal with law enforcement at protests. 

Of course, a variety of community media outlets have been doing stories on the elections results and what this might mean for their futures. The outlets serve communities all over the U.S. in a variety of languages and provide a lens on the elections and post-elections analysis that larger media outlets may not be able to provide - one that goes beyond exit polling to the heart of how their respective communities and thinking and feeling right now.
