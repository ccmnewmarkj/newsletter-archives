AAPI communities devastated by mass shootings around Lunar New Year
Red Chinese Lanterns Hanging Under the Night Sky. Credit: Milada Vigerova
We at CCM are deeply saddened about the back to back mass shootings affecting Asian American and Latinx communities in California this past week. Deepening these tragedies were the revelation that both shooters in the separate incidents were identified as Asian men, both senior citizens. During these mass shootings, which have become frustratingly common in the U.S., community media is more important than ever for the information, resources, and honored memories of victims only journalists intimately familiar with these communities can provide.

Our Asian Media Initiative Director Kavitha Rajagopalan has gathered some of the insightful coverage of the shootings done by community media outlets in the wake of the shootings, over on Twitter. She also suggests reading, listening to, and supporting the following outlets in both Los Angeles and the Bay Area:
  • Sing Tao Daily
  • Nguoi Viet
  • Rafu Shimpo
  • World Journal LA+SF
  • The Yappie
  • Mochi Magazine
  • Asian Journal
  • News for Chinese
  • India Currents
  • CaliToday
  • The Trace, a newsroom dedicated to covering gun violence, also has reporting that may be useful to read

Even as AAPI communities join other communities of color in grappling with escalating white supremacist violence and hate crimes, the recent mass shootings seeming to involve perpetrators targeting members of their own communities is difficult to process. Anh Do, Metro reporter at the L.A. Times, grew up in the Nguoi Viet newsroom in the area. Do has been sharing the painful questions the AAPI communities in Los Angeles and Half Moon Bay are asking themselves in light of the identity of the shooters and makes the connection with another mass shooting by an elderly Asian man targeting Asian parishoners at a church in Laguna Woods, California last May.

Uvalde, Texas is majority Latinx, and on May 24, 2022 a Latino shooter killed 19 children and two adults and injured 17 others in their school, which he had also once attended. Community media journalists bring a deeper understanding of their community to explain crimes committed against them, by their own. In the case of Uvalde, Michelle Garcia of Palabra notes the shooter's issues are related to the issues of a "constructed war zone" around the U.S.-Mexico border. She wrote: "That the gunman was Latino and his victims were largely Latino children attending a historically segregated 'Mexican school,' does not reflect the absence of race and racism. On the contrary, it may well have been the consequence of an apparatus tinged with nativism and racism that stokes self-hatred."

Furthermore, the Half Moon Bay and Monterey Park communities are reeling from losing so many elders. For immigrant communities, older people are the link to traditions and cultures from thousands of miles away. They have also been the living embodiments of the historical struggles of communities of color - whether immigrant, Indigenous, or those brought here against their will. Geniece Crawford Mondé wrote about what it means that most of the Black victims of the May 14, 2022 shooting in Buffalo, New York had survived the Jim Crow era and made it well into old age only to be killed by a white nationalist while grocery shopping.

We cannot forget, however, that community media is run by journalists from the communities affected and to that end, may also be processing trauma and grief. The Dart Center for Journalism and Trauma has a host of tipsheets and resources to help those covering the tragedies, particularly a guide for those covering gun violence. The Asian American Journalists Association is also holding a grief/healing session for those affected by the recent shootings on January 29 at 4 PM PST/7 PM EST. Jeanie Y. Chang, LMFY, CCTP, CIMHP, an AAPI mental health expert and certified clinical trauma professional, will be facilitating the session.

Consider also supporting the Monterey Park Lunar New Year Victims Fund, all of the proceeds go to victims and surviving families.

News from Inside the Community Media Sector

 

The Lillian Holofcener Charitable Foundation emptied its coffers to put $1 million towards the establishment of the Baltimore Beat, a free newspaper for the Black community in the city that is staffed by Black editors and writers. Lisa Snowden-McCray leads the print and digital newspaper and explains the process of how the grant was secured and a business plan was developed for success.

The American Journalism Project announced it has awarded $3.25 million in grants to  Enlace Latino NC, Fort Worth Report and Nebraska Journalism Trust. Congratulations to the recipients who will use the investment to find ways to significantly increase revenue and ensure long-term sustainability including hiring for key roles on business and operations teams as they also expand into their communities.

The Tulsa World, based in Tulsa, Oklahoma, and Rapid City Journal in Rapid City, South Dakota are adding reporters to their newsrooms in a new partnership with ICT, formerly Indian Country Today. These reporters will focus on producing coverage of the two areas’ Indigenous communities. The work of these reporters will appear on their respective print and online publications, parent company Lee Enterprise's other 75 news publications in 26 states, indiancountrytoday.com and ICT’s daily newscast.

Congratulations to the cohort of 13 news organizations selected by American Press Institute for a year-long program using the group’s Source Matters tool! The tool allows outlets to track who is quoted in local stories and assess if the sources they quote accurately reflect the communities they serve. Source Matters is also currently accepting new partners.

Dozens of journalism groups representing more than 3,000 newsrooms around the country have joined the newly formed Rebuild Local News as members. Founder Steven Waldman said the group aims to deliver $3-5 billion in support for local news companies through a combination of philanthropy, business initiatives, and government proposals.

A water pipe burst over the holidays and ruined much of the inventory and office of Third World Press, the country’s oldest independent and continuously operating Black-owned publisher, located on the South Side of Chicago. There is a crowdfunding campaign set up to help the publisher recover some of its losses.

Fellowships & Opportunities

Report For America helps local newsrooms report on under-covered issues and communities by sending early-career and mid-to-late-career reporters and photographers to newsrooms throughout the country. RFA Corps Members serve for 2-3 years and earn a salary. There are currently more than 55 positions available. Deadline is January 30.

The National Institute for Health Care Management (NIHCM) Foundation is taking applications for its Health Care Digital Media Award which recognizes excellence in digital media that improves understanding of health care topics through analysis grounded in empirical evidence. Deadline is January 31.

The Newmark J-School is now accepting applications for its Entrepreneurial Journalism Creators Program. Students will earn a certificate after completing the 100-day training. Independent or employed journalists can apply to develop their own newsletters, podcasts, local sites, and other niche news products. Media creators and entrepreneurs in New Jersey are eligible for a full scholarship to attend the program, thanks to funding from the Center for Cooperative Media, a $4,000 value. Applicants must be based in New Jersey. Deadline is February 3 and other scholarships are available upon submission.

The Society of Environmental Journalists is hosting its annual conference which will take place in Boise, Idaho this year and is offering Travel Diversity Fellowships in partnership with The Uproot Project. Deadline is February 6.

The Open Notebook‘s Covering Science Slack is a peer-mentoring community for local and general assignment reporters and editors. Those (staff or freelance) at U.S.-based media outlets who don’t consider science to be their beat but who are interested in incorporating science coverage into their work are encouraged to apply to join the Slack group. Deadline is February 8.

The Next Challenge is awarding $130,000 to eight groundbreaking for-profit and nonprofit startups that will reinvent media in the coming decade. One grand prize winner will receive up to $50,000. Deadline is February 17.

The International Center for Journalists is accepting applications for the Arthur F. Burns Fellowship. After language training, Fellows work as temporary staff members at host newspapers, magazines, and radio and television stations in Germany for nine weeks. Deadline is March 1.

The International Women's Media Foundation is accepting applications and nominations for three awards: The Anja Niedringhaus Courage in Photojournalism Award, which recognizes the importance of visual journalistic work that inspires us to take action and compels us to better understand the world. The Gwen Ifill Award is open to U.S. women and non-binary journalists of color working in the news media. Candidates for the award will be evaluated on criteria including their record of outstanding achievement in journalism, and the extent to which they represent the values Ifill embodied, including in the areas of mentorship, leadership, and commitment to diversity in journalism. The Courage in Journalism Awards honor the brave journalists who report on taboo topics, work in environments hostile to women, and share difficult truths. The deadlines for all three are March 3.

Type Media Center is accepting applications for the Ida B. Wells Fellowship. The one-year fellowship helps reporters complete their first substantial work of investigative reporting by providing a $25,000 award and intensive mentoring from dedicated editors at Type Investigations. Deadline is March 12.

Very Asian Foundation is offering a Creators Grant, a microgrant opportunity for Asian American, Native Hawaiian, and Pacific Islander individuals who have pivoted their careers toward their creative passions. Deadline is March 19.

Muslim journalists are welcome to express interest in joining a virtual network that is explicitly inclusive of and prioritizes Muslim and Muslim-adjacent journalists, particularly including populations that are marginalized within mainstream Muslim communities and or populations who are historically marginalized in general, like Black Muslims, queer and trans Muslims, Shi'i Muslims, etc.
