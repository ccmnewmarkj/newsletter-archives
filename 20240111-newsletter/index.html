CCM’s new multi-initiative program complements the promise of $500 million for local news

Press Forward, announced in September 2023, is a national coalition of 22 donors who have pledged to infuse local news organizations with half a billion dollars over the next five years. In theory this is the angel investor community media outlets have been seeking for years; a transformative force that could quench news deserts and amplify crucial voices.

However, almost immediately local and community media leaders of color raised alarm bells - will there be a commitment to funding outlets like those that CCM’s Black, Asian, and Latino Media Initiatives advocate for and work with on a daily basis? Journalist organizations like the National Association of Black Journalists (NABJ), National Association of Hispanic Journalists (NAHJ), and Asian American Journalists Association (AAJA), Indigenous Journalists Association (IJA), and several others all signed an open letter citing several key points about how any philanthropic efforts are welcome but also carry the “responsibility to be inclusive, intentional and transparent about how funding from this initiative is distributed.” 

It’s a documented fact that nonprofits led by people of color win less grant money and are saddled with more conditions for funding than organizations that are not. Many of you know all too well the issues with obtaining and maintaining the funding you need to serve your communities in the best way as well as sustain your own livelihoods as journalists. CCM has even partnered with the National Trust for Local News (NTLN) on research about sustainability and succession in community media outlets, funded largely by The Lenfest Institute for Journalism. The report compiled results of a survey conducted by CCM of 103 leaders of community media outlets serving racial, ethnic, or linguistic communities in 24 states. One of the main takeaways from this report is that 53% of community media outlets surveyed said they expected to go out of business within five years if current revenue trends continue.

Amethyst J. Davis, the founder of the Harvey World Herald, a news outlet based in the Chicago metropolitan area, further explained the challenges of fundraising for organizations like hers for Nieman Lab, writing: “Philanthropy, I believe, isn’t about determining where you will invest. It’s about figuring out where you won’t. Invite-only grant processes. Websites that don’t list staff with direct contact information.” 

Press Forward appears to be listening to media leaders like Tracie Powell, founder of The Pivot Fund, and Dr. Meredith Clark, director of Northeastern University’s Center for Communication, Media Innovation and Social Change, who co-authored a paper on making journalism philanthropy more effective. The initiative’s first round of $48 million of funding was awarded to several BIPOC-led and -serving organizations. 

But, CCM’s Directors Cheryl Thompson-Morton (Black Media Initiative), Kavitha Rajagopalan (Asian Media Initiative), and Jesenia De Moya Correa (Latino Media Initiative) don’t want to think of fundraising as a passive job necessity - they want to empower community media publishers through a new training cohort. 

While institutional funding issues for ethnic and community media persist, there are still ways in which outlets can put themselves in the best position to receive funding, sustain operations, and continue to serve their audiences. 

Their three-month, tuition-free, online program seeks to equip Asian, Black and Latine news leaders with the skills to develop business strategies, sustainable revenue streams around fundraising, grant writing, how to prospect your audience, how to plan campaigns, and to share information and resources on the philanthropic landscape to uncover new funding options. The Fundraising for Community Media program is the result of a collaboration between CCM and The Lenfest Institute for Journalism’s News Philanthropy Network.

Up to 15 news outlets will learn about engagement tactics used by peers and coaches, how to dismantle taboos around accessing money, and ways to connect these learnings to their outlet's strategies for delivering public service journalism through biweekly virtual sessions from April to June led by other, culturally-competent community media publishers in the industry. 

Applications open Friday, January 12, 2024 and close Monday, February 19 at 11:59 pm EST. 

News from Inside the Community Media Sector

Congrats to our friends at the Center for Cooperative Media on the publication of their first report about U.S. Democracy Day! The report, by Mission Partners, highlights the evolution of U.S. Democracy Day from an idea in a Twitter thread to a 163-member collaborative with participants across 43 states that poses the question: what does it mean for newsrooms to defend democracy? 

Three cheers for Word In Black, a collaborative of 10 Black media publishers, on forming a public benefit corporation! Local Media Foundation, an organization affiliated with Local Media Association and in which WIB was incubated, will serve as a supportive stakeholder. 

Poynter reports that as independent local news publishers reimagine business models, they face a fresh set of challenges. 

Also from Poynter, the latest news on the unexpected staff shakeup at relatively new outlet, Houston Landing.

Speaking of stunning staff changes, the Executive Editor of The L.A. Times Kevin Merida is stepping down this week and the reason looks to be a conflict with the owners, the Soon-Shiong family, over Merida’s decision to restrict journalists who had signed a letter condemning Israel from covering the country’s ongoing conflict involving Hamas and Palestinians, per The New York Times. 

Texas Observer reports on how an “unconventional citizen journalist” stood up for free speech by suing the Laredo public officials who had her arrested. 

A Brazilian fact-checking organization has been using a ChatGPT-esque bot to answer reader questions, Nieman Lab reports. 

Also from Nieman Lab, a new study asked participants to take to Google and do further research on both real and fake stories. But, looking more into fake stories led these readers further from the truth.

Substack, the popular host of several journalists’ independent newsletters, says it will remove Nazi publications from the platform.

Radio shows surprising resilience even in a rapidly changing media world, CNBC reports.

Fellowships & Opportunities

Entrepreneurs and creators in journalism are invited to apply for the Entrepreneurial Journalism Creators Program (EJCP) at CUNY’s Newmark J-School. This unique 100-day online certificate program is now open for applications through February 2. 

The Pulitzer Center provides grants to cover the hard costs of reporting projects. The only broad parameter is that projects address global systemic crises. The Pulitzer Center supports projects, not just one-off stories. The overarching mission is to raise awareness of underreported global crises, so the most successful applications demonstrate that the journalist has thought about how to reach the widest possible audience. Deadline is rolling. 

​​​​The Lever is looking for an editorial fellow to produce a weekly aggregated article. It is a nonpartisan, reader-supported investigative news outlet that holds accountable the people and corporations manipulating the levers of power. Fellowship pays $200/week, plus $500+ payment for any additional accepted pitches. Pitching guidelines are here. Deadline is rolling.

Reynolds Journalism Institute’s Pictures of the Year International 2023 competition is open for entries. Deadline is January 14.

NPR has opened applications for its Reflect America Fellowship, a year-long position aimed at bringing a greater diversity of voices to NPR's news coverage. The fellow will join NPR's Climate Desk and report on vulnerable communities, particularly Indigenous communities. Deadline is January 26.

Applications are being accepted for the Fund for Investigative Journalism’s Winter 2024 grant cycle. Deadline is January 29.

The Social Science Research Council seeks applicants for the third cohort of its Just Tech Fellowship. This fellowship supports and mobilizes diverse and cross-sector cohorts of practitioners to imagine and create more just, equitable, and representative technological futures. Deadline is January 31.

​​​​​Penn State University’s Donald P. Bellisario College of Communications is accepting nominations for the Bart Richards Award for Media Criticism, which honors work that evaluates news media coverage of significant subjects or issues. The winner will attend the award ceremony at the National Press Club and talk on the subject for which they are being honored. Deadline is January 31.

The entry portal is open for the PEN America Emerging Voices Fellowship, which provides mentoring and a $1,500 stipend for early-career writers from communities that are traditionally underrepresented in the publishing world. Deadline is January 31.

Biographers International Organization is accepting applications for the Frances “Frank” Rollin Fellowship, which awards $5,000 each to two authors working on a biographical work about an African American figure or figures whose story provides a significant contribution to our understanding of the Black experience. Deadline is February 1. 

The Pulitzer Center is taking applications for the 2024 Richard C. Longworth Media Fellowships, which are open to freelance and staff print, broadcast, and online journalists based in Chicago or elsewhere in the Midwest. The $10,000-$20,000 fellowships support international reporting by Chicago and Midwestern journalists. Deadline is February 1. 

​​​​​​Columbia University is accepting applications for the Ira A. Lipman Center For Journalism and Civil and Human Rights’s Fellowship, which provides a $10,000 grant plus editorial and reporting guidance. Each fellow produces a 5,000-word story or series regarding civil and human rights broadly construed, such as voting rights, use of force by police, employment and other discrimination, human trafficking, child labor and more. Deadline is February 15.

The Carter Institute at NYU is accepting applications for the Matthew Power Literary Reporting Award, which awards $12,500 to support the work of a promising early-career nonfiction writer on a story that uncovers truths about the human condition. Deadline is February 22.

​​​​​The City Bureau, in Chicago, runs a Civic Reporting Fellowship that offers 16 weeks of learning and work experience for emerging journalists with little or no professional journalism experience. The spring session will begin April 8; pay is $6,000. Deadline is March 3.
