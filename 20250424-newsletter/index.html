A tailored approach to sustainability

CCM kicked off its inaugural Community Media Sustainability Initiative cohort this week with leaders from five New York City metro area publications. The program pairs the leaders with three mentors who specialize in editorial, marketing, and digital strategy with the end goal of increasing audience engagement and revenue for the long term. 

Publishers will receive targeted, one-on-one mentorship tailored specifically to accelerate their success and amplify their impact. Through the pilot program, these leaders will actively collaborate in a supportive community, sharing innovative strategies and best practices with peers facing similar challenges. After completing a comprehensive needs assessment, each publisher will engage in weekly strategy sessions with dedicated mentors who will craft customized action plans aligned with their unique goals and resource capabilities.

The mentors will help them to fill coverage gaps, improve their marketing tools, refine a media kit for potential advertisers, and work on a digital strategy that can enable them to better market their products. They will also assist the newsroom leaders with optimizing their media operations and collecting data to help them better understand their audiences. 

“Implementation of the strategy from the mentors is critical. Long term sustainability is key here,” Jehangir Khattak, CCM’s Director of Communications, said. 

Darlie Gervais, CCM’s Advertising Boost Initiative Director, explained that "this program is about giving newsrooms the tools and space to think differently about how they operate—how they connect with audiences, how they grow, and how they evolve. It’s not just coaching, it’s capacity building, designed to meet each newsroom where they are and help move them forward."

As Khattak and Gervais, who are running the program, both noted, each of the three areas of strategy complement each other. “The digital analytics and marketing work are very much connected to the editorial strategy,” Gervais said, adding that the newsroom leaders will document their progress, gaps, and hurdles throughout the program so mentors can better tailor a custom strategy for their publications. 

“For many publishers, even with extensive education and experience in journalism, much of the learning process has been self-taught, which can feel isolating. The cohort is valuable not only for the information, mentorship, and relationships with guiding professionals, but also because participants become part of a community sharing this experience—throughout these 12 weeks and beyond. That is our intention with this work,” said Carla Zanoni, who oversees CCM and leads Newmark J-School’s Engagement Journalism M.A. program.

The newsroom leaders selected for the cohort are: 

Scott Stiffler from Chelsea Community News, a hyperlocal news, arts, events, info, and opinion website serving Manhattan's Chelsea neighborhood and its surrounding areas. 

Julia Moak from Greenpointers, a hyperlocal site for news, events, food, and culture from north Brooklyn. 

Pat Stevenson from the complementary community newspapers serving the African American community through Harlem Community News, Queens Community News, Brooklyn Community News and Bronx Community News.

Ibrahim Chowdhury from Prothom Alo, a weekly, Bangla-language, print newspaper covering the whole city for the Bangladeshi community. 

Maria Amado from Westchester Hispano, a Spanish-language print and digital newspaper serving the Latino community in Westchester County and surrounding areas including Putnam and Rockland counties and part of southern Connecticut.

Our mentors include digital analytics expert Sarah Darby, currently the Director of Audience Insights at Chicago Public Media which includes WBEZ and the Chicago Sun-Times; marketing expert Ashley Grimm, publisher of three Charlotte-based magazines and the owner of a marketing agency, and Mythili Sampathkumar, a widely published freelance journalist who also served as the Managing Editor for CCM’s Elections Reporting Mentorship last year who will serve as the editorial mentor. 

Following the program, newsroom leaders will document key metrics that show the impact of the program allowing CCM to further refine the programming for future cohorts.
