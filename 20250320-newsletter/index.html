News 

Nieman Lab reported that local newsrooms like Chalkbeat and Midcoast Villager are using the AI-powered database LocalLens to listen to local governments’ public meetings, search transcripts, and find sources for their stories. The tool uses AI to transcribe and summarize these meetings from across the country. 

The Cortland Standard in central New York State, a family-owned newspaper which began printing in 1867, announced it has shut down its print operations, in part due to tariffs on newsprint from Canada. It was one of the five oldest family-owned newspapers in the U.S. 

As nearly 2,000 people have been or will be laid off from the National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA), local meteorologists are concerned about what will happen to their jobs of providing daily weather forecasts because NOAA maintains key sources of information from the National Hurricane Center and the Weather Prediction Center, per Poynter.

The Open Notebook, a resource for journalists covering science, partnered with Reynolds Journalism Institute to start building a training module and toolkit to help journalists integrate science reporting into their daily news coverage. Their goal is to highlight that many stories about pollution, education, reproductive health, AI, and other topics are all science stories as well as other beats. 

The Journalist’s Resource explored the methods journalism students at the University of California - Berkeley used to expose how some police officers in California were hiding misconduct charges on their employment records. The piece is part of a series of interviews with finalists for the Goldsmith Prize for Investigative Reporting at the Shorenstein Center on Media, Politics, and Public Policy. 

With the accelerating closure of digital news outlets, journalists increasingly face the loss of their published work to link rot when web archives disappear or remain unmaintained. Fortunately, Poynter reports that freelance multimedia journalist Brandon Tauszik, working with The Starling Lab for Data Integrity at Stanford, is developing a solution to help journalists preserve their online work. 

Public radio stations across North Carolina joined forces to create the North Carolina Newsroom, Capitol Bureau to provide more robust coverage of the statehouse, Nieman Lab reported. The collaboration includes WUNC, Blue Ridge Public Radio, WFAE, WHQR, and WFDD and is funded by the Corporation for Public Broadcasting. Similar initiatives have been launched in 13 other states as well.

Fellowships & Opportunities

Underscore Native News is accepting applications for the Underscore Indigenous Journalism Fellowship. The program equips and encourages the next generation of indigenous journalists. Each summer, the organization hosts and trains a college journalism student or early-career journalist for an immersive 10-week program. Fellows earn an hourly salary, fully paid housing on the campus of Lewis and Clark College in Portland, OR, and a scholarship upon completion. Deadline is March 20. 

Local Independent Online News (LION) Publishers seeks applicants interested in joining one of ten peer learning groups.  Each group will be led by a LION community ambassador and meet monthly from April to September to discuss challenges, solutions, and resources. Final sessions of the groups will be held at the Independent News Sustainability Summit in St. Louis this fall. The groups focus on Spanish language, BIPOC, email-first initiatives, fundraising, immigrant news leaders, tech stack development, rural newsrooms, and more. Deadline to apply is March 21. 

ProPublica is taking applications for its yearlong Investigative Editor Training Program. Ten journalists will participate in a weeklong boot camp focused on building skills around conceiving  and producing investigative projects that expose harm and have impact. Editors learn to manage reporters who are working with data, documents and sensitive sources, including whistleblowers, agency insiders and people who have suffered trauma. The training takes place in New York in June. Apply by March 24. 

The 19th seeks applications for the 2025-26 cohort of the Frances Ellen Watkins Harper Fellowship. Alums of Historically Black Colleges and Universities who are early to mid-career are awarded one-year, salaried positions in reporting, audience engagement, and product management with the news outlet. Apply by March 31. 

The Carter Center’s Rosalynn Carter Fellowships for Mental Health Journalism invites applications to support a diverse cohort of journalists from the United States, Ireland, Qatar, and the United Arab Emirates. These fellowships equip journalists with resources to report on mental health—one of the world's most underreported health issues. . Former First Lady Rosalynn Carter established the program in 1996 to help dismantle stigma and discrimination through powerful storytelling. Apply by April 2.
