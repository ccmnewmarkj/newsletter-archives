Coming up at CCM this month 


​​​​CCM hosts three virtual AI training sessions this March, featuring data journalism, prompt writing, and image creation. Mike Reilley, Senior Lecturer at University of Illinois Chicago and JournalistsToolbox.ai founder, leads each session with two topics. Register separately for each unique session.

March 12: Fact-Checking Tools & Best Practices and Data Journalism from 12-2 PM EST 
Explore ways to fight the spread of disinformation online through the use of innovative tools to detect spreaders of false information on social media, doctored video and audio, and tracking fact-checked stories. You will also learn how to use AI tools to scrape sites for data and analyze it. 

March 19: Intro to AI Tools and Prompt Writing from 12-2 PM EST
Harness tools to save journalists time and effort, the legal and ethical issues involving the use of AI in journalism, and some pitfalls to avoid. Participants will also walk through some prompt writing exercises to prevent bias and have tools present responses in their preferred format. 

March 26: AI Tools for Journalists and Image Creation from 12-2PM EST
Receive hands-on time to explore several different AI tools, including those used to create images through prompts, and discuss each tool’s strengths and weaknesses.

News 


CCM and Newmark J-School’s J+ initiative are thrilled to announce the inaugural cohort of our AI Community Engagement Lab. Over the course of the year these 18 journalists will explore how AI can help community newsrooms strengthen their audience engagement, build trust within the communities they serve, and expand their reach. They represent Black, Latino, AAPI, and immigrant communities in New York, Illinois, Kansas, Texas, Arizona, California, and Washington. Participants will engage in biweekly virtual classes, hands-on workshops, online coaching, and a final project — all designed to equip them with practical AI applications for community journalism. 

New York Amsterdam News is turning its Harlem newsroom into a museum, WNYC reported. 

The John S. and James L. Knight Foundation is granting the American Journalism Project $25 million, one of its largest-ever grants. The money will go towards expanding AJP’s support to 60 local newsrooms and to creating the Knight Resiliency Lab, which will provide expertise to help local news organizations become sustainable, per Axios. 

The Associated Press has launched its Local Investigative Reporting Program, led by Ron Nixon. Building on AP’s Local News Success Team, which localized national stories for member audiences, this new team connects with state and local news outlets to support their investigative needs, help with the use of AI tools, and provide training, data analysis services, and connections to staff and subject matter experts. 

Time for Kids is taking media literacy to a new level and training the coming generation of journalists before they even enter high school, Poynter reported. 

Newswell, a nonprofit affiliated with Arizona State University, acquired three newsrooms - Stocktonia, Times of San Diego, and the Santa Barbara News-Press - through donation. Nieman Lab reported that with its grant, Newswell aims to “work toward long-term sustainability and provide back-end support [to the newsrooms] like HR, accounting, IT, and legal support, as well as ‘audience, membership and advertising expertise.’”

Media Power Collaborative, a group of media workers, researchers, and advocates under nonprofit group Free Press, released its policy agenda which describes local news a public good, akin to roads, schools, water, and electricity and calls for public funding to help sustain it rather than hedge funds, private owners, or newspaper chains. 

Journalism education organization, the Allbritton Journalism Institute and its nonprofit news arm NOTUS, have launched the AJI-NOTUS Washington Bureau Initiative. The program seeks to help local news outlets’ audiences understand what their elected officials are doing on Capitol Hill even if those outlets do not have a Washington correspondent themselves. The participating newsrooms include Oklahoma Watch, San José Spotlight, Verite News, Times of San Diego, Santa Barbara News-Press, Stocktonia, and The Assembly based in North Carolina.

Fellowships & Opportunities

Underscore Native News is accepting applications for the Underscore Indigenous Journalism Fellowship. The program equips and encourages the next generation of indigenous journalists. Each summer, the organization hosts and trains a college journalism student or early-career journalist for an immersive 10-week program. Fellows earn an hourly salary, fully paid housing on the campus of Lewis and Clark College in Portland, OR, and a scholarship upon completion. Deadline is March 20. 

ProPublica is taking applications for its yearlong Investigative Editor Training Program. Ten journalists will participate in a weeklong boot camp focused on building skills around conceiving  and producing investigative projects that expose harm and have impact. Editors learn to manage reporters who are working with data, documents and sensitive sources, including whistleblowers, agency insiders and people who have suffered trauma. The training takes place in New York in June. Apply byMarch 24. 

The 19th seeks applications for the 2025-26 cohort of the Frances Ellen Watkins Harper Fellowship. Alums of Historically Black Colleges and Universities who are early to mid-career are awarded one-year, salaried positions in reporting, audience engagement, and product management with the news outlet. Apply by March 31. 

The Carter Center’s Rosalynn Carter Fellowships for Mental Health Journalism invites applications to support a diverse cohort of journalists from the United States, Ireland, Qatar, and the United Arab Emirates. These fellowships equip journalists with resources to report on mental health—one of the world's most underreported health issues. . Former First Lady Rosalynn Carter established the program in 1996 to help dismantle stigma and discrimination through powerful storytelling. Apply by April 2.
