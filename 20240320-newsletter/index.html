New York State could pass several bills to support local media ecosystem

We want to thank our readers as we test out new strategies on how best to deliver news to you about community media! Check your inboxes every Wednesday for our newsletter featuring articles, news links, job listings, events, opportunities, and tips. 

Photo courtesy of NYS Senate Media Services

In addition to our Black, Latino, and Asian Media Initiatives, a cornerstone of CCM’s work involves working with community media publishers to help them become more financially sustainable. Part of this could be through training sessions on how to get advertising revenue, specifically from government agencies’ advertising budgets which are trying to target the communities served by publishers. Another part of that work, spearheaded by Advertising Boost Initiative Director Darlie Gervais, also involves pushing for governments to enact legislation to support a vital public service like community media journalism. CCM is engaged in efforts within New York State to support all initiatives aimed at revitalizing news outlets like the proposed bills in NY State Legislature committees right now. 

We have already worked with Chicago to pass an Executive Order in October 2022 committing at least half of the city’s annual departmental advertising spending to community media outlets. That was a replication of our efforts right here in New York City where we worked towards passing a groundbreaking 2019 executive order and the subsequent 2021 legislation, which delivered over $25 million in advertising dollars to more than 200 community media outlets in New York City in fiscal years 2020 and 2021. 

Of course, more efforts are needed to support community media outside of urban areas as well. Northwestern University’s Local News Initiative noted in its State of Local News 2023 report that “counties with 10 or more outlets are overwhelmingly urban or dense suburbia, while news deserts are usually rural.” 

In some exciting news, there is a slate of proposed bills which were introduced in the New York state legislature last winter that are in various stages of being considered by the state Assembly and Senate for voting that could positively impact both urban and non-urban community media in the state. 

The Local, Community, and Ethnic Media Commitment Act, introduced last April to the Senate and May to the Assembly and supported by the New York Press Association, would not only formally establish criteria to define community and ethnic media and have the state maintain a public directory of said outlets but also would require state agencies publish certain critical information in community and ethnic media outlets across the state. The proposed bill states: “It is essential that all residents have meaningful access to state programs, services, and activities, and that the public safety, health, economic prosperity, and general welfare of all New Yorkers is furthered by expanding access to state programs and services, regardless of native language, race, color, gender, national origin, ethnicity, religion, sexual orientation, disability or immigrant status.” One way to increase access to these programs is by disseminating information about them in media that is specifically serving those New Yorkers, in languages and mediums they can understand and easily access.  

Supported by the New York News Publishers Association and Empire State Local News Coalition, the Local Journalism Sustainability Act was introduced to the state Senate in January 2023 and Assembly in February of last year. This bill would have a direct impact on the financial viability of community media outlets and make audience strategies a key focus for publishers if passed. The proposed bill would provide tax credits to consumers for subscriptions to local news outlets and to news organizations for the employment of local news journalists. 

Text from the proposed Local Journalism Sustainability Act 

BronxNet Television supported the introduction of the Community Media Reinvestment Act to the state Senate in January of last year and the Assembly last March. The proposed legislation would help address the impact that the proliferation of satellite and streaming services have had on “local video programming responsive to the unique needs and interests of New Yorkers, including public, educational, or governmental access television programming.” Essentially, streaming and satellite services would be charged an excise tax for operating in the state. The taxes would then be deposited into what the proposed bill would establish as the Community Media Reinvestment Fund. After a contribution to the state’s general fund, municipalities, and operating expenses, the proposed fund would then disburse about 40% revenue to “entities responsible for operating and administering public, educational, and/or governmental access channels in the state.”

The New York City Association of Local and Ethnic Media (NYCALEM) supported the introduction of legislation last January to the state Senate and March to the Assembly that would establish a state-wide office of ethnic and community media and would require state agencies to seek to direct at least 50% of its total spending on advertising to ethnic and community media outlets. The latter provision is modeled off of the 2019 Executive Order and the 2021 legislation passed in NYC. Per the proposed bill: “Legislating an office dedicated to supporting these outlets can contribute to bridging cultural gaps and fostering a more harmonious and integrated society. Members of ethnic and community groups often rely on media outlets that cater specifically to their communities for relevant and accurate information. Establishing an office focused on supporting such media can help ensure that these communities have reliable access to news and information.” 

"Publishers, journalists, broadcast managers, and stakeholders in journalism's future must unite in supporting these critical efforts," Gervais explained. 

Stay tuned to this space as we track the progress of these bills and speak to those supporting and opposing the proposed pieces of legislation as well as members of the NYS Senate and Assembly who are sponsoring them. 

If you are looking to draft and introduce proposed legislation, CCM can offer resources and guidance. Please contact darlie.gervais@journalism.cuny.edu for that information.
