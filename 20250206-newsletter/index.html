News 

News veteran Tennyson Donyéa relaunched Black In Jersey late last year, as reported by the Center for Cooperative Media to mark Black History Month. 

The Institute for Nonprofit News released a do-it-yourself guide to help newsrooms explore collective fundraising. 

The Maryland General Assembly introduced HB 51, requiring news outlets to give 120-day notice to communities, workers, and the Labor Department before any sale. Both Rebuild Local News and the News Guild support the bill, noting it could prevent private equity firms from acquiring and reducing local news coverage.

El Tímpano is protecting its immigrant sources by changing its reporting practices, according to Nieman Lab. The Bay Area news outlet announced the policies at the end of last month, ahead of the changes to immigration policy planned by the current presidential administration. 

Also from Nieman Lab, a network of AI-generated newsletters targeting “small town America.”

For the first time in its 225-year history, the Keene Sentinel of New Hampshire changed its business model and started a journalism fund and community advisory board. 

Small news outlets that focused on federal staffers landed big scoops and saw an increase in traffic, per CNN. 

Trusting News shared strategies on how to help audiences stay informed but not overwhelmed during particularly busy news cycles. 

Northwestern University’s Medill Local News Initiative explored how Meta’s policy reversal could mean more misinformation for communities lacking local news. Poynter also covered the issue of Meta’s impact on fact-checking in news deserts.

NEWSWELL, a project at Arizona State University offers an innovative approach for giving local newsrooms the support they need, reports Poynter. 

The Center for Cooperative Media featured a Q+A with digital marketing and revenue management platform BlueLena about how it supports local publishers with sustainable reader revenue strategies. 

The Local Media Association chronicles how a community activist became a journalist and publisher because he had gained the trust of his Florence, Colorado community.

Local media can leverage local identity and history to stand apart and create new value, reports the American Press Institute.

Maine Public Radio highlights Maine Trust for Local News' success with branded content revenue.

And, here’s a message from nonproft environmental news outlet Grist: Grist stories are available to republish and translate for free; as long as there is a republication button on the story, it can be republished. Translations are permitted as long as the translator's name is listed on the article page. Images are often available, but check first with syndication@grist.org. For questions, email Rachel Glickhouse.

Fellowships & Opportunities

The application process is open for early-career journalists to apply to the California Local News Fellowship, a state-funded initiative through the University of California - Berkeley to support and strengthen local reporting, with a focus on underserved communities. This full-time, paid fellowship will place early-career journalists (with up to five years of experience) in California newsrooms for two years, starting in September 2025. Deadline is February 21. 

Applications are open for the Nonprofit News Business Certificate, a 9-month, project-based training program that will be a game-changer for nonprofit local newsrooms in the United States at a time when the nonprofit news sector is surging. The certificate, developed by J+ at the Newmark J-School, in partnership with the American Journalism Project and Blue Engine Collaborative, will focus on business and financial management for leaders of nonprofit news organizations. Deadline is March 3. 

The Carter Center’s Rosalynn Carter Fellowships for Mental Health Journalism is accepting applications to support a diverse cohort of journalists from the United States, Ireland, Qatar, and the United Arab Emirates. Fellows are deeply committed to exploring some of society’s biggest mental health challenges. The mental health journalism fellowships program was founded in 1996 by former First Lady Rosalynn Carter based on an essential premise: to give journalists the resources they need to report on mental health — one of the world’s most underreported health issues — and help dismantle through storytelling the stigma and discrimination that millions of people face every day. Deadline is April 2.

Events

Feb 13 (Online): Join our Black Media Initiative for an event on how Black Media Archives Black History. Director Cheryl Thompson-Morton will moderate a panel with Brandon Nightingale of Howard University; Dr. Allissa V. Richardson from the University of Southern California; and Savannah Wood of Afro Charities about printing and archiving stories and photos overlooked by mainstream media and how Black media is leveraging those archives for revenue and impact.

Feb 13 (Online): An investigation by the Newmark J-School’s Equity Through Data Project uncovered a large and troubling list that includes parts of some of New York City’s most iconic roadways. In this training on “Using Data to Spotlight Infrastructure Vulnerabilities,” the data and related reporting will be shared with news outlets across New York City so they can do their own stories, geared for their audiences. The information and resources to be previewed at this training will include enhanced tools to use available data to spotlight potential vulnerabilities for communities throughout the five boroughs.

Feb 26 (Online): Trusting News hosts an event on strengthening your coverage through honest newsroom conversation and dissent.

May 15-16 (Denver): The Center for Cooperative Media at Montclair State University is hosting the 2025 Collaborative Journalism Summit. The Summit is still open to session pitches and will feature two days of lightning talks, workshops, and participatory discussions about a wide array of topics and projects.
