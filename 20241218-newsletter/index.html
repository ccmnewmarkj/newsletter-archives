Year in Review

Members of NYC community media join CCM for its holiday party at Newmark J-School December 12, 2024 Credit: Helina Selemon

It’s been a busy and rewarding 2024 at CCM! From programming to in-depth research reports to supporting community-focused reporting about the historic election, here’s a look at some of the work our team did this year to ensure the sustainability of community media outlets and journalists. 

As our Executive Director Mikhael Simmonds said: “In a year that tested every corner of the media ecosystem, community media rose to the challenge, proving that resilience and innovation aren’t just survival tactics—they’re the heart of ensuring every community’s voice is heard.”

As part of CCM’s mission of supporting the business side of news outlets and not seeing fundraising as just a passive necessity, we also hosted our inaugural fundraising cohort where a group of 15 AAPI, Black, and Latine-led newsrooms were empowered to learn fundraising strategies like grant writing skills and donor prospecting. The tuition-free program, in collaboration with The Lenfest Institute for Journalism’s News Philanthropy Network, is the first of its kind, and was designed to address the specific issues of media leaders of color like the historical trend of giving organizations led by people of color less money than those that are white-led and a potential over-reliance on advertising revenue. 

Throughout the year we also focused on editorial support, specifically about the elections at the local and state levels, and the upheaval during the presidential race. We produced newsletters beginning last November on how journalists can best frame their reporting based on what their audience needs, shared resources on fighting disinformation, and other topics. We were thrilled when our friends at Democracy Fund shared our newsletters with their readers. 

​​​​Importantly, we hosted two more cohorts surrounding elections reporting, ballot initiatives, and politics. Beginning in the spring, we selected 16 seasoned community media journalists from the New York metro area for our Elections Reporting Mentorship. CCM Director of Communications Jehangir Khattak, Research and Development Associate Jennifer Cheng, and Managing Editor/Mentor Mythili Sampathkumar, organized training sessions on how to cover elections, the mechanics of getting political ads as well as the ethics of accepting political advertising, and held one-on-one meetings with each of the journalists throughout the program to produce nearly 50 stories for news outlets across the boroughs and metro area in English, Spanish, Chinese, Arabic, and Nepali. 

“These stories brought community perspectives, which were missing in the mainstream media,” Khattak explained. The stories (first round, second round and third round) covered everything from political ideas carried over from home countries, artists’ and high school students’ thoughts, explainers for immigrants on how to vote for the first time, oft-ignored ballot initiative explainers, the influence of Black Greek life on the presidential election, local races in Queens and Long Island, voting for the incarcerated, and the impact of redistricting to name just a few. Khattak called the mentorship program, made possible with the generous support of the NYC Mayor’s Office of Media and Entertainment, just one of CCM’s “big success stories” of the year.

Khattak and Sampathkumar were also able to highlight CCM’s elections-related media support work in a guest lecture session for participants in the National Press Foundation’s Widening the Pipeline Fellowship over the summer.  

Not to be outdone, our national initiatives also ran a National Elections Reporting Grant program! Led by Black Media Initiative Director Cheryl Thompson-Morton, Asian Media Initiative Director Kavitha Rajagopalan, and Latino Media Initiative Director Jesenia De Moya Correa and initiative-specific coaches, nine newsrooms in the states of Georgia, Michigan, Nevada, Pennsylvania and Wisconsin were able to receive help with in-language editorial consulting, training sessions, physical safety, newsgathering rights, polling, and more. They also received grant funds which they were able to use for funding reporting from the campaign trail, equipment, and adding to their freelance budgets, among other costs associated with elections reporting. These news outlets produced three rounds of stories and community events on everything from how to register to vote; what political action looks like; Indian American, Black, Arab, and Latino voters’ outlooks; voting for third parties and what the electorates in Michigan and Georgia were experiencing, and a slew of reactions to election results in their states and across the country. 

All three national initiatives also held their own elections-related events with De Moya Correa discussing safety issues and the outlook for Latino journalists going forward after November 5. She said: “Our newsrooms in Latino community media saw some light at the end of the tunnel this 2024. Despite the challenges we look forward to, I'm hopeful that this 2025 we will be able to expand our reach and effort, build on new initiatives for our audiences and care for each other in the sector.” 

Thompson-Morton held extensive sessions on the role of Black media during this election cycle and what appealing to young Black voters through messaging looks like. Rajagopalan hosted the AMI Elections Forum as well.  

Of course, not everything CCM did this year focused on the elections! 

By far, the biggest project undertaken by Rajagopalan was the AMI’s groundbreaking report, AAPI News Media: Origins and Futures. Complementing Rajagopalan and Cheng’s work on publishing the first-ever map and directory of AAPI community-serving media in the U.S. and its territories last December, the report explores how diverse AAPI communities – from San Francisco’s Chinatown to New York City’s Little Syria – established and lost their news media sectors during the 19th and early 20th centuries, only to re-establish a vibrant news media ecosystem the AMI documented in the directory and map. 

Published on May 26, the 100th anniversary of the 1924 Immigration Act (Johnson-Reed Act) that banned all immigration from Asia, it is more than just a timeline of events but also discusses the rise of digital disinformation and its harmful effects on everything from the pandemic to elections, online harassment, overseas influence, and other pervasive threats facing AAPI media, as well as the need to keep working at making these outlets, which serve the fastest-growing demographic in the U.S., more sustainable, safe, and reliable. As Rajagopalan said, 2024 was “a critical year for AMI, expanding on our original research to pilot innovative approaches to building revenue and capacity in this long-overlooked media sector. AAPIs continue to face threats to their safety and barriers to voting, and our programming lays the groundwork for supporting this underserved community in these challenging times.” 

Looking back on publishing the historic map and directory, Cheng noted that “a year into the release, I am looking forward to 2025 when we will update and revamp our other directory maps, and continue to provide tools to help showcase and visualize the breadth and diversity of the community news media across the country into next year and beyond.” 

The AMI and Rajagopalan were also given the Community Impact Award by the Asian American Journalists Association at its conference in Austin this past August, in part for the map and directory but also fostering a sense of community through its continued operation of the Asian Media Women's Network. The director had also appeared on a panel with community media publishers to discuss the future landscape for AAPI media. 

Khattak also conducted a session on community media outlets as small businesses and the struggles of getting loans as a result. The Business Initiative Corporation of New York, Empowerment Zone at the Bronx Economic Development Corporation, and Ponce Bank joined CCM to talk about new small business loans backed by the U.S. Small Business Administration and programs to help small businesses develop the financial, technological, and strategic skills they need to thrive.

Thompson-Morton conducted a workshop on how community media publishers can build their tech stacks. The goal was to make building out your outlet’s tech toolbox less intimidating and overwhelming. Any outlet, whether in Black media or otherwise, can make everything from publishing, distributing, and monetizing content easier and more effective with the right tech stack. The event was an offshoot of the Black Media Green Book, Thompson-Morton’s 2023 report, a resource that makes connecting with freelancers, experts and technology partners easier and more accessible for Black media outlets. 

The BMI also brought back the popular Black Media Futures conference, a half-day, virtual event which featured panels on reparations for Black Media, leveraging AI, and voting, plus a freelancer networking session. Fostering more connections and sharing or pooling resources for the sustainability of the entire community media landscape is something Thompson-Morton predicts we will see more of in the near future: “In 2024, we saw both great triumphs and challenges. Looking ahead to 2025, we know there will be more obstacles, but I expect to see increased collaboration and cooperation between community media outlets to face future headwinds together.” 

Speaking of tech and sharing resources, Simmonds has been focused on how community media can best use AI in 2024. AI has been in the news nonstop this past year as the number of ways it can be used seems to grow leaps and bounds by the day, with no signs of slowing down. Journalism has been grappling with how best to use generative AI in a variety of ways. CCM hosted a session on visuals and AI and one on identifying fake images and video with John Smock, Newmark J-School’s Director of Photojournalism - follow-ups to our 2023 session on generative AI uses in community newsrooms. 

Any new technology can be intimidating at first, but particularly with AI, Smock said “there is so much almost mystical language around AI that anthropomorphizes it…people are freaked out by it.” However, he also noted that the potential to add value to a community media outlet’s audience is too great to be scared to test the waters.

In 2025, Simmonds “expects these vital community-serving outlets to push even further, embracing new technology and collaboration to cut through the noise and keep their communities informed.” 

In that vein, CCM has partnered with J+ at the Newmark J-School to offer the AI Community Engagement Lab kicking off early next year. The new six-month training program is designed to empower community journalists with artificial intelligence tools and techniques and enhance audience engagement. The program is tuition-free thanks to the generous support of the Walton Family Foundation. 

Darlie Gervais, CCM’s Advertising Boost Initiative (ABI) Director, had a busy year too while tracking potential New York state legislation affecting community media, holding sessions with publishers about advertising revenue issues, and helping select publishers develop a crucial asset for obtaining advertising: media kits. These documents not only tell the story of a community media outlet, but also explain why advertisers should choose to do business directly with a publication through audience demographic data and clear print and digital pricing options.   

Gervais, on the ABI, said: "At the heart of [it] is a commitment to equity, representation, and financial sustainability for community media. We have seen what’s possible when these outlets are given the resources they deserve. As we reflect on 2024 progress, my hope in 2025 is to see these vital outlets not just recognized but fully resourced to thrive—positioned to grow their revenue, expand their reach, and continue serving New York City’s diverse communities. Let's continue building on this work together to ensure community media are supported by investments that match their critical role."

Here’s to a wonderful 2025 for all of community media!
