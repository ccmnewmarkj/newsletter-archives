Why do free subscriptions matter in an election year? 

First, a shout out to Newmark J-School Dean Graciela Mochkofsky for an amazing panel at the Knight Foundation Forum in Miami this past week, titled “Empowering Local News.” You can see Mochkofsky, S. Mitra Kalita of URL Media, Alicia Bell of the Borealis Foundation, and John Palfrey of the MacArthur Foundation speak about how newsrooms can become “the glue for communities” in a recording of the session. 

Next, we came across an important study conducted by the American Journal of Political Science and University of Pennsylvania about the impact of giving potential readers free subscriptions to newspapers. 

The study, titled “Unsubscribed and Undemanding” and conducted in 2021 but published last year, showed that out of a little over 2,500 people offered free subscriptions to the Pittsburgh Post-Gazette and Philadelphia Inquirer, less than two percent of people actually accepted the offer. 

In a year of repeated mass layoffs at several large media outlets and budget cuts  - the trend of not even being able to give away free media subscriptions could be seen as a troubling sign of a lack of trust in media in what will be a long election year. Dr. Daniel Hopkins of UPenn, who conducted the study, told the Columbia Journalism Review the premise of the study came to him after writing his book ‘The Increasingly United States’ (University of Chicago Press, 2018). “[D]eclining interest and access to local news forces voters, who are not otherwise familiar with the specifics of their local governments’ agendas or legislators, to default to national partisan lines when casting regional ballots. As a result, politicians are not held accountable, voters are not aware of the issues, and the candidates who get elected reflect national ideologies rather than representing local needs.” 

In other words, community media is essential to upholding democracy in our communities across the country now more than ever. But, building trust in the media and community engagement are as well. 

It’s part of the reason why CCM will be publishing a series of newsletter issues on election reporting - you can read our first two in the series here and here. 

Much of the Knight Foundation Forum conversation and discussions, held by so many publishers and staff at organizations like ours, involved and continue to involve supporting local news with increased and targeted funding, but also enabling news outlets to implement revenue generating models. CCM explores this issue through our flagship Advertising Boost Initiative and our inaugural cohort to train community media publishers with the skills necessary to proactively pursue fundraising. The Black Media Initiative also conducted its inaugural cohort in 2023 for the Bridge Project: Audience Revenue, a free six-month program that provided technology and design support for Black publishers looking to launch a subscription, membership or donation campaign.

The variety of approaches is important because what works for one media outlet may not work for the other - the experiment with the Pittsburgh and Philadelphia newspapers are proof of that.
