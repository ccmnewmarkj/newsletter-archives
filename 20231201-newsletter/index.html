All about the numbers: using data in your stories 

It’s no longer enough to produce news stories around interviews alone. A credible article must be backed by data presented in an engaging and compelling way, according to Sandeep Junnarkar, Data Journalism Program Director at the Newmark J School. 

He led a training this past week with CCM about how to build compelling stories around data that can help serve community media audiences. You can watch the full recorded session here. 

The goal of data journalism is to inform and clarify stories for readers or viewers who have questions about how we as journalists know if a story is accurate. Including data in our reporting can provide assurances to our audiences about a particular story but also serve to highlight patterns or trends. However, “numbers alone can be numbing…data needs to be humanized. We still are storytellers,” Junnarkar pointed out. 

Part of the issue with journalists not traditionally including data in our stories - although Junnarkar noted that trend is changing - is that it can seem daunting for those without an expertise in math, statistics, or coding. Junnarkar said the key to overcoming this hurdle though is to “think of data as another source with a story to tell…You have to interview the data.” Asking descriptive and conversational questions as you would to a local official from city hall or a local business owner can help in analyzing the data you are studying. 

Junnarkar said the great part about ‘interviewing’ the data is that “this source is not going to hang up on you. This source is not going to [evade] your questions. This source will spend as much time as you want with it!” 

Not all data journalism stories are full of numbers either, but data is used to bolster anecdotal reporting. He gave an example of looking into school funding. If you interview the data and find that spending increased by 5% every year, but this past year it only increased 2% - that is a question you can ask a city official to explain. Asking why there was a cut to a particular school program, showing press officers the trend you identified in the data, can force officials to answer questions more concretely than if you did not have the numbers to back up your line of questioning. 

Junnarkar also dove into more complex analysis of data sets through coding and automating some of those processes and the emergence of artificial intelligence, but said that while technical skills can help going forward, “the editorial judgment of what makes a good data story” will still be crucial.

A groundbreaking project coming from our Asian Media Initiative

Our Asian Media Initiative Director Kavitha Rajagopalan, along with CCM's Research and Development Associate Jennifer Cheng are developing the first-ever directory and map of nearly 700 in-language and community based news media for and by AAPI across the U.S. and its territories, ranging from hyperlocal outlets published on messaging apps to legacy newspapers serving major urban hubs to new digital outlets for younger audiences and historically marginalized AAPI communities. The directory and map will offer a number of interactive features enabling users to filter and sort data and generate tables and data visualizations on different segments of the ecosystem, and will be accompanied by a user guide and fact sheet about the ecosystem. This is the first effort dedicated to visualizing and representing this vast and diverse ecosystem, ahead of a critical election year and amid ongoing discrimination and violence toward diverse AAPI communities.

Every listing in our directory has been verified using open-source and public record information, but if you'd like to directly verify your outlet's details, please fill out this survey.

Stay tuned to this space and your inbox for updates on the map, directory, related events, and a report on the landscape of AAPI community media coming out early next year. 

News from Inside the Community Media Sector

Big congratulations to Karen Rundlet, who joins the Institute for Nonprofit News as its new CEO! Rundlet was previously the Senior Director for the Journalism Program at the John S. and James L. Knight Foundation. 

The Mayor’s Office of Ethnic and Community Media here in NYC released its Annual Advertising Spend Report for FY2023. This report provides a picture of City advertising spending in the wake of the COVID relief funds from the Federal government ending and our current budget crisis.

From Associated Press, there is a new survey on the use of generative artificial intelligence in newsrooms. It can be taken anonymously and will help researchers at Northwestern and the University of Amsterdam write up the findings in a report in 2024. 

Nieman Lab asks if public media podcasts facing a “Moneyball” moment: “In an era where the “easy money” is gone, celebrity sluggers are beyond reach, and commercial outfits are pulling back, public radio orgs can win by leaning into data and ideas that helped them create the art form.”

An OP-ED in Poynter discusses how local media needs to reclaim the religion beat amid the recent conflict involving Israel, Palestine, and Hamas. 

Substack, the popular newsletter management platform, will now offer a suite of video tools, the Washington Post reports. It could make the platform another avenue for community media to reach audiences with video directly in their inboxes instead of YouTube. 

Poynter offers an explainer on Press Forward, the much-anticipated $500 million initiative for local news. 

Northwestern University’s Medill School of Journalism and its Local News Initiative have published the first part of its ‘State of Local News 2023’ report. “The data and insights collected and analyzed in this 2023 report on The State of Local News paint the picture of a country and society increasingly divided between the journalism-have’s — mostly residents in more affluent cities and suburban areas where alternative news sources are gaining traction — and the journalism have-not’s, those in economically struggling and traditionally underserved metro, suburban and rural communities.”

The Objective has a piece on how community access television is “more than a soundbite.”

Also from The Objective, a piece that makes the case for education reporting to stop using the phrase “culture war.”

The Institute for Nonprofit News presents three case studies exploring lessons learned from nonprofit news organizations to illustrate what DEI (diversity, equity, and inclusion) efforts in journalism look like now.

A new paper from researchers at the University of Kansas and Northeastern University takes a deep dive into examining gaps in journalism curriculum to solve the news desert crisis. 

On a related note, KCRW in Los Angeles explores if teaching media literacy in schools help fight misinformation. And, News Literacy Project offers a downloadable infographic explaining confirmation bias and motivated reasoning: “it's incredibly easy to get sucked into an echo chamber where every piece of news a person sees or seeks out validates their belief or identity. Confirmation bias and motivated reasoning are two cognitive biases that contribute to a fractured info environment where nuance gets squashed and facts are often inconvenient.”

Newsroom Robots featured Newmark J-School’s Jeremy Caplan in its roundup of how 10 news industry leaders use ChatGPT in their personal lives. You can also subscribe to the podcast hosted by Nikita Roy, who tackles different topics at the intersection of artificial intelligence and journalism.

Fellowships & Opportunities

The Pulitzer Center provides grants to cover the hard costs of reporting projects. The only broad parameter is that projects address global systemic crises. The Pulitzer Center supports projects, not just one-off stories. The overarching mission is to raise awareness of underreported global crises, so the most successful applications demonstrate that the journalist has thought about how to reach the widest possible audience. Deadline is rolling. 

Sierra Club/Sierra Magazine is hiring an editorial fellow. This is a 6-month fellowship geared toward early career journalists interested in environmental justice, climate science, and conservation. This is a fully remote fellowship that can be done from anywhere in the US. It runs from January - June on part time hours (28 hours/week), and pays $20/hour. Except for those two hours of weekly meetings, specific working hours are flexible. They are accepting applicants on a rolling basis until the positions are filled.

Fortune Media is accepting applications for the Fortune Newsroom Fellowship running from January through June. Fellows can work in newsrooms in New York, Chicago, or San Francisco or remotely. Deadline is rolling. 

The James Beard Foundation has opened the entry portal for the 2024 James Beard Awards. This year they have split the media awards into Journalism Awards and Broadcast Media Awards. Deadline is November 30; later deadlines for entries published in December 2023.

The LA Press Club is accepting applications for their six-month Foot in the Door Fellowship. Journalists from communities historically excluded from the media with less than five years in journalism are eligible to apply. Deadline is December 4. 

Rowhome Productions is taking applications for its 3rd annual Rowhome Fellowship. The goal is to nurture and support the next generation of audio producers in Philadelphia. The fellowship is open to new producers in the Philadelphia-area looking to launch their audio storytelling and podcast production careers. Deadline is December 8. 

The Journalism and Women Symposium (JAWS) Health Journalism Fellowship will support eight emerging and early career journalists who have an interest in and passion for upping their healthcare reporting skills. Fellows’ projects are supported with a $4,000 grant, a one-year membership to JAWS, and complimentary registration and travel expenses to CAMP for 2024. Deadline is December 8.

The CatchLight Global Fellowship focuses on the power of visual storytelling to change the world. Proposals must represent excellence in the visual field and innovative engagement ideas. $30,000 stipend, plus personal development opportunities, including executive coaching. Deadline is December 9.

USC Annenberg Center for Health Journalism is taking applications for the 2024 California Health Equity Fellowship. Fellows receive grants of $2,000-$10,000 to cover expenses for their projects, a week of training, and five months of mentorship. Freelancers must have an assignment. Deadline is December 10.

The Reynolds Journalism Institute is accepting applications for the 2024 Women in Journalism Workshop, scheduled for April 12-14 at the University of Missouri. Deadline is December 15.

The International Women’s Media Fund has opened the entry portal for the 2024 Kim Wall Memorial Fund grants, which award $5,000 to cover reporting on stories about “subcultures” and related costs including travel, logistics, insurance, visa fees, professional stipends, and payments for producers and translators. Women and nonbinary journalists with at least a year of professional experience are welcome to apply. Deadline is December 17. 

IRE is accepting applications for the 2024 Gary Marx Scholarship to attend the NICAR Conference March 7-10 in Baltimore. Deadline is January 8

The Chronicle of Philanthropy is accepting applications for its Philanthropy & Nonprofit Accountability Fellowship from US news organizations, with preference given to local or regional reporting teams. The fellowship is directed by the Chronicle of Philanthropy, a nonprofit news organization that covers nonprofits, foundations, and others involved in advancing the social good. The Chronicle is committed to helping local news teams access the tools and training they need during the fellowship and over the long haul to better inform and engage their audiences on topics as varied as the arts, climate, housing, and health. Deadline is January 8.

NPR has opened applications for its Reflect America Fellowship, a year-long position aimed at bringing a greater diversity of voices to NPR's news coverage. The fellow will join NPR's Climate Desk and report on vulnerable communities, particularly Indigenous communities. Deadline is January 26.

The Social Science Research Council seeks applicants for the third cohort of its Just Tech Fellowship. This fellowship supports and mobilizes diverse and cross-sector cohorts of practitioners to imagine and create more just, equitable, and representative technological futures. Deadline is January 31.

Biographers International Organization is accepting applications for the Frances “Frank” Rollin Fellowship, which awards $5,000 each to two authors working on a biographical work about an African American figure or figures whose story provides a significant contribution to our understanding of the Black experience. Deadline is February 1. 

The Pulitzer Center is taking applications for the 2024 Richard C. Longworth Media Fellowships, which are open to freelance and staff print, broadcast, and online journalists based in Chicago or elsewhere in the Midwest. The $10,000-$20,000 fellowships support international reporting by Chicago and Midwestern journalists. Deadline is February 1. 

The Carter Institute at NYU is accepting applications for the Matthew Power Literary Reporting Award, which awards $12,500 to support the work of a promising early-career nonfiction writer on a story that uncovers truths about the human condition. Deadline is February 22.
