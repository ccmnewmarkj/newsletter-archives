Gearing up for Election Day and post-elections reporting

We are only four days away from the historic 2024 Election Day, but early voting has begun in several states and mail-in ballots have already been returned by millions of Americans. Our jobs as journalists are heating up. 

Our National Elections Reporting Grant Program is wrapping up its coverage with more reporting from Michigan, Georgia, Nevada, Pennsylvania, and Wisconsin as the presidential candidates crisscross these states and make stops in New York and Washington, D.C. 

NotiVisión Georgia published a useful FAQ for Spanish-speaking voters covering logistics, documents needed, and Georgia employment law that requires employers to give people time to vote. They also have an explainer on ballot initiatives, translating them into Spanish and interpreting them. The Atlanta Voice also provided service journalism to its readers with a piece on early voting, how to find polling places, and requirements with a story on the record number of Black voters who have already cast early voting ballots and how Atlanta-area votes break down. 

The strength of community media is deep coverage of hyperlocal races - the kinds of races for elected officials who have a major impact on the day to day lives of Americans that larger media outlets are often not able to cover. The Arab American News is covering the Dearborn and Dearborn Heights Board of Education elections and school bond initiatives in the area. They also have a story on how the Trump, Harris, and Stein presidential campaigns are courting Muslim voters and their respective stances on the crisis in the Middle East. BridgeDetroit has an explainer piece on how Michigan counts the number of eligible voters and cleans up its voter rolls as voter fraud and verifying election results are issues of concern for election officials and campaigns. 

Latino Press in Detroit has a piece on the necessity of tailored outreach and education to address the unique challenges faced by the Latino community (p. 17) - and why experts felt that relying on basic Spanish resources will fall short of what is needed to mobilize this demographic effectively.

Nyob Zoo TV in Milwaukee has a video story on how different generations of the Hmong community are looking at this election. The Philippine Times of Southern Nevada also took a generational look at their community - how Filipino American youth are shaping the political landscape in the Las Vegas region. 

Al-Bustan News interviewed Senate candidate Leila Hazou on why she calls for the urgent legitimizing of third-party voting as she runs to represent Pennsylvania for the Green Party. 

A special thanks to the Jonathan Logan Family Foundation, MacArthur Foundation, and The Tow Foundation for their support of the program. 


Black Media’s role in this election 

We understand the unique role community media plays in elections - newsrooms on the frontlines of democracy; sometimes the only news outlets holding local power accountable or explaining complicated ballot initiatives. 

That role becomes even more important when we talk about voter demographics and those communities’ media. Three years ago, the Black Media Initiative published Why Black Media Matters Now, a comparative analysis between Black and mainstream media during the last presidential election cycle. The research showed that Black media talked about topics of importance to Black communities more often, and connected news events to history in a way mainstream media did not. 

On October 17, Black Media Initiative Director Cheryl Thompson-Morton was joined by Aswad Walker, Associate Editor of The Houston Defender; Kaia Shivers, Founder/Editor in Chief of Ark Republic and Black Farmers Index; and Macollvie Neel, Special Projects Editor of The Haitian Times to discuss how that work has evolved in this election cycle. You can watch a recording of the event here. 

The panelists discussed how their coverage has evolved, specifically how misinformation about the Haitian community spread on the campaign trail and how that impacted The Haitian Times and its work covering the election, what challenges each of these outlets have, and what support could be most useful to them. 

For more information on the BMI and elections resources, please contact Cheryl at cheryl.thompson-morton@journalism.cuny.edu.
