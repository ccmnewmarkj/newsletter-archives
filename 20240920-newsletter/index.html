News from Inside the Community Media Sector

If you have been following the election news, you know that former President Donald Trump and VP nominee, Senator JD Vance, both perpetuated misinformation about Haitian immigrants in the town of Springfield, Ohio. The Haitian Times has been reporting on the aftermath of the spread of that misinformation as have local outlets in the region, from attacks on the community to school closures due to threats. 

Presidential candidate VP Kamala Harris’ campaign has granted a limited number of interviews to the media, but is stepping up their efforts in the run up to November, focusing on local news outlets and social media, podcasts, and streaming channels - venues where an increasing number of younger voters are getting their news, The New York Times reports. She also recently sat down with the National Association of Black Journalists to discuss misinformation, health care, and the economy. 

NPR has a story about how Instagram made all teen accounts on the platform private in a move to improve safety and address longstanding security concerns. 

Verge reports on how Google has a plan for its search and advertisement functions to help people distinguish real images and fake ones, a crucial component in the larger fight against disinformation. 

The Local Media Association has five big takeaways from the recent conference of the Association of LGBTQ+ Journalists (NLGJA). 

American Press Institute has four ideas on election engagement that can lead to longer-term relationships.

An op-ed from Columbia Journalism Review explores a new collaborative model for mapping the US local news landscape. 

Poynter spoke to Ryan Sorrell, founder of Kansas City Defender, about following Ida B. Wells and Claudia Jones in “honoring the legacy of the radical Black press” and whether he considers himself a ‘media organizer’ or a journalist.

Nieman Lab reports on a study about why journalists end up staying in our profession, even while so many leave. 

The American Journalism Project announced its new effort to revitalize local news in Los Angeles. “The L.A. Local News Initiative will launch a nonprofit organization that will operate and support local newsrooms in Los Angeles to provide coverage at neighborhood, regional, and state levels in service of L.A. communities.” The initiative, which has raised $15 million so far, is also searching for a leadership team. 

Better News offers some examples of how to use live events to bolster elections coverage. 

An AI chatbot helped Americans who believe in conspiracy theories “exit the rabbit hole,” Nieman Lab reports.

Fellowships & Opportunities

J+ has created the AI Journalism Labs to empower journalists not to be afraid of this new technology but to experiment with it, apply it to their news careers and become part of the conversation. After a successful pilot program, we are inviting experienced journalists at all levels to explore generative AI through theory and practice in one of these 3-month hybrid, tuition-free and highly participatory programs in 2025: AI J Lab: Leadership or AI J Lab: Adoption. If you are a mid-career journalist or news technologist in a management position or a news executive, please consider applying for the AI Journalism Lab: Leadership. If you are a reporter, editor, technologist or educator in a news organization with little or no AI experience, please consider applying for the AI Journalism Lab: Adoption. Deadline is October 18.

The Pulitzer Center is seeking applications from journalists who want to report stories as part of Connected Coastlines, a nationwide climate reporting initiative in U.S. coastal states. Started in 2019, this initiative is building a consortium of newsrooms and independent journalists across the U.S. to report on the local effects of erratic weather patterns on coastal populations using the latest climate science. Deadline is rolling. 

IRE is accepting applications for The Chauncey Bailey Journalist of Color Investigative Reporting Fellowship. The yearlong program is intended to prepare and support a journalist of color for a solid career in investigative reporting. The program also provides an opportunity for the fellow’s news outlets to benefit from the fellow’s investigative skill-building. Deadline is September 21. 

The Solutions Journalism Network is seeking pitches for its Train-the-Trainers October 2024 program from U.S.-based journalism trainers, editors, reporters, and journalism school educators interested in (or already) leading solutions journalism training for journalists. This is an opportunity to develop solutions journalism training skills and to support solutions journalism projects with SJN's accreditation and support. Deadline is September 23. 

Poynter is taking applications for its Leadership Academy for Women in Media (March 2025). Designed for female and nonbinary journalists, this program will support you to strengthen your managerial strengths; navigate ethical decision making; strategize ahead of difficult conversations and negotiations; manage up, down and across generations; delegate with purpose; and create your unique formula for work-life chemistry. Deadline is December 6.

Events

September 23-25 (Chicago): LMA Fest is a conference hosted by The Local Media Association. The program highlights strategies for philanthropy, AI, digital sales, first-party data. 

September 24 (Online): Communities of color have been the focal point of elections and campaigns, especially over the past few years. But because of a history of media harming those communities, many lack trust in news outlets. Join Election SOS’ Massarah Mikati in conversation with Brandon Tensley, Capital B political reporter, Kavitha Rajagopalan, CCM’s Asian Media Initiative Director, and Vanessa Maria Graber, News Voices Director at Free Press, as we explore how you can authentically reach and engage these communities with your election coverage, and make your reporting relevant to their daily lives.

September 30 (NYC): Learn the latest on covering crucial campaign finance issues in the ‘Going Deep on Covering Campaign Finance’ workshop, co-led by OpenSecrets, a leading journalism nonprofit focused on covering federal and state campaign finance issues as well as following dark money, and Newmark J-School’s Andy Lehren, director of the investigative program. The event is co-sponsored by CCM.

October 1 (NYC): ‘On the Skirmish Line’ is part of a three-part series on Covering Elections from the Journalism Protection Initiative at the Newmark J-School, co-sponsored by CCM. The event will focus on press freedom and the safety challenges of reporting on the U.S. election. This panel discussion will open with a presentation from Gabe Rottman, director of RCFP’s Press Freedom and Technology Project, on a settlement agreement with the NYPD and a soon-to-be-released report from the Justice Department that could strengthen protections for the press. The conversation — moderated by Joel Simon, founding director of the Journalism Protection Initiative — will also cover the evolving climate for protest coverage from a legal and practical perspective. 

October 3 (online): In partnership with the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services and the Risk Less. Do More. campaign, this free, 90-minute Poynter webinar featuring CNN Chief Medical Correspondent Dr. Sanjay Gupta will help journalists debunk false narratives about vaccines and respiratory illnesses, find out about the common falsehoods that experts are tracking, and access reliable data and legitimate information about vaccination rates and trends in the communities journalists cover. 

October 3 - November 1 (Online): The Online News Association is holding a series of training sessions this fall for journalists interested in using AI tools. Upcoming events include case studies featuring newsrooms that have implemented AI into their workflow, and “AI, Copyright and Legal Considerations for Journalists, Freelancers and News Organizations.”

October 15 (NYC): “Can We Stop Online Harassment?” is co-hosted by Newmark J-School and Freedom of Press Foundation. Campaigns of online harassment have become a key election tool, used to intimidate and punish critical reporters. This session, featuring a panel of experts and journalists, will look at how the strategies and tactics of self defense have evolved, and what journalists can do in this shifting landscape to protect themselves online. 

October 23 (Online): The Local Media Association is hosting a webinar on 10 things to do to ensure your search traffic grows, presented by Arkin Consulting.
