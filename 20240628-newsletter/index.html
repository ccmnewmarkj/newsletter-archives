News from Inside the Community Media Sector

A big congratulations to Sewell Chan, who will be the new Executive Editor of the Columbia Journalism Review! Chan has been leading the Texas Tribune for the past three years. 

Reuters Institute for the Study of Journalism released its 2024 Digital News Report. The Guardian also created an unofficial GPT that will answer questions about the report, per American Press Institute.

An update from Poynter about The Marion County Record, the small-town newspaper that drew national support following a police raid. After the raid the Kansas newspaper faced lawsuits, had higher circulation, but also mourned the death of the publisher's 98-year-old mother following a police raid of her home. 

The Local Media Association published perspectives on Juneteenth from Black media leaders,

Nieman Lab reports on whether journalism’s trust problem is about money, not politics.

Better News shares ways The News Reporter (Columbus County, NC) generated new digital revenue through email newsletters. 

Better News also reported on products serving local voters that you can learn from for your 2024 election coverage.

The Atlantic detailed the management crisis The Washington Post has been experiencing of late. 

Yet another media company - in this case, Time - has struck a deal with OpenAI, Axios reports. 

California lawmakers advance tax on Big Tech to help fund news industry, per The Los Angeles Times.

Nieman Lab ran tests and found that ChatGPT is hallucinating fake links to its news partners’ biggest investigations.

As Poynter reports, L.A. Taco was forced to furlough its staff. Reader support brought them back within 24 hours.

The American Prospect spoke with Josh Marshall about how he has sustained Talking Points Memo amid the wreckage of modern journalism.

Fellowships & Opportunities

The New York Times Local Investigations Fellowship is now accepting applications. This one-year investigative reporting fellowship is aimed at developing the next generation of great reporters who will do accountability journalism at the local level. The NYT is looking for fellows who have a few years of professional experience covering a local beat and who are ready to tackle an ambitious investigative project but lack the resources and guidance to produce it. Under the guidance of Executive Editor Dean Baquet and his handpicked group of veteran editors, these fellows will receive the rare opportunity to learn the judgment, skills and techniques needed to excel at investigative journalism from the best in the business. The fellows will spend a year, funded by The New York Times, producing signature investigative work focused on their state or region and publishing it in The Times. Deadline is September 3.

Events

July 9-13 (Los Angeles): The National Association of Hispanic Journalists is hosting its annual conference and expo with panels, workshops, and mixers. There will be a full day dedicated to a climate and environment workshop. 

July 10 (Wichita, KS): Hosted by Wichita Professional Communicators, ‘Seized but Not Silenced’ is an event with Marion County Record publisher Eric Meyer. The small-town newspaper made headlines after its offices were illegally raided last summer and one of its co-owners died after police also raided her home. “Governmental secrecy and weaponization of law enforcement to stifle political opposition are among the biggest challenges democracy faces in an era of fractious polarization and political populism from both the right and the left. And, how the city has handled the case publicly is an example of precisely what not to do,” according to organizers.

July 16  (Minneapolis): The East Town Business Partnership is hosting a panel: Journalism in the Digital Age. Panelists from local media outlets like Sahan Journal, Racket MN, and Downtown Voices will discuss these outlets' abilities to leverage technology in an accessible and responsive manner within the Minneapolis communities they serve. 

July 18 (Redmond, OR): The Fund for Oregon Rural Journalism is hosting the Oregon Rural Journalism Conference. Sessions include interviewing help, searching for public records, an election coverage insights, and more. 

July 31 - August 4 (Chicago): The National Association of Black Journalists is hosting its annual convention and career fair with panels, workshops, and mixers. Proposals for sessions, moderators, and panelists are open as well. 

August 7-11 (Austin, TX): The Asian American Journalist Association is hosting its annual conference with panels, workshops, and mixers. The theme this year is ‘Meeting the Moment’
