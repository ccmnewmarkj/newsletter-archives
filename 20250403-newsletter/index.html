News 


CCM’s Asian Media Initiative Director Kavitha Rajagopalan wrote about the sense of belonging AAPI media tries to foster and the threats - both existential and structural - these outlets face, for The Emancipator. 

The GroundTruth Project will pivot from editorial content to focusing even more on helping sustain local news through its Report for America initiative, which places journalists in local newsrooms with funding, after the departure of founder Charles Sennott. 

ProPublica discussed its strategy of using a truck with a billboard for developing more sources in the federal government, per Nieman Lab. 

The Philadelphia Inquirer closed its community news desk, which was established three years ago as an initiative independent of the rest of the newsroom to better engage with the city’s traditionally underserved communities. The elimination appeared to be part of a cost-cutting measure, Poynter reported. 

The Sopris Sun and its Spanish-language sister publication Sol del Valle, both serving communities in Colorado, shared with the Local Media Association how it received nearly $380,000 in grants and formed a partnership with Aspen Daily News in one year. 

Six new affiliates have joined the News is Out collaborative, a group of news outlets across the country amplifying news from and about the LGBTQ+ community. The latest affiliates cover community news in Ohio, Michigan, Arizona, Florida, and the Carolinas. 

The U.S. House of Representatives held a hearing under the Delivering on Government Efficiency (DOGE) subcommittee last week called “Anti-American Airwaves: Holding the heads of NPR and PBS Accountable.” Poynter reported about the testimonies of executives from the two public networks as well as the Heritage Foundation, which issued the Project 2025 report that included a call to defund the Corporation for Public Broadcasting. The hearing could determine federal funding for the public networks, their affiliates, and, subsequently, may impact  any partnerships with community media outlets in the affiliates’ coverage areas. ​​

Fellowships & Opportunities

Colorado Public Radio is accepting applications for the Max Wycisk Digital News Fellow. Fellows will learn about attracting, earning trust of, and developing relationships with audiences They will produce clean and confidence-inspiring digital content on multiple platforms. The fellowship is specifically designed to support recent graduates and early career journalists, and folks looking to pivot into this field. CPR suggests applying before April 9. 

The Center for Health Journalism at the University of Southern California is accepting applications for its National Fellowship. The program helps journalists and their newsrooms report deeply and authoritatively on the health, welfare and well-being of children, families and communities. The fellowship includes an in-person session, five months of guidance on a reporting project, and a grant. The deadline to apply is April 9. 

The International Women’s Media Foundation hosts the Elizabeth Neuffer Fellowship that provides academic and professional opportunities to women journalists covering human rights and social justice. Fellows complete research and coursework at MIT’s Center for International Studies and participate in internships with media outlets, with a stipend. The deadline to apply is April 20. 

For early career journalists, the Robert Novak Journalism Fellowship at the Fund for American Studies is taking applications. Fellows can pursue projects requiring deep reporting they otherwise would not be able to in the year-long program which also provides a grant for income and project costs. The deadline to apply is April 27. 

Apply to be a Visiting Journalist at the Russell Sage Foundation. Journalists have the opportunity to do a reporting project for up to four months alongside scholars at the foundation about any topic falling under RSF’s “mission to "improve the social and living conditions in the United States." The deadline to apply is May 6.

Events

March 23 - April 6 (Online): This course from the Knight Center will teach journalists, editors and other writers about Writing Tight and Editing Tighter: How to Keep Your Articles Short Enough to Get Read.

April 8-9 (Nashville): The American Press Institute hosts the Local News Summit on Local Identity, History and Sustainability to explore how community-based news organizations can leverage local identity, geography and history to best contribute to a community’s well-being. This is an invitation-based event. 

April 14 - May 11 (Online): The Knight Center hosts a free, four-week training session, Prompt Engineering for Journalists, with Joe Amditis, an expert in AI and journalism at Montclair University. 

May 15-16 (Denver): The Center for Cooperative Media at Montclair State University is hosting the 2025 Collaborative Journalism Summit. The Summit is still open to session pitches and will feature two days of lightning talks, workshops, and participatory discussions about a wide array of topics and projects.

May 19-20 (Detroit): Online News Association (ONA) and Newsroom Robots host the AI Leadership Summit to gather leaders and executives to discuss how AI has impacted the news industry and newsroom leadership and culture in outlets of all sizes. 

May 29 - June 1 (Los Angeles):  The Association of Health Care Journalists hosts Health Journalism 2025, a four-day conference of field trips, sessions, and workshops. Journalists will be joined by health care professionals, policymakers, and other experts to tackle the most pressing issues facing health care, public health, and news coverage. 

September 3-5 (St. Louis): Local Independent Online News (LION) Publishers hosts the Independent News Sustainability Summit, a national conference that will gather independent news publishers, industry experts, and supporters of independent news to share expertise, lessons learned, and actionable advice on running a news business for solopreneurs and others.
