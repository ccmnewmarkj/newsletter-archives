CCM Highlights 

The Lenfest Institute for Journalism’s 2025 Beyond Print Grant Program will award up to $400,000 in total funding to help independent local newspapers transition from print to sustainable digital operations. Grants of up to $50,000 per organization will support projects that advance digital transformation, diversify revenue, and engage younger audiences through new platforms like podcasts, newsletters, and social media. CCM is excited to partner with Lenfest to co-host an information session on October 30 which will include a briefing on the program, a demonstration of the Beyond Print online diagnostic and toolkit, and an introduction to Lenfest’s many free resources for publishers. 

The program is focused on print and print-first outlets. Newsrooms with a regularly published print product and annual revenues of at least $500,000 are eligible to apply for the grant. Eligible projects must align with one or more focus areas—ranging from digital product development and customer engagement to newsroom workflow modernization and event-based revenue. Grantees will share their results in the Beyond Print Toolkit, contributing insights and best practices for the broader local news industry.

Community Media Minute

What's happening in the community media world

Deep South Today, a nonprofit network encompassing Mississippi Today, Verite News in New Orleans, and The Current in Lafayette, Louisiana, has announced plans to launch a regional investigative reporting center with The New York Times. This initiative is a collaboration with NYT’s  Local Investigations Fellowship and will include a lead investigative editor, full-time investigative and data reporters, and fellows from The Times. The project also receives support from Big Local News, a Stanford University program that provides journalists with data, tools, and collaborative opportunities. By co-publishing stories with The New York Times and offering content to other local news organizations, the initiative seeks to expand the reach and impact of accountability reporting. Smaller newsrooms can tackle complex issues and ambitious reporting projects with collaboration rather than competition, but national outlets can also benefit with an expanded audience and more engagement. 

Northwestern University’s Local News Initiative published the 2025 State of Local News Report, tracking roughly 8,000 news outlets from around the country. The report revealed that nearly 40% of U.S. local newspapers have disappeared since 2005, leaving 50 million Americans – mostly in rural areas – with little or no access to reliable local reporting. While there has been some growth in digital startups and public broadcasters, however, news deserts have expanded to more than 200 counties. Philanthropic funding and state-level support for local outlets has increased, especially with large-scale commitments from Press Forward and programs like CCM’s Advertising Boost Initiative. However, per the report, funding patterns remain uneven and often bypass rural and lower-income communities. For community media publishers, this report underscores both a crisis and an opportunity. As traditional outlets vanish and public broadcasters lose federal funding, community-based and independent publishers have become the last, and often only, trusted news providers for millions of Americans. The findings highlight the urgent need for sustainable business models, local collaborations, and investment in underserved regions.

One Good Read

Useful studies and reports to include in your week's reading

Press Forward has launched two toolkits – one for newsrooms and one for funders. The ‘Words That Work’ newsroom toolkit is designed to help local news organizations clearly communicate their value to audiences, funders, and stakeholders. Developed through national surveys, focus groups, and public message testing, the toolkits’ goal is to help publishers move from  simply believing in the importance of their work – to effectively and persuasively communicating it.  

The toolkits offer practical, research-backed tools for building both audience engagement and financial sustainability. It provides adaptable messaging, guidance on how to balance community connection with editorial independence, and tested language for explaining journalism’s role in civic life. By using this toolkit, local outlets can more effectively demonstrate their impact, foster philanthropic investment, and increase subscriptions. 

Pro Tip

A tip or tool for your newsroom

The Science Reporting Navigator, created by The Open Notebook and the Reynolds Journalism Institute, is an interactive toolkit designed to help community and local journalists confidently integrate science into their everyday reporting. Whether covering wildfires, health concerns, or local policy, reporters often need to interpret scientific data, explain research, and combat misinformation—especially during crises when accurate, contextual information can save lives. This resource makes science accessible for journalists without specialized backgrounds by offering practical lessons, templates, and guides on reading scientific papers, interviewing experts, and analyzing data. For community media journalists, the Navigator is especially important because it strengthens local coverage, empowers reporters to ground their stories in evidence, and helps build public trust through nuanced, fact-based storytelling that serves the needs of their audiences.

Fellowships & Opportunities

ProPublica is taking applications for its Photojournalism Fellowship. The NYC-based, two-year program is open to anyone with at least three years of professional photojournalism experience, as a staffer or freelancer. The Fellow will report to the senior editor of visual storytelling and work most closely with visual editors and reporters.The deadline to apply is December 1. 

The National Press Foundation is accepting applications for its Widening the Pipeline Fellowship, which focuses on helping journalists of color with seven or less years of experience stay in and lead newsrooms around the country. The yearlong program involves virtual workshops, in-person training in Washington, DC, and one-on-one mentorship with experienced industry professionals. The deadline to apply is December 14.
