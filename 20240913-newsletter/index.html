With just under two months to go, CCM helps community media boost elections coverage

This year, CCM continues its long history of supporting community media in its function of covering electoral politics in a way that directly serves the communities they represent and inform.  

We were excited last month to launch a crucial National Elections Reporting Grant program for community media outlets across the country to help bolster their 2024 elections coverage, a complement to our Elections Reporting Mentorship in New York and our other partnerships which include Knight Election Hub, Sunlight Search and Chip50. 

This election is not only historic for having the first female, Black and South Asian candidate running for president, but also because of the diverse set of candidates across the country in state and local elections. Voter engagement, particularly for voters of color, has never been more important for candidates and the health of American democracy. 

Community media outlets and journalists produce stories that directly relate to and serve their geographic and ethnic communities in a way larger media outlets will not and cannot when it comes to elections, especially. Community media is community engagement, simply put and as a result is an under-utilized tool to understand voters’ concerns, patterns, and feelings in an election year. 

With help from Knight Election Hub, we’ve connected outlets to resources that can help their reporting capacity. For instance, in our Reporting in an Age of Conflict workshop we shared how journalists can learn from conflict mediators on how to report on deeply polarizing topics, common in the political landscape of the 2024 elections. Our webinar on Appealing to Young Black Voters focused on sharing insights about how to improve reporting and better engage with the newest generation of voters who often get their information from social media rather than traditional news outlets.

As presidential candidates battle it out for voters’ confidence across the country and social media influencers produce event, rally, issue coverage that is so specific to their audiences, it’s important to keep in mind the saying 'all politics is local.’ It has never been more important than when talking about politics reporting from a hyperlocal and community-specific lens in this election year. We could not be prouder to have partnered with such  a diverse set of print, television and digital community media outlets across the country to produce essential journalism through our national program: Al-Bustan News, Atlanta Voice, BridgeDetroit, Khabar Magazine, Latino Press, NotiVisión Georgia, Nyob Zoo Milwaukee TV, The Arab American News, and The Philippine Times of Southern Nevada.

Program participants are working with our Black Media Initiative Director Cheryl Thompson-Morton, Latino Media Initiative Director Jesenia De Moya Correa, and Asian Media Initiative Director Kavitha Rajagopalan. They are also assigned an external editor: Cristian Arroyo Santiago, Farah Saafan, Fiza Pirani, Leezel Tanglao and Taylor Eldridge. “The editorial consultants provide added support for these newsrooms, ensuring they have the resources they need to cover the election for their community with more depth and impact. Their expertise helps empower newsrooms to better inform and engage their communities during critical election periods,” Thompson-Morton said.

These newsrooms received grant funding, training on safety issues in elections and political rally reporting, how to spot and report on campaign finance-related stories, and polling stories, as well as other support for their elections coverage.

The national program follows our New York metro area Elections Reporting Mentorship. Sixteen seasoned community media journalists were mentored, edited, and trained for three months and produced a total of 48 stories across outlets in English, Spanish, Arabic, Chinese, and Nepali. The outlets serve communities from the Bronx to Long Island and published a wealth of coverage about the youth vote in northern Manhattan, a first-time voters’ guide for new U.S. citizens, how home country politics affects voting in American elections, the impact of Black sororities and fraternities on VP Kamala Harris’ campaign, the rights of jailed voters, and much more. The fellows also participated in training sessions led by experts on elections reporting basics, the ethics of campaign advertising while reporting on local candidates, and fact-checking. 

CCM has also been producing newsletter issues for the last year covering various election-related topics from tips on how to improve coverage beyond just the ‘horse race’ to the growing problem of misinformation.
