CCM Highlights 

CCM is excited to partner with Newmark J-School’s Journalism Protection Initiative for “Is the US Still a Safe Haven for Journalists Under Threat?”, an off-the-record conversation with journalists from across the world about their experiences in the U.S. They will discuss navigating growing anti-immigrant rhetoric and increasing restrictions on U.S. visas for fellowships and temporary stays. The visas have historically provided a refuge for journalists coming from countries with less press freedom. 

  • Gustavo Gorriti – Investigative journalist from Peru, director of IDL-Reporteros, known for exposing government corruption; currently a Knight Press Freedom Fellow.
  • Yasmine El Rashidi – Egyptian journalist and writer (joining via Zoom from Cairo), contributing writer, New York Review of Books
  • Baktygul Chynybaeva – journalist from Kyrgyzstan, specializing in human rights, and former correspondent with Radio Free Europe/Radio Liberty
  • Mikhail Zygar – journalist, historian, and best-selling author, known for his work on Russian politics, propaganda, and authoritarianism
  • Moderated by Joel Simon – Executive Director of the Journalism Protection Initiative

Taking place on Oct. 14, this in-person event is the second of JPI’s Fall 2025 Safety Series, a three-part program co-sponsored by CCM, which explores the biggest threats to media safety and press freedom in the U.S.

Community Media Minute

What's happening in the community media world

After four of its journalists were shot with pepper spray bullets and tear-gassed while covering protests outside the Broadview Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) facility, Block Club Chicago - joined by Chicago Headline Club, the Illinois Press Association, and others - has filed a lawsuit against the federal government. A U.S. district judge heard arguments from both sides at an emergency hearing on October 6 and is expected to issue a ruling later this week. Block Club reported their lawyers are requesting a court order to stop ICE agents from  “indiscriminately using chemical weapons against journalists, asserting their First Amendment right to report.”

The Pittsburgh Downtown Media Hub, funded by the Benter Foundation, is a subsidized office space to house seven hyperlocal media outlets. The model demonstrates that foundations can support journalism beyond monetary grants by offering physical infrastructure, professional amenities like podcast studios, and opportunities for knowledge-sharing that individual newsrooms couldn't afford independently, Poynter reported. Since opening in late 2022, the Hub has not only helped sustain small news operations but has also done a small part to revitalize  downtown Pittsburgh.

One Good Read

Useful studies and reports to include in your week's reading

Over the past 20 years, more than 3,200 local American newspapers have disappeared, creating news deserts without reliable news coverage, which then undermines the information infrastructure that supports democratic participation. This report from the Center for Innovation & Sustainability in Local Media at the University of North Carolina-Chapel Hill Hussman School of Journalism and Media examines the distribution of foundation and nonprofit donor resources across the U.S. alongside where news deserts are located, revealing where regional funding could be activated and where significant gaps remain. The research focuses on available resources rather than actual giving patterns, showing what could be possible rather than what currently exists. This paper offers a practical blueprint for understanding the philanthropic landscape in your region and how your situation compares to other communities nationwide. This information is critical for developing effective fundraising approaches, forming partnerships, and determining whether to focus on local donors or seek external support through national programs and policy advocacy. 

Pro Tip

A tip or tool for your newsroom

The Journalist’s Resource, a digital publication based at Harvard Kennedy School's Shorenstein Center for informing journalists, offers useful advice on how to report on research studies. First, a list of 13 questions reporters and editors can ask themselves to take a critical look at studies, even if they are not familiar with that subject matter. This could be particularly useful for political reporting, when  elected officials cite a study to explain their stance or vote on a bill. There are also links to reputable professional organizations that help reporters verify the legitimacy of the study in question.  

The Journalist's Resource also gives timely guidance and a word of caution regarding AI-generated summaries of studies, which time-crunched newsrooms may lean on for support in interpreting research findings. Scholars weigh in on why the over-generalizations produced by the summaries could be harmful and erode trust between a newsroom and its audience. Finally, they offer tips to help avoid blanket statements: for example, pay attention to a study's sample size, and do not assume findings apply beyond the study's research scope.

Additional Training Opportunities

October 14 and 21 (Online): The Reynolds Journalism Institute will host Report Like an Anthropologist, a two-part training series on how small newsrooms can use anthropological tools like ethnographic interviewing and semiotic mapping to do better journalism. Participants will walk away with a workbook and continued access to the trainers for ongoing help in using the tactics and tools discussed. 

October 15 (Online): The Online News Association will host a training on how newsrooms can use AI tools to transform editorial and commercial workflows. Topics covered by audience and revenue strategy staff from Thomson Reuters will include: how to analyze newsletter data, forecast content performance, and more effectively support business and editorial functions. 

October 16 (Online): Local Media Association will host a webinar on what a publisher needs today to succeed. Some of the topics covered include: the tech and tools publishers may need; how to effectively employ content management systems, billing, accounting systems; and business models and infrastructure publishers could replicate. 

October 16 (Online): The Chronicle of Philanthropy, a publication that covers the business and government policies of the fundraising and development world, and The Lenfest Institute will host a session on donor trends and how to apply insights from the philanthropic field to fundraising for media outlets and projects. Attendees will also receive a discount on a subscription to the publication. 

November 5 (Online): The Online News Association will host a training on how journalists can create impactful social media videos solo, presented by Julia Munslow, senior editor at The Wall Street Journal in charge of social video strategy on LinkedIn, Instagram, and TikTok. Munslow will cover how to script and film videos and give real-time feedback about on-camera presence. Participants will leave the session with a piece of mock content to take back to their news outlets. This training is ideal for community media journalists running small or solo operations.
