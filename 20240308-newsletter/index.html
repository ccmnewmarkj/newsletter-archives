News from Inside the Community Media Sector

Congratulations to environmental news outlet Grist for adding its fourth regional reporting partnership, this time with Blue Ridge Public Radio to provide coverage on climate change and other pressing environmental stories from and about Appalachia in the Blue Ridge Mountain region.

We’re excited to see what is happening in the Midwest now that Press Forward Minnesota – a local chapter of the national initiative to infuse local media with $500 million over the next few years – – and one of its main funders, the Midwest-based Joyce Foundation, has partnered with The Pivot Fund. Having done similar work in Georgia, TPF hopes to replicate its efforts in Minnesota, Michigan, and Illinois to “map the “emerging news landscape” of information sources trusted by communities of color,” Nieman Lab reports.

Rebuild Local News has published a toolkit of best practices for implementing a tax credit or grant program that supports small business advertising in local news outlets. This toolkit includes a “model bill” which communities can work from to implement local legislation that could provide a tax incentive to advertise in community media, in turn providing a more stable policy environment to sustain news outlets through ad revenue. 

For International Women’s Day 2024, Women Press Freedom and the Coalition for Women in Journalism brought together a panel to discuss the United Nations’ #InvestinWomen campaign, the nuances of investments in journalism during a rash of layoffs, and the rise of AI. 

Better News is looking into how The Fayetteville Observer’s engagement work is giving Black professional women a chance to shine.

THE CITY asked AI to map its stories across New York City in an effort to ensure the news outlet is truly covering all parts of the city and to identify gaps in its coverage. 

In light of recent news from Meta, which owns Facebook, Instagram, and Threads, that it will not be promoting what the company determines is “political news content,” LinkedIn doubles down on news as social rivals retreat, Axios reports. 

Native News Online published its second annual impact report to illustrate its growth and impact as “a digital news organization in Indian Country.”

The American Press Institute has expanded its collaboration with the Associated Press to bring a monthly series of webinars geared towards local and community media leaders in the run up to the 2024 elections. The seven-part webinar series will run on the last Thursday of each month from March through September. Stay tuned to our newsletter’s Events section (below) for the links to each webinar and our own series of newsletter issues exploring various topics regarding election coverage (you can read our first three in the series here, here, here).

On a related note, Columbia Journalism Review offers a piece on how the collapse of local newsrooms made all politics national.

An op-ed in Poynter: Why the Report for America program, which places journalists in local newsroom jobs across the country, will no longer place corps members in hedge fund-owned publications. 

A piece from the Center for Journalism Ethics at the University of Wisconsin-Madison argues that public funding in local journalism is no silver bullet. But, some industry leaders argue it’s necessary.

In the wake of the Los Angeles Times’ Latino Community Desk being victim to mass layoffs, conversations about coverage of the Latinx community are once again at the forefront. (You can follow and read a bit more about it from our Latino Media Initiative Director Jesenia De Moya Correa and her recommendations on where to go for news in Southern California.) Better News took a look at how local news organizations are improving coverage for Latinx communities across the country. 

Nieman Lab reports on how Black women get their political news matters for this election.

Fellowships & Opportunities

The Newmark J-School’s McGraw Center for Business Journalism is now accepting applications for its McGraw Fellowship for Business Journalism. The fellowships provide up to $15,000 and editorial support for experienced journalists to produce deeply-reported investigative or enterprise stories on critical economic, financial, and business topics. Previous McGraw Fellows have explored a wide variety of issues – and you don't need to be a business reporter to apply. Many have been generalists, or cover areas such as health care, inequality, or the environment. Journalists from diverse backgrounds are strongly encouraged to apply. The Fellowship is open to both freelance and staff journalists in all forms of media with at least five years’ professional experience. For further information and examples of previous Fellows’ work, go to mcgrawcenter.org or contact the Center at mcgrawcenter@journalism.cuny.edu. The deadline for the spring fellowship is March 31. 

Newmark J-School is also taking applications for its Executive Program in News Innovation and Leadership. This year-long certificate program — which runs from September 2024 to June 2025 — invites global media leaders with backgrounds in editorial, product, business and technology from local, national, and international news organizations to apply. The program operates on a hybrid model, allowing participants to engage in virtual sessions while convening three times a year for in-person residencies in New York City. Akin to an MBA, the curriculum imparts holistic leadership, business, and management training to senior-level journalists and media managers. Deadline is May 3.

ProPublica is now taking applications for its Investigative Editor Training Program. This year’s program will begin in June 2024 with a weeklong boot camp in New York that will include courses and panel discussions on how to conceive of and produce investigative projects that expose harm and have impact. The editors will also get training in how to manage reporters who are working with data, documents and sensitive sources, including whistleblowers, agency insiders and people who have suffered trauma. The program continues with a yearlong mentorship pairing and virtual continuing education sessions. Deadline is March 11. 

The Pulitzer Center is now accepting applications for its new StoryReach U.S Fellowships. The yearlong Fellowship opportunity is a chance for forward-thinking journalists and local and regional newsrooms to collaborate with peers and the Center’s staff to unlock the full potential of storytelling and innovative audience reach. Deadline is March 18. 

Mother Jones is accepting applications for its Ben Bagdikian Fellowship Program, which offers a crash course in investigative journalism. Fellows at Mother Jones dive deep into every aspect of a national multimedia outfit—covering breaking news, assisting and conducting investigations, ensuring accuracy and impact, and seeing from the inside how awards are won. Deadline is March 24. 

In collaboration with News Corp and The Wall Street Journal, the International Center for Journalists is offering journalists from around the world an opportunity to sharpen their digital journalism skills through its Fellowship for Digital Innovation. The program supports developing data- and/or digitally-driven projects. The 2024 program includes digital innovation training, grant funding, and newsroom placement with WSJ. Deadline is March 24. 

University of Southern California’s Center for Health Journalism is accepting applications for its National Fellowship, which helps journalists and their newsrooms report deeply and authoritatively on the health, welfare, and well-being of children, families, and communities. The program prepares fellows to cover a major enterprise health or social well-being reporting project in the months that follow the initial week of intensive learning in Los Angeles. Deadline is April 10. 

Southern California journalists who have been affected by layoffs are welcome to apply for employment support through the Asian American Journalists Association - LA Chapter’s Randy Hagihara Uplift Fund. Deadline is June 2.

Events

March 10 (Los Angeles): As part of the the 14th Annual Conference for the Consortium of Universities for Global Health (CUGH), the Pulitzer Center, Johns Hopkins Bloomberg School of Public Health, and Global Health Now are hosting a Communications Workshop. The event is open to the public. Journalists will discuss their reporting methods, working with sources, and how experts in scientific fields can better communicate their research to the public. Global health experts will share their experiences in working with journalists and how they make their research accessible to a range of audiences.

March 12 (Online): Pulitzer Center is hosting a webinar with journalists on how to use ocean data for reporting. Ocean data is diverse and can range from information about vessels, cargo, currents, and biodiversity, to scientific databases about salinity and pollution levels.

March 14 (Online): CCM’s Black Media Initiative will be hosting the second annual Black Media Futures conference, a half-day virtual event in commemoration of the 197th anniversary of the establishment of the Black press. Building on last year’s conversations, the conference will delve into the present challenges and future prospects of Black media.

March 21 (Online): CCM is hosting a workshop that will delve into the process of applying for New York City press credentials. It will also explain the changes introduced by Local Law 46 of 2021 which transferred the responsibility of issuing press credentials from the NYPD to the Mayor’s Office of Media and Entertainment (MOME) and established the Press Credentials Office (PCO).

March 25 (NYC): Columbia Journalism School is hosting the Ida B. Wells Symposium. This half-day symposium will feature journalists, educators, and scholars who embody Ida's courage, tenacity, and fight for justice and equality.

March 28 (Online): American Press Institute and Associated Press are hosting a webinar regarding threats in the 2024 elections. AP reporters will talk about tackling misinformation, the use of artificial intelligence to mislead voters, the effects of conspiracy theories on local elections, security vulnerabilities and foreign interference, and threats faced by election workers.
