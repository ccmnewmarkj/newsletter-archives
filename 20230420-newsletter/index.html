Our Black and Asian Media Initiatives collaborate

A group of people hold each other

We are proud to announce the first collaboration between CCM’s Black Media Initiative, led by Cheryl Thompson-Morton, and our Asian Media Initiative, led by Kavitha Rajagopalan, on an important topic for all community media: reporting on gender-based violence in the communities. 

It is a shared challenge in both communities and we hope through this collaboration to start a dialogue on not just intersecting responses via community media but also possible shared or overlapping solutions. 

On May 3, the BMI and AMI will co-host a news briefing for community media journalists covering Asian and Black communities with expert advocates serving Black and Asian American survivors of gender-based violence. Speakers include Rebekah Duvall-Taylor, Executive Director, Black Women Lead: Black Femme Fund; Jeehae Fischer, Executive Director, Korean American Family Service Center; Yasmeen Hamza, CEO, Womankind; Zainab Muzaffar, Senior Associate Director of Communications and Development, Sakhi for South Asian Women; and Shenikqwa Phillip, Program Manager, Black Women in Motion. 

In the fall, ahead of Domestic Violence Awareness Month, the collaboration will publish a resource guide with tipsheets, reporting tools, recent research and data, expert interview sources, and will announce joint training opportunities for Asian American and Black community media journalists.

You can read more about the collaboration here as well as register for this free webinar.

Stories from our Small Business Fellows

Since February, our newest fellowship cohort of 16 community media journalists in the New York City metro region has been reporting on local, community-owned businesses’ challenges, successes, and lessons learned three years after the global pandemic began. The program has been funded by the New York City Mayor’s Office of Media and Entertainment. 

The fellows serve Spanish and Russian speakers; and South Asian, African, Filipino and Caribbean communities. They cover parts of Long Island and northern New Jersey, and NYC neighborhoods including Bushwick and Ridgewood, Jackson Heights and Parkchester.

Some of the published pieces include a look at the hard road faced by New York City taxi medallion owners and drivers by Fellow Sujeet Rajan for the India Overseas Report. Fellow Ricky Rillera wrote about a catering service and a funeral home owned and operated by members of the Filipino community in Queens for the Philippine Daily Mirror. Fellow Parveen Chopra described the struggles for four South Asian beauty parlors to stay open during the pandemic in Hicksville, Long Island for The South Asian Times. Fellow Anna Bradley-Smith documented for The Brownstoner the issues faced by a Brooklyn florist owned by a Mexican immigrant during the pandemic when government assistance was not easily accessible. Caribbean American Weekly reporter and Fellow Pearl Phillip profiled an entrepreneur with an incredible story of overcoming struggle. Stay tuned to our Twitter account for more of the Fellows’ work about small businesses and the pandemic’s continued impact on their communities. 

To see the first set of published stories, check out this map, follow us on Twitter and read more about the Fellowship.

Fellowships & Opportunities

High Country News is accepting applications for the Virginia Spencer Davis Fellowship, which is designed for early-career journalists interested in reporting on rural communities in the West, and how those communities are changing. It will provide on-the-ground reporting opportunities and a chance to dive deep into important stories, as well as professional mentorship and career development. Deadline is April 21. 

The Solutions Journalism Network is hosting the nation’s first HBCU Educator / Black Press Academy & LEDE Fellowship, focused on the power of solutions journalism to increase trust, engagement and equity in journalism. This fellowship will bring together members of the Black press, which has a deep legacy of response-focused reporting, and HBCU educators keen to sharpen their skills in solutions journalism. Deadline is April 24.

The Business of Fashion seeks applicants for the Black Journalists Fellowship, a six-month, part-time, paid reporting fellowship with the editorial team, designed to empower aspiring Black journalists. Deadline is April 28.

The National Center for the Preservation of Democracy at the Japanese American National Museum is accepting applications for the Irene Yamamoto Arts Writers Fellowship. This year’s inaugural fellowship provides two $5,000 unrestricted awards to promising writers of color who are focused on art criticism and/or reporting about the visual, performing, or media arts. The deadline is May 1.

The Solutions Journalism Network is building a cohort of U.S.-based, rural-serving newsrooms to receive six months of training and mentorship that supports the integration of solutions journalism into the newsroom workflow. Deadline is May 1. 

STAT is accepting applications for the Sharon Begley Science Reporting Fellowship, a one-year program intended for early-career U.S. journalists from racial and ethnic groups underrepresented in the profession and will prepare them for a successful career in science journalism. Deadline is May 1. 

Colorado Public Radio is accepting applications for the Max Wycisk News Fellow for Digital Audiences. Fellows will learn about attracting, earning trust of, and developing relationships with audiences They will produce clean and confidence-inspiring digital content on multiple platforms. The fellowship is specifically designed to support recent graduates and early career journalists, and folks looking to pivot into this field. Deadline is May 5. 

The 19th is recruiting for its 2023-2024 fellowship cohort of the Frances Ellen Watkins Harper Fellowship. Launched in partnership with Nikole Hannah-Jones, Howard University’s Knight chair in race and journalism, it will provide recent graduates, mid-career alums, and former students of Historically Black Colleges and Universities with full-year salaried and benefit-laden fellowships in the areas of reporting, editing, audience engagement and news product and technology. Deadline is May 15. 

Altavoz Lab is accepting applications for its 2023 Environmental Fellowship, designed to produce collaborative projects to strengthen bilingual (English and Spanish) reporters at community outlets that serve Black, Indigenous, immigrant and other communities of color in the U.S. with the goal of publishing stories that will enable local audiences to participate fully in democracy. Deadline is May 15.

Grist is accepting submissions for Imagine 2200: Write the future, its climate fiction short story contest. This is an invitation to writers from all over the globe to imagine a future in which solutions to the climate crisis flourish and help bring about radical improvements to our world. Deadline is June 13.
