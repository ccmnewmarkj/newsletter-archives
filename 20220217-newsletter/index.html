NYC Spends Even Bigger on Community Media in 2021

In 2021, New York City government agencies spent a record $15.6 million on advertising in more than 230 community media outlets serving Black, Latinx, Asian, immigrant, and neighborhood communities across all five boroughs. This represented an astounding 82% of the city’s total $19.1 million total spending on print and digital advertising, far greater than the 50% mandated in a 2019 mayoral executive order.

This week, CCM published an update to our December 2020 independent report, based on our independent analysis of city budget data. Read it here.

The mandate came into effect in 2020, delivering a desperately needed $10 million to a community media sector facing drastic losses in advertising revenue during the COVID-19 pandemic. A powerful corrective to the glaring inequity in how city agencies advertised, the executive order was so effective that dozens of publishers launched an open letter campaign, calling for it to be made into permanent law.

They were heard: in the summer of 2021, New York City became the first U.S. city to permanently commit at least 50% of city government advertising dollars to the most trusted news sources of its most vulnerable residents, and created New York’s first ever Mayor’s Office of Ethnic and Community Media.

Now, community media publishers have launched a second open letter campaign, urging New York’s governor and state legislators to follow suit, likewise committing state government agency advertising dollars to community media and passing proposed legislation to sustain local journalism.

Visit CCM’s Advertising Boost Initiative to learn more about our efforts to support community media in accessing essential government funding, and be sure to follow us on Twitter for updates.

Crisis at The Chicago Reader

Last week, news broke of a stalemate on the board of The Chicago Reader, threatening its transition to nonprofit status after many years of financial instability. Tracy Baim, The Reader’s co-publisher and founder of CCM regional partner Chicago Independent Media Alliance, has shepherded the nonprofit transition process and is currently facing challenges. Read a compelling oped about the crisis in today’s Sun-Times here, with good historical context, and read the statement from The Reader Union here.
