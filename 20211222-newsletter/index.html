2021 Year-End Lookback

In this second year since CCM’s relaunch and national expansion, we have been so gratified to be able to deepen our connections and impact with community media publishers and creators in New York and nationwide, and together with our regional partners. It’s been a tremendous year of learning and building, laying a strong foundation with new research, powerful conversations and impactful programming for another year of growing sustainability and visibility for community media nationwide in 2022. 

This year saw important expansions in both the Latino Media Initiative and Black Media Initiative (BMI) and efforts to create greater visibility for Asian media. Building on our 2020 report showing the need for cohesive Spanish-language style guidance on race and gender, we launched our original Guia Antirracista in our first public event of the year, in January. The 2021 Latino Media Summit brought together more than 800 people in a three-day celebration of innovation and creativity in journalism for and by Latinx communities. The bilingual master’s degree program – the first such program at a journalism program in the U.S. – welcomed a new director, Carmen Graciela Díaz. We continued to expand the Border Network, adding more than a dozen new outlets to the Border Media Map. 

Under the leadership of a new director, Cheryl Thompson-Morton, the BMI launched a groundbreaking new content analysis report showing exactly why Black Media Matters Now, more than ever. Following a months-long needs assessment with dozens of Black media publishers, the BMI Exchange Sessions kicked off a monthly community space and resource-sharing network, bringing together some 150 journalists working in Black media to discuss strategies on organizational models, external revenue streams, and audience revenue. We added some 50 outlets to the Black Media Map & Directory.

Following a moving town hall where Chinese American, Korean American, and Filipino American community media leaders shared how they were responding to hate crimes, CCM launched an eye-opening report in May on Asian Media on the Front Lines of pandemic, anti-Asian violence, and disinformation crises in their communities.

CCM’s new NYC media map is searchable by outlet name and type, community served, geographic reach, and language.

In New York City, community media leaders built on the success of the Advertising Boost Initiative with an open-letter campaign urging that the executive order mandating equitable city government agency advertising be made into permanent law – and in June 2021, the city council did just that, creating the country’s first ever office of ethnic and community media. We launched a new pilot, the City Elections Initiative, to explore election-related advertising as another sustainability pathway for community media, and to support community media in deepening and expanding their local elections coverage – with impressive results we shared in this report. We released a new interactive New York City media map to accompany our recently updated directory of New York City community media, Many Voices One City.

We hosted a three-part public Polarization Conversation Series, three candidate forums with 12 New York City mayoral candidates, briefings with public leaders and subject matter experts on climate displacement and redistricting, and five trainings open to all community-based journalists and editors on such topics as Newspack and intermediate SEO. We published 23 newsletter issues, showcasing news about and for community media. And we continued to collaborate with our regional partners at the National Federation of Community Broadcasters, Chicago Independent Media Alliance, and Media in Color to design new programming, replicate sustainability strategies, and develop shared visions for community media nationwide.
CCM is grateful to be in community with our incredible partners at National Federation of Community Broadcasters, Chicago Independent Media Alliance, and Media in Color.

We are excited for the new year ahead, for the many ways our partners and others are building out sustainability and training initiatives similar to ours in their own networks, and for the growing awareness of community media informing public policy initiatives to sustain local journalism. We thank you for being a part of our community, and for keeping us in yours.

Our newsletter will go on a short hiatus for the holidays, and will return in mid-January with a new format and new publication schedule. Stay tuned! Meantime, we at CCM wish you all a happy, safe holiday season and a happy new year!
News From Inside the Community Media Sector

MiC's Creators Lab Wraps 

Designed together with our California-based regional partner, Media in Color, and Newmark J-Schools Entrepreneurial Journalism Creators Program, The Creators Lab concluded its inaugural cohort last week. Over the course of 90 days, 16 founders of podcasts, newsletters, and other local media outlets serving communities of color across California participated in intensive workshops on a range of topics to help them build their businesses and grow their reach.
