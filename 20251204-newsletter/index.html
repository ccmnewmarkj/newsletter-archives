📰Community Media Minute🌎

What's happening in the community media world

☕🗒️In Camden, Maine, a new experiment in community journalism is underway: the Villager Cafe is helping subsidize Midcoast Villager, a weekly newspaper born when four local papers merged in 2024. Customers buying breakfast or a latte at the café are indirectly supporting the newsroom, which sits just upstairs. Staff can meet with readers in the café, building connections and trust. Since the café opened in April 2025, circulation revenue is already 40% higher than what all four papers generated on their own. The publisher says the goal is simple: if the community shows up for the café, the café can help keep the newsroom alive.

📖One Good Read📒

Useful studies and reports to include in your week's reading

📚🏘️ Journalism + Design Lab, a research initiative of The New School, published a Community News Roles framework that lays out the many ways people contribute to local information ecosystems beyond traditional reporting – from documenting neighborhood life to facilitating conversations, sharing reliable updates, and helping neighbors navigate resources. The project emphasizes that trusted community institutions like libraries, colleges, and grassroots organizations can train and support residents to take on these roles. Early findings show that this approach can broaden the reach and credibility of local news, especially in communities long underserved by legacy media.

🗣️Pro Tip🗒️

A tip or tool for your newsroom

🌎🩺The Lancet, a widely regarded, independent medical journal, published its 2025 Countdown report which indicated 2024 was the first year global temperatures exceeded 1.5°C above pre-industrial levels – and the health effects are already showing up. More than half of the 20 climate-and-health indicators it tracked had worsened year to year, with several reversing earlier progress. The Journalist’s Resource pulled out 11 story angles for local reporters, from heat risk for outdoor workers to gaps in hospital preparedness and unequal health impacts in low-income neighborhoods. It’s a ready-made roadmap for reporting climate change as a present-day public health issue instead of a distant threat.

🧑🏽‍🏫Fellowships & Opportunities✒️

Ohio University is accepting applications for its 2026 Kiplinger Fellowship. The focus will be on ‘Saving Local News: A Practical Guide’ and will be held at the Scripps College of Communication in Athens, Ohio, between May 11-14, 2026. The deadline to apply is December 7. 

The National Press Foundation is accepting applications for its Widening the Pipeline Fellowship, which helps journalists of color with seven years or less of experience stay in and lead newsrooms. The yearlong program involves virtual workshops, in-person training in Washington, D.C., and one-on-one mentorship with experienced industry professionals. The deadline to apply is December 14. 

The Sojourners Journalism Cohort is accepting applications. The five-month part-time program is open to writers from historically marginalized communities and offers hands-on, paid remote training as freelance journalists. The cohort will accept three news reporters and three opinion writers who will each write 3-5 faith and justice articles for sojo.net. The deadline is December 15. 

Columbia Journalism School is accepting applications for its Knight-Bagehot Fellowship. The program is open to experienced journalists and offers the opportunity to enhance their understanding of business, economics, finance, and technology. Fellows spend two semesters at the J-School and take most of their classes at Columbia Business School. The deadline is January 31. 

The Tarbell Fellowship is a one-year program for journalists interested in covering artificial intelligence. Fellows will participate in a paid, nine-month placement at a major newsroom from June 8, 2026 to May 31, 2027. Fellows will also take part in a 10-week course covering AI and journalism fundamentals and attend a weeklong journalism summit in the San Francisco Bay Area. The deadline to apply is January 7. 

Columbia University’s Spencer Fellowship for Education Reporting is accepting applications. The program is open to journalists and educators who want to develop and publish an ambitious long-form journalism project that advances public understanding of education. The fellowship combines study and research with advising from faculty experts at Columbia, Teachers College and elsewhere. The deadline is February 3.
