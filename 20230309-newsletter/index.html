Asian American collective begins an important conversation
On March 3-4, the Asian American Digital Politics Collective (AADPC) was convened by Dr. Jonathan Ong, Associate Professor of Global Digital Media at University of Massachusetts Amherst, bringing together experts in the fields of media studies and communications, Asian American studies, history, information sciences, and public policy, along with community advocates and community media journalists to examine and consider possible defenses against misinformation and disinformation in Asian American communities. CCM's Asian Media Initiative Director Kavitha Rajagopalan was invited to present preliminary findings from the in-progress research into the AAPI community media landscape.

She shared a preliminary list of community media outlets that the AMI research team has identified to include in its forthcoming national AAPI media map and directory. Serving 22 AAPI communities in 22 states and Washington, D.C., in 25 languages, these outlets represent a snapshot of the present AAPI media ecosystem. The research team at UMass Amherst is planning to monitor their social media accounts to assess how they navigate misinformation, disinformation, fake news, state propaganda, and polarizing rhetoric, and how equipped they may be to defend against divisive and false information.

Photo from Dr. Jonathan Ong
As Rajagopalan explained during the workshop, AAPI communities do not have a lack of news to report or resources to share, but what is really needed is “accurate, reliable, culturally relevant and in-language local journalism.”

However, that is more nuanced than it may seem. As she noted, “before we can draw any conclusions about the capacity or even willingness of AAPI-serving community media to defend against disinformation, we need to understand the ecosystem on a granular, state-by-state, community-by-community level.” 

Her presentation illustrated the landscape of Asian American community media across the U.S., based on preliminary findings and the first national Asian Media Map and Directory, established by CCM. Rajagopalan stressed the need to “understand the specific vulnerabilities, narrow funding structures, and resource depletion affecting AAPI media,” particularly local, independent, community-serving media organizations. 

That is a crucial gap CCM is looking to fill with its ongoing needs-based assessments and further developing the Map and Directory. Rajagopalan also shared CCM’s research methodologies in order to collaborate and refine them.  

Some of the initial findings she was able to share during the workshop included: 

  • While Asian Americans and Pacific Islanders remain underserved in the wider media ecosystem, many AAPI also remain underserved in the AAPI community media ecosystem as well
  • We need more and better data on how generational divides and language access have an impact on how engaged an outlet’s audience can be 
  • We need more research into what is stopping existing community media outlets from serving AAPI communities more fully, beyond funding 

As part of the effort to learn more about the Asian community media landscape, CCM will host a free workshop exclusively for members of the Asian Media Women's Network on April 12, on desktop public records investigative research. If any readers would like to be part of the Network, they can message Rajagopalan at kavitha.rajagopalan@journalism.cuny.edu.

News from Inside the Community Media Sector



Congratulations to Signal Ohio, for raising more than $5 million to launch a second newsroom in Akron, Ohio, joining a sister newsroom in Cleveland.

As a digital news start-up, the Border Belt Independent partners with local newspapers to provide them with long-form stories at no cost. It helps grow audiences and provides in-depth reporting. Here’s an interview with them to see how they provide these deep-dives for North Carolina newsrooms.

Black-owned newspaper publishers and consulting firm Deloitte are working together to launch The Exchange, a pilot project designed to co-create and distribute business content tailored for diverse audiences. The consortium of outlets partnering to produce the content includes five members of the Word In Black network. Ten other Black-owned outlets working through the Local Media Consortium and Local Media Association will distribute the content. 

The American Press Institute is hosting the Local News Summit on Opinion, Civic Discourse and Sustainability on April 12-13 in Austin, Texas. If you have ideas about or experience in how local opinion sections can build new products for diverse audiences, use community engagement to support civic discourse and expand its revenue opportunities, request an invitation for the remaining spots at the invite-only summit no later than Sunday, March 12. 

The LIFT Project’s Dr. Danielle Brown spoke with the American Press Institute about how her organization works to help newsrooms center communities, particularly marginalized people, and focus on the solutions the communities need rather than the legacy media approach of “fixing” the newsroom.  

Revolt’s Detavio Samuels says advertisers have fallen short on commitments to Black-owned media companies. Related to that, this episode of Roland Martin’s Unfiltered tackles that issue with a panel of Black advertising agency owners discussing the connection between corporations, majority-white advertising agencies, and the historical loss of revenue for Black-owned media.

Poynter deep-dives into the need for journalists to understand the power of community engagement to earn trust. 

In 2019, Meta (as Facebook) announced an ambitious $300 million project to fund “news programs, partnerships, and content” over the next three years. The Tow Center has compiled a database of all the Meta Journalism Project (formerly Facebook Journalism Project) initiatives publicly announced since 2018 that were both aimed at local news organizations and had a direct funding component to see if the company actually fulfilled its commitment. 

Something for community/local broadcasters to keep an eye on, Major League Baseball has hired three executives as it seeks to possibly take over local broadcasts of 17 teams after a few regional sports networks have faced financial challenges.

Fellowships & Opportunities

Elevate is the International Center for Journalists’ news business hub focused on helping C-level journalists who are running small and medium-sized media outlets develop their business skills so that their newsrooms can become sustainable and grow. Participants will have the opportunity to learn from corporate executives, technologists, journalism trainers, media investors, and a prominent university that specializes in entrepreneurship. You can register for the pre-Elevate sessions in March for a chance to move on to the next phase of training and mentorship. Deadline is ASAP. 

The Poynter-Koch Media & Journalism Fellowship is a year-long fellowship for early-career journalists who are aspiring future leaders in the industry. It runs from August to May, with weekly two-hour meetings and three in-person meetings. Fellows are placed in full-time positions with news organizations and will survey topics such as: media law, journalistic ethics, and source relationships; interviewing, reporting, and storytelling; digital tools, social platforms, and analytics; and a suite of immersive electives in professional skills and journalism. Read here for more details. Deadline is March 10.

ProPublica is taking applications for its Investigative Editor Training Program, which is designed to help expand the ranks of editors with investigative experience in more newsrooms across the country, with a focus on people from underrepresented backgrounds. Deadline is March 13.

Vermont Law School is accepting applications for its Media Fellowships. Enhance your journalism skills and deepen your understanding of environmental, agriculture and farm systems, or animal law and policy during the summer of 2023. Deadline is March 20.

Newmark J-School’s Harold W. McGraw, Jr. Center for Business Journalism is accepting applications from experienced journalists for its Fellowship for Business Journalism. Grants up to $15,000 and the editorial support to produce deeply reported enterprise and investigative stories that delve into critical economic, financial, or business issues across a wide array of subjects are provided. You don’t need to be a business reporter to apply; many of the previous Fellows have been generalists, or cover beats such as health care, education or the environment. Deadline is March 31.

The Native American Journalists Association is accepting submissions for the 2023 National Native Media Awards. All entries must have been published or aired in 2022. Deadline is March 31.  

The AL DÍA Foundation is looking for candidates for the Félix Varela Fellowship which will offer a full year of comprehensive training in AL DÍA News Media headquarters in Philadelphia. Deadline is April 3. 

The Council for the Advancement of Science Writing (CASW) is accepting submissions for its Victor Cohn Prize for Excellence in Medical Science Reporting. The prize seeks to honor a writer for a body of work published or broadcast within the last five years which, for reasons of uncommon clarity, accuracy, breadth of coverage, enterprise, originality, insight and narrative power, has made a profound and lasting contribution to public awareness and understanding of critical advances in medical science and their impact on human health and well-being. Deadline is June 30.
