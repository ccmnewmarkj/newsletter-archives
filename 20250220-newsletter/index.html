News 

What is needed for support organizations to more efficiently and effectively serve the local news field? A report by Commoner Company, a civic media organization co-founded by former Local Independent Online News Publishers (LION) deputy director Anika Anand and City Bureau co-founder and Newmark J-School Entrepreneur-in-Residence Darryl Holliday, explored this question in their inaugural report. In this new body of work, CCM is mentioned as an example of leadership in the Educators space. 

The American Press Institute has a roundup on how local news organizations are covering federal government policies for their audiences. 

The Economic Hardship Reporting Project makes the case for having more people from working-class backgrounds in journalism and more news outlets that cater to a working-class audience in the Columbia Journalism Review. 

Down in the County used photojournalism to build up its audience in rural North Carolina, Better News reported. 

Nieman Lab highlighted a recent study published in academic journal Journalism Studies titled, “Converting Online News Visitors to Subscribers: Exploring the Effectiveness of Paywall Strategies Using Behavioral Data.”  

ProPublica documented how it and other community journalists report on the impact of cities dismantling homeless encampments, sharing best practices on how to do so responsibly.

Journalism project Mapping Black California used AI and other technologies to build tools that would help better report on Black communities across the state, Hacks/Hackers reported. 

Student journalists at the University of Texas - Dallas went on strike last fall after school administrators fired the editor of the student newspaper and began overseeing content. Now they are back with an alternative newspaper they distribute all over campus by hand, per Texas Tribune.

The New School announced the launch of the Community College Civic News Challenge, an initiative with the goal of creating more opportunities for people in community colleges across the South to get involved in news systems. 

The American Press Institute shared its “ethical roadmap” for collaborating with news influencers, a growing cohort within journalism who share, curate, and report news for their audiences via social media platforms like Substack and TikTok.

Fellowships & Opportunities

The application process is open for early-career journalists to apply to the California Local News Fellowship, a state-funded initiative through the University of California - Berkeley to support and strengthen local reporting, with a focus on underserved communities. This full-time, paid fellowship will place early-career journalists (with up to five years of experience) in California newsrooms for two years, starting in September 2025. Deadline is February 21. 

Local Media Association invites family-owned and independent local media organizations to apply to be part of the second cohort of its Family and Independent Media Sustainability Lab. Ten organizations will be paired with consultants who will spend one year working on business strategies and achieving financial sustainability for the organizations. Deadline is February 27. 

The Tarbell Fellowship is now taking applications for its one-year program for early-career journalists interested in covering artificial intelligence. The program involves a nine month placement in a major newsroom, a 10-week study group covering AI governance & technical fundamentals, and a week-long journalism summit in San Francisco. The Tarbell Fellowship provides a stipend of $50,000 to support these placements. Deadline is February 28. 

Applications are open for the Nonprofit News Business Certificate, a 9-month, project-based training program that will be a game-changer for nonprofit local newsrooms in the United States at a time when the nonprofit news sector is surging. The certificate, developed by J+ at the Newmark J-School, in partnership with the American Journalism Project and Blue Engine Collaborative, will focus on business and financial management for leaders of nonprofit news organizations. Deadline is March 3. 

The 19th is taking applications for the 2025-26 cohort of the Frances Ellen Watkins Harper Fellowship. Alums of Historically Black Colleges and Universities who are early to mid-career are awarded one-year, salaried positions in reporting, audience engagement, and product management with the news outlet. Deadline is March 31. 

The Carter Center’s Rosalynn Carter Fellowships for Mental Health Journalism is accepting applications to support a diverse cohort of journalists from the United States, Ireland, Qatar, and the United Arab Emirates. Fellows are deeply committed to exploring some of society’s biggest mental health challenges. The mental health journalism fellowships program was founded in 1996 by former First Lady Rosalynn Carter based on an essential premise: to give journalists the resources they need to report on mental health — one of the world’s most underreported health issues — and help dismantle through storytelling the stigma and discrimination that millions of people face every day. Deadline is April 2.

Events

Feb 24 (Washington, DC): The Center for Journalism and Democracy at Howard University, the Freedom of the Press Foundation, and the Investigative Reporting Workshop at American University are hosting “Protecting Your Devices, Your Sources and Yourselves,” an all-day, free digital security training. The sessions will cover digital security fundamentals, including the basics of risk assessment, account security and other essential topics. See more details and the agenda here. 

Feb 25 (Online): CCM’s Latino Media Initiative is hosting “Past & Future: How the Latino news media is serving its communities,” a virtual conversation about the Latino news sector's core strengths and areas for growth, revenue and editorial strategies, and models for audience engagement and funding. 

Feb 25 (Online): The Journalist’s Resource is holding a webinar on "Covering Immigration Stories in 2025" using reliable data and original story ideas.

Feb 26 (Online): Trusting News id hosting an event on strengthening your coverage through honest newsroom conversation and dissent.

April 8-9 (Nashville): The American Press Institute is hosting the Local News Summit on Local Identity, History and Sustainability to explore how community-based news organizations can leverage local identity, geography and history to best contribute to a community’s well-being. This is an invitation-based event and requests must be submitted by February 25. 

May 15-16 (Denver): The Center for Cooperative Media at Montclair State University is hosting the 2025 Collaborative Journalism Summit. The Summit is still open to session pitches and will feature two days of lightning talks, workshops, and participatory discussions about a wide array of topics and projects.
